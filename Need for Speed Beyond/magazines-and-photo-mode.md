# Magazines and Photo Mode

## Magazine features

The city's underground car magazines approach the protagonist once he is worth putting on a cover.

**Trigger.** An offer arrives when two conditions are met at the same time: the player holds a title the magazine cares about ([Titles](reputation.md#titles)), and owns a car above a **Presentation** threshold ([Presentation](cars-and-building.md#presentation)). Offers scale: early magazines want a 2-Presentation build; the last ones will not photograph anything under 5.

| Magazine | Culture | Wants |
|---|---|---|
| **Torque & Tenths** | Old Guard | Naturally aspirated, low class, immaculate, no flash. Explicitly does not want Jake in the frame. |
| **Sideways** | Ridge Union | Rear-wheel drive, angle kit visible, mountain backdrop |
| **Quarter** | Dockline | Drag builds, wheelie bars, cages, engine bay open. Does not care whether Jake is in the frame. |
| **Boulevard** | Riverside | Stance, finish, interior, boot — presentation above all. Wants the owner in the frame. |
| **Redline** | Ringrunners | Aero, long gearing, speed machinery, expressway backdrop |
| **Private Plate** | Meridian | Class S and X only. Hypercars, nothing else. |

## The shoot

The player controls the photograph with a free camera:

- **Car.** Any owned car that meets the magazine's brief and threshold.
- **Location.** Any district; the backdrop is the district's real geography.
- **Time of day and weather.** Set freely for the photograph; the game clock is unaffected.
- **Panels.** Doors, hood and boot open or closed, independently.
- **Camera.** Position, height, angle, focal length, depth of field.
- **Jake.** A fixed set of positions relative to the car: behind the wheel, leaning on a wing, sitting on the sill with the door open, standing at the front, arms folded against the rear quarter, or out of frame.

**Jake's position is a pose, not movement.** He is placed, he holds, and the shot is taken — no walking, no free control. The rule that the player is always in the vehicle holds, because a photograph is not play. This is the one place Jake is visible outside the car without a cutscene, and he is visible the way a person in a magazine photograph is: standing still, on purpose, for a camera.

### Scoring

The magazine issues a brief, and the shot is graded against the brief, not against taste. *Quarter* asked for the engine bay, so a beautiful photograph with the hood closed scores badly. *Private Plate* asked for a hypercar, so a perfectly composed shot of a built Civic is rejected outright. *Boulevard* asked for the boot build, so the boot had better be open.

| Result | REP |
|---|---|
| Brief met, strong composition | +600 |
| Brief met, weak composition | +300 |
| Brief partially met | +100 |
| Brief ignored | Offer withdrawn, no REP, and the magazine will not approach again for that tier |

### Afterwards

**The cover exists in the world.** It appears on newsstands, in shop interiors and in NPCs' garages, and crew members reference it. A player who photographed a Dockline drag car for *Quarter* will hear about it from Dockline — and from Riverside, who will have opinions.

**Why magazines exist.** Only three things in the game produce REP without winning a race: escaping the police, showing a new build at a night meet, and this. Magazine features are how a player who builds obsessively converts that work into standing, and they make the whole customisation system — interior, engine bay, boot audio, none of which makes a car faster — matter to progression instead of sitting beside it.

## Photo Mode

The same camera exists outside the magazines. **Photo Mode can be opened anywhere in Nitroit or at any Revada venue, whenever the car is stationary**, with the same controls: free camera, panel states, time of day, and the same fixed positions for Jake.

It scores nothing, unlocks nothing, and is never requested by anyone. It exists because the game spends a hundred hours asking the player to make a car look like something, and giving them no way to look at it would be absurd.
