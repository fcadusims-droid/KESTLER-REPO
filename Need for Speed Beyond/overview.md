# The Revada Festival

## What it is

The **Revada Festival** is a large sanctioned motorsport event held across the state of [Revada](revada.md), a day's drive west of Nitroit: grandstands, paddocks, scrutineering, official timing, sponsor presence, a rulebook.

It is **not** Formula 1, GT3, or any factory-backed professional series. There are no works teams, manufacturer programmes or driver contracts, and no homologated race cars built from the ground up for a rulebook.

Nor is it a street meet with cones. It sits deliberately in between: **legal, organised, scrutineered, public — and not yet professional.** The *ProStreet* model: built road cars, not race cars ([Vehicle classes](cars-and-building.md#vehicle-classes)); private entrants, small independent teams and sponsored tuning shops; closed tracks, scheduled race days, a published rulebook, official timing, grandstands, and a championship at the top.

**It is open to the public, and it is a bridge rather than a destination.** Spectators buy tickets, the standings board is posted where anyone can read it, and professional teams send scouts. Nobody in the festival is at the top of the sport; a meaningful number are trying to get there, and a few will ([The invitation](ending.md#the-invitation)).

It runs one edition a year, for several months, with events on scheduled official dates ([Season](season.md)). Its advertising reaches Nitroit before Jake has finished with the city, but registration only opens to him once he holds every district ([Registration](team.md#registration)).

## Sanctioned culture

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who is in it** | A deliberately mixed field: semi-professionals who race for a living or close to it, shop-sponsored entrants, funded privateers, small teams with two or three good people, and a few genuine professionals between contracts. No manufacturer money, works programmes or engineering departments; support is commercial and local, not industrial. |
| **What a car is for** | Winning within a rulebook. Visual identity still exists — livery, sponsor decals, team presentation — but is subordinate to legality and effectiveness. An illegal car is not a bold statement; it is a car that does not start. |
| **What the rules are** | Published in advance, identical for everyone, enforced by scrutineers who check cars before the event and spot-check them after ([Regulations](regulations.md)). |
| **What legitimacy looks like** | Ranking: timing sheets, standings boards, championship points. The scene does not care who you are, only what the clock and the classification say. |
| **What is technically permitted** | Only what each discipline's regulations allow. Everything else is rejected at scrutineering. |
| **What the risk is** | Disqualification, penalties, suspension, and failing to qualify. |

## Who makes the rules

**In the festival, nobody the player can beat sets the rules.** This is the structural inversion of the city, where taking a district transfers rule-setting authority to the protagonist ([Territorial rule](street-scene.md#territorial-rule)). Here the rules come from an organising body that does not race and cannot be challenged: class regulations, technical scrutineering, conduct penalties and scheduling are fixed and external.

The protagonist can win everything in the festival and never gain authority over it. That is the point.

## Why it is hard

The festival is the hardest content in the game, for six compounding reasons — none of them arbitrary difficulty inflation.

1. **The field is better.** City racers are amateurs in cars they built themselves. Festival competitors are semi-professionals and professionals with a prepared car, a mechanic who knows the circuit, and a season of practice on it. Festival AI runs tighter lines, brakes later and more consistently, defends without error, and does not make the unforced mistakes city AI makes under pressure. A build that dominates a district will finish mid-pack in its first festival event.
2. **The festival gives the player nothing to compete with.** No car is awarded, loaned or provided for any event, including championship rounds, and no staff are supplied. Every car entered was acquired in the city and built by the player; every person on the team was met there. Its prize money never covers what a season costs ([Prize money and sponsorship](team.md#prize-money-and-sponsorship)). The festival is a competition entered using the product of the life Jake spent avoiding it.
3. **Every event is regulated.** Cars that are quick on the street are often illegal on the circuit. The player must bring a car that is legal for that discipline and class, and then make it as good as the rules permit.
4. **Breadth is mandatory.** A team qualifies only if someone on it is competitive in every one of the five disciplines ([Qualification](rank-and-championship.md#qualification)). One exceptional car cannot carry the player; five compliant, purpose-built cars are the minimum ([The five-car requirement](disciplines.md#the-five-car-requirement)).
5. **He cannot enter alone.** Registration is by team: five drivers and five mechanics, all named people recruited in the city ([Team](team.md)). The hardest entry requirement in the game is not a car or a lap time; it is ten people willing to put their season in the hands of a street racer.
6. **The car is now simulated.** Tyres come up to temperature and fade, brakes fade in the long formats, and damage degrades the car live with no restart ([Simulation layer](driving-and-damage.md#the-simulation-layer)). None of this existed in the city. A player arriving from five acts of arcade street racing will lose their first festival event on the opening lap, on cold tyres, and not understand why.

None of the six is an engineering problem. There is no pit wall, fuel number or stop to plan; every one is solved with the car moving ([What strategy means](season.md#what-strategy-means)).

## The shift in mindset

The festival asks the player to think about the car differently, and this is the hard part.

**In the city, the car is an expression.** The player builds what they want, how they want it, and the only question is whether it is theirs. Nothing is prohibited or inspected. A subwoofer in the boot, a wide-body kit that fits nothing, neon under the sills, an engine from another manufacturer — all legal, because legality is not a concept the street scene has.

**In the festival, the car is a submission.** It is handed to an authority that checks it, line by line, against a rulebook not written for the player's benefit. The question is no longer what the player wants the car to be, but what it is allowed to be, and how far it can be pushed *inside* that.

| | City mindset | Festival mindset |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal of a build** | Make it mine | Make it legal, then make it optimal |
| **Constraint** | Money | Regulation, then money |
| **What "better" means** | Faster, or more me | Faster inside a fixed box |
| **Who decides** | The player | The rulebook, read by the mechanic |
| **What gets removed** | Nothing | Most of what the player installed in the city |

**The first registration is where the player feels this.** They bring their favourite car — built over five acts, photographed for a magazine, driven to the top of the city — and registration rejects it for four separate breaches: the boot build is illegal ballast, the body kit is outside the dimensional limits, the tyres are not a permitted compound, and the nitrous cannot be there at all. The car is not bad. The car is not *for this*.

**The skill the festival rewards is the one the city never asked for: working inside somebody else's constraints and finding room in them.** That is his father's job description. It is also the thing Jake has spent the game refusing to do — and the mechanic is the person who teaches it to him ([Mechanics](mechanics.md)).

## What carries over

| Carries over | Does not carry over |
|---|---|
| Every car in the garage | City REP |
| All money and outstanding debt | Any district authority |
| All installed parts and Blueprints | Any rule-setting privilege |
| Shop relationships and credit terms | Access unlocked by city tier |
| Individual NPC Standing — and the roster it makes possible | The ability to compete alone |
| Driving skill, entirely | — |

Jake arrives in Revada with everything he built and none of what he became: a stranger in another state with a garage and ten people who agreed to make the drive.
