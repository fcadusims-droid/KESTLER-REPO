# The Season

Where events are held is in [Revada](revada.md#venues). How city time is spent between race dates is in [Time](time.md#during-a-season).

## The field

A festival season fields **thirty-two registered teams**: sixty-four cars.

Not every team contests every discipline. A team registers per discipline, and most register in two or three, so the specialists the protagonist meets in Drag are largely not the ones he meets in Drift. A discipline typically draws around thirty entrants.

**Eight teams are registered in all five disciplines**: Jake's, and seven rival teams, each with a leader who races everything ([Team leaders](rank-and-championship.md#team-leaders)). Only these eight can qualify for the championship. The seven rival all-discipline teams are built to contend, so at least three of them qualify in every season and the Circuit Final never has fewer than four cars.

## How an event runs

Grip (Open and Class) and Sprint run in **heats** when entries exceed a grid:

| Stage | Structure |
|---|---|
| **Heats** | Entrants are split into groups of eight. Each heat runs the full event. |
| **Final** | Eight cars: the best heat performers — heat winners first, then heat seconds, and so on, with ties broken by heat race time. With four heats, the top two of each heat go through. |
| **Classification** | The final sets places 1–8; everyone else is classified behind them ([Classification](rank-and-championship.md#classification)). |

- **Both of a team's cars can appear in the same heat.** Seeding separates teammates only in the Drag bracket.
- **Drag** runs qualifying and a sixteen-car bracket; **Drift** runs solos and tandems; **Speed**, **Time Attack** and **Sector Shootout** have no grid and classify by time ([Running order](disciplines.md#running-order)).

## The season

| | |
|---|---|
| **Race dates** | 14 across about three and a half months of in-game time |
| **Events per date** | 2 to 4, from different disciplines |
| **Events per discipline, per season** | 8 to 11 |
| **City window between dates** | 6 to 8 in-game days, two of them travel ([Time](time.md#during-a-season)) |
| **Championship** | 4 rounds plus the Circuit Final, after date 14 ([Championship](rank-and-championship.md#the-championship)) |

Eight to eleven events per discipline make qualification survivable and the Grade system meaningful: a bad round is recoverable; a bad discipline is not.

## Race length

**Festival races are short** — a deliberate calibration, not an omission.

| Discipline | Length |
|---|---|
| **Grip — Open / Class** | 3 to 6 laps, by circuit |
| **Grip — Time Attack** | One timed lap, one attempt, plus an out-lap |
| **Grip — Sector Shootout** | One full lap, sectors timed independently |
| **Sprint** | 8 to 20 minutes point to point — the long format |
| **Drag** | Seconds per run |
| **Drift** | Solo runs and one tandem across one session |
| **Speed** | 5 to 12 minutes point to point — the other long format |

Nothing runs for an hour and nothing requires a stop. A driver rolls out of the paddock, races, and rolls back in.

## What strategy means

**Strategy is a set of decisions made at speed, inside the race, with no menu open.** That is all of it:

| Decision | Where it comes up |
|---|---|
| **When the tyres are ready** | The opening lap of every circuit event. Push on cold rubber and lose the car; wait too long and lose the position. |
| **How hard to lean on the tyres** | Long Sprints and Speed runs, where fade is gradual and the driver decides whether the last third matters more than the first. |
| **When to spend the brakes** | Long formats only. Heavy braking every corner fades them; the driver chooses which corners are worth it. |
| **Attack or hold** | Every wheel-to-wheel event. Contact costs points through the conduct multiplier, so an overtake that would be free in the city is a calculation here. |
| **Slipstream** | Speed and the long Sprints: when to sit in it, when to pull out, whether to give it to a teammate. |
| **Committing in a tandem** | Drift: how close is close enough before the contact risk outweighs the synchronisation score. |
| **Damage management** | Everywhere. Live damage does not repair mid-event, so a driver carrying a broken splitter decides whether to keep racing it or settle for the classification he has. |

**Strategy is never:** choosing a stint plan before the start; deciding when to pit (there are no pits); managing a fuel number; reading telemetry; watching the race from a timing screen.

**The test the festival sets:** can the player hold a race together from green to chequered, in a car that is being worn down, against people who do not make mistakes, without touching anybody? In the city the answer never mattered. Here it is the whole score.
