# Campaign

## Act structure

| Act | Title | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The Unknown | A cheap car, a hostel, and a city that has never heard of him. |
| **2** | Ascent | Multiple districts contested. Crews, rivalries; police attention escalates. |
| **3** | King of the Streets | Final districts taken. The Revada Festival appears in Nitroit for the first time, late. |
| **4** | The Hollow | Everything is won and nothing is resolved. Defending, the last Invitationals, and the only axis left. |
| **5** | The Choice | The registration act: roster, mechanics, five legal cars, salaries. |
| **Epilogue** | Beyond | The Revada calendar opens. A second career begins at zero Rank. Ends with the championship, a professional contract, and a cemetery. See [Ending](ending.md). |

Nitroit never closes. Once the Epilogue unlocks, the player moves between Nitroit and the Revada calendar for the rest of the game.

Districts are taken by beating each crew's hierarchy and then its leader, never by reaching a title ([Taking a district](street-scene.md#taking-a-district)). Once taken, a district has to be defended ([Defending a district](street-scene.md#defending-a-district)).

## Act 1 — The Unknown

**The game starts in a hostel bunk with $15,000 and no car.** Jake has been in Nitroit a few weeks. He knows nobody, owes nothing, has told no one anything true about himself, and has been walking everywhere. See [Backstory](backstory.md).

### The opening sequence

The opening is a short, non-interactive cutscene and the only point at which the game begins outside a car: a coach pulling into the downtown terminal, a bag, a hostel desk, and then Jake crossing Nitroit on foot and by bus over some weeks. It ends on Pier Road at the eastern edge of the Docks, at a fenced used-car lot next to a machine shop.

**From the moment he buys a car, the player is in it.** The game is played entirely from inside a vehicle. Jake never gets out under player control: no on-foot movement, no walking around a garage, no exploring on two legs. Every location the player visits, they drive into. The cutscenes — four story cutscenes and the travel sequence to and from Revada — are the only exception ([Production scope](production-scope.md)).

### The lot

**Halvorsen Motors** is eleven cars on cracked asphalt behind a chain-link fence, and every playthrough begins there.

Three of the cars are for sale to Jake. The rest cost more than $15,000 would allow with anything left for parts; they are visible and labelled, and not an option this week.

The three are deliberately unequal in character and equal in price. None is better; each is the cheap end of a different tradition in the city.

| Car | What it is | Points toward |
|---|---|---|
| **Rear-drive coupé, eighteen years old** | Tired straight-six, soft suspension, rust in the arches. Slow, balanced, endlessly modifiable; the natural start for anyone who wants the back end to move. | Ridgeline |
| **Front-drive hatchback, light, high-mileage** | Small naturally aspirated four, nothing broken, nothing special. The most forgiving of the three and the quickest to be competitive in a low class. | The Old Quarter |
| **Rear-drive V8 sedan, two owners, one repaint** | Heavy, torquey, hopeless in corners, and the fastest of the three in a straight line before anyone touches it. | The Docks |

- **The choice gates nothing.** Every district, event, crew and storyline is available whatever Jake drives, and the car can be sold or replaced within Act 1. The choice decides which part of Nitroit is easy first and which is a fight, and the player makes it before knowing that.
- **Nobody recommends one.** The lot's owner answers questions about all three honestly and does not steer. No NPC behaves as though there is a correct answer.

### What the purchase establishes

The first car the player controls is one Jake chose and owns outright, and **the game never gives him a car for free at any point in either half** — not as a reward, not as a story beat, not in the festival, not after the championship. A Loyal friend may lend him a car while his own is impounded, but a loan is not a gift ([Borrowing a car](police.md#borrowing-a-car)).

**The car is paid for with the one exception Jake allows himself.** The money in his pocket is what is left of the $25,000 he took from the inheritance — the cost of leaving, and the only part of his father's money he has ever spent ([The inheritance](backstory.md#the-inheritance)). Everything he buys after this is bought with what he wins.

The machine shop sharing the lot's fence is **Ward & Sons Machine** ([Cast](cast.md#shops-and-mechanics)). The first car purchase and the first mechanic relationship therefore happen within an hour of each other, in the district whose crew leader, Delia Ward, is Curtis's sister — so whatever Jake takes from her, he also pays for at the shop next to where he started ([Family ties](relationships.md#family-ties)). None of this is signposted; it is simply where the cheap cars are.

**He is better than everyone he meets, and he loses anyway**, because his car is worse, he does not know the roads, and he has never been chased by anyone. Act 1's difficulty curve is not about teaching the player to drive; it is about a trained driver discovering that driving was never the only thing that mattered.

**The hostel is temporary.** It holds one car ([Property](property.md)), and the first economic goal the game sets is somewhere with a second space.

### The name

**Nobody in Nitroit knows who Jake is, and that is why he is there.** Through Acts 1 to 5 he is nobody's son. Magazines print his first name and his car; crews call him whatever they decide to; the police have a plate and a file. **Spaid never comes up**: he does not volunteer it, no NPC recognises it, and the city has no reason to care. Nobody in Nitroit ever knew who John Spaid was.

**The festival prints it, and nobody reads it.** Every festival entry is filed under a legal name, so all season the standings boards in Revada say *J. Spaid*. Nobody connects it to anything. To the paddock he is one more unknown from a street scene.

**It comes out only when he wins.** After the Circuit Final, with his name at the top of the board and a championship beside it, a few people work out whose son he is — and the professional team that comes to the paddock afterwards is among them. There is no revelation scene and nobody confronts him; it is simply known, quietly, by the people who matter, and it is what makes [the invitation](ending.md#the-invitation) land. He spent five years being told what that name obliged him to do, then crossed a country to get away from it, and the name is what he wins with.

## Act 2 — Ascent

Multiple districts are contested, crews and rivalries develop, and police attention escalates. The systems that drive this act are [Street scene](street-scene.md), [Reputation](reputation.md), [Relationships](relationships.md), [Police](police.md) and [Street events](street-events.md).

## Act 3 — King of the Streets

The final districts are taken. The act concludes when every district is held, which grants the title King of the Streets ([Reputation](reputation.md#titles)) and opens festival registration ([Registration](team.md#registration)).

### The festival arrives

**The festival does not exist in the game until Act 3 is nearly over.** Through Acts 1 and 2 there is no mention of it anywhere — no billboard, no radio, no line of NPC dialogue. Every structural signal says the game has no second half: districts are falling, the REP ladder is visibly running out of rungs, and the last crew leader is in sight. **The player is supposed to think they are finishing the game, and to be right until they are not.**

Late in Act 3, with five or six districts held, the Revada Festival appears in Nitroit all at once, the way a real event with a marketing budget appears in a real city. **It can be seen, heard and talked about from then on, but it cannot be entered:** registration opens only when Jake holds every district ([Registration](team.md#registration)).

| Channel | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Radio** | Stations start running spots, then the news at the top of the hour covers it as a story. Each station carries it in character ([Radio](radio.md#radio-and-the-rest-of-the-game)). |
| **Billboards** | Bought across the expressway and the commercial strips over about a week of in-game time. |
| **Television** | Shop interiors, garage waiting areas, bar screens; highlight packages from previous seasons. |
| **Magazines** | The newsstands that have been printing Jake's covers start carrying festival preview issues. |
| **NPCs** | Crew members bring it up unprompted; shop owners mention it while working. It becomes what the city talks about. |

**The advertising sells the festival as a door, not a race series.** Revada is where drivers get seen: teams scout it, careers start there, it is the last open entry point into professional motorsport for anyone who did not come through a factory junior programme, it runs once a year, and if you are good and not in it, that is a decision you made. *An event you cannot afford to miss.*

**The timing is the design.** The festival lands when the player has run out of city to conquer, on a wave of advertising that promises a professional career, in a game that has spent three acts establishing that its protagonist will not go near one. Everybody else reads the advertising as a chance. Jake reads it as the thing that killed his father, wearing a sponsor's logo.

**He says nothing about it.** Asked directly, he changes the subject. This is the only place in the game where his avoidance is dramatised without a single line naming it.

## Act 4 — The Hollow

**Act 4 begins when the last district falls and nothing is left to take.** Every crew is beaten, every territory held, the title is King of the Streets, and the REP bar has no tier above it. The game has spent three acts training the player to answer every question by winning something; Act 4 is where that stops producing anything.

| Content | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Defending** | Holding a district is not permanent. Ranked racers challenge their district's leader, and challenge sooner when he stops appearing there; losing hands the district to the challenger, who must then be beaten to retake it. Defending is REP-neutral — there is no gain available, only loss avoided — and every district held keeps paying its commission ([Defending a district](street-scene.md#defending-a-district)). |
| **The last Invitationals** | Whichever of the nine are outstanding, including the two unaffiliated racers. Each can be raced once per act, so Act 4 is a fresh set ([Street events](street-events.md#invitationals)). |
| **The List** | The police do not stop. Act 4 is where a player who wrecked patrol cars for three acts discovers what they bought ([Police](police.md#the-list)). |
| **Relationships** | The only axis with anywhere left to go. Every uncompleted Loyal storyline is still available, and Act 4 is the last stretch before the roster locks at registration ([Crew storylines](crew-storylines.md), [Team](team.md)). |
| **Magazines** | Cover-tier offers requiring builds the player has had no reason to make until now ([Magazines](magazines-and-photo-mode.md)). |

**Four of those give the player nothing they do not already have. One does.** Nobody says which. The player works it out from the fact that everything else has stopped paying, and what they do about it is the act.

**A player can leave Act 4 with no relationships at all.** Nothing forces the Loyal storylines and nothing gates Act 5 behind them. That player reaches registration, cannot field a legal team, and has to go back — not because the game blocked them, but because they spent an act proving they needed no one and were wrong.

**Act 4 ends when Jake drives out of Nitroit and into Revada for the first time**, months after the first festival billboard went up. Not when he registers or qualifies: when the player chooses to go. The fourteen-hour drive alone is a cutscene ([Revada](revada.md#not-an-open-world)); at the end of it he pulls up at the perimeter of a venue with a race meeting running — not a festival round, since no season has started — and sits with the engine off, watching through the fence. He does not get out. It is the only story beat in the game triggered by going somewhere rather than winning something.

## Act 5 — The Choice

**Act 5 is the registration act, and the only act that is not about racing.** The festival's entry requirements arrive all at once, and Jake has none of them.

| Requirement | What Act 5 is about |
|---|---|
| **Five drivers** | One per discipline, from the six crews that supply them or the two unaffiliated racers. Whoever is Friendly or better now. Whoever is not, is not coming. See [Team](team.md#recruitment). |
| **Five mechanics** | Five of the six shops that can supply one, each mechanic assigned to a discipline. Shop Standing is built by using shops; a player who bought everything from one place has one mechanic. See [Mechanics](mechanics.md). |
| **Five legal cars** | Each built to a different discipline's rulebook. Most of the player's garage is illegal and must be rebuilt or replaced. See [Disciplines](disciplines.md#the-five-car-requirement). |
| **Salaries** | Ten of them, unless relationships waive them. The largest sum the game ever asks for. See [Team](team.md#salaries). |
| **Sponsorship, optionally** | A Loyal Meridian contact covers all salaries. See [Team](team.md#salaries). |

**Act 5 is a conversion act**: everything accumulated in the city becomes something the festival accepts, or it does not come. Money becomes cars. Territory converts to nothing, and the game does not soften that. REP converts to nothing; there is no exchange rate. Relationships become a roster. See [Festival overview](overview.md#what-carries-over).

**The dramatic content is in what refuses to convert.** Jake discovers that most of what he built has no value where he is going, and that the part which does — the people — is the part he paid least attention to.

**Act 5 ends on a submitted registration form**, in the second of the game's four story cutscenes. No race, no victory, no confrontation. He fills it in, and the Epilogue opens.
