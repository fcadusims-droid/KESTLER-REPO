# The Street Scene

## Where the scene came from

**Nitroit's street scene is not one culture.** It is five founding cultures and two districts that have none, divided by neighbourhood because the work was.

The city grew in waves: the port, then the mills that fed on it, then the iron mines in the northern hills, then the plants that turned ore into product. Each wave of workers settled where the work was, and their children built cars there. A hundred years later the jobs have mostly not come back, but the car culture stayed exactly where it was planted.

**So a district's discipline is not arbitrary.** Dockline runs drag because the docks ran on grudge money for eighty years. Ridge Union drifts because the families who settled the hill brought the practice with them. Riverside judges builds because the people who built that scene were never let into the ones that only cared about speed.

| District | Founding lineage | What it became |
|---|---|---|
| **The Docks** | Longshore families — a Black waterfront local that organised in the 1930s, alongside Irish and Polish gangs from the mills | Grudge drag racing, money on the hood |
| **Old Quarter** | Southern European immigrants — Italian, Portuguese, Greek — who brought sports cars and a hostility to shortcuts | Naturally aspirated circuit purism |
| **Ridgeline** | Japanese-American families who settled the hill after the war, then a wider Asian-American scene through the 1990s | Drift and mountain running |
| **Expressway Ring** | None. The newest crew, built by kids from every other district who wanted a road nobody owned | Top speed, and no heritage at all |
| **Industrial Flats** | All of them. Neutral ground since the plants closed and the lots emptied | Everything, mixed |
| **Riverside** | Mexican-American families, and a lowrider and custom tradition older than the street racing around it | Build quality judged as seriously as lap time |
| **The Heights** | The families who own the mines, the mills and the port | Hypercars, and a private club |

**How the game handles this:**

- Culture is expressed through **what a district races, how it judges, what it refuses, and how its people talk** — never as a label on a character.
- Every crew has members from outside its founding community, and the newer crews have no founding community at all.
- Nobody in the game gates entry by who someone is. Every district gates by **what car you brought**, which is a rule about the car.
- The city's history is present in shop signs, family names recurring across generations, the languages heard at meets, the food at night meets, and what older NPCs remember. It is texture with mechanical consequence, not a demographic table.

## Districts and crews

The city has **seven districts**, each controlled by a crew. Each crew is a complete competitive identity: discipline, machinery, entry rules, and a reason for existing. A district is a filter as much as a region: what the player may bring into it decides what they must build to win there, and no two districts ask for the same car. Classes are defined in [Cars and building](../systems/cars-and-building.md#vehicle-classes). Leaders are listed in [Cast](../story/cast.md#crew-leaders).

### Dockline — The Docks

*Container yards, half-mile straights, poor lighting, standing water.*

- **Lineage:** Longshore families, three and four generations deep. The waterfront local organised in the 1930s and its membership has been predominantly Black ever since; the mill gangs alongside it were Irish and Polish. The racing came from the same place the union did — dangerous work, good money, and a shift ending at two in the morning.
- **Discipline:** Drag, and nothing else.
- **Events:** Quarter-mile, half-mile, roll racing from 100 km/h, standing-start launch challenges.
- **Entry rules:** Rear-wheel drive only. No aero of any kind. Classes B to S.
- **How it works:** Grudge racing — two people, a private bet, no published times, nothing recorded. The money is the point and the secrecy is part of it; a Dockline racer who tells you his times is lying. There are also **gambler nights**, where everyone pays in and the winner takes most of the pot.
- **Philosophy:** Power and launch. Nothing in a Dockline build helps it turn; cornering is an admission you did not build enough engine. They race anyone who can cover the bet and respect nobody who cannot.

### The Old Guard — Old Quarter

*Narrow street grid, tight corners, heavy civilian traffic, stone kerbs.*

- **Lineage:** The Italian, Portuguese and Greek families who filled the Quarter when the mills were hiring, bringing a sports-car tradition rather than a hot-rod one. The club has met in the same social hall since the 1960s; two of its founders are still alive and still come.
- **Discipline:** Grip — multi-lap circuit racing on public streets.
- **Events:** Multi-lap street circuits, timed single laps, sector runs.
- **Entry rules:** Classes C and B only. Naturally aspirated engines only. No nitrous.
- **Philosophy:** The oldest and most conservative crew. Forced induction and nitrous are ways of buying what a driver should earn. Their restrictions strip everything but the driver out of the equation, and their events are the hardest in the city to win on raw speed.

### Ridge Union — Ridgeline

*Mountain road, sustained elevation change, blind crests, no barriers.*

- **Lineage:** Japanese-American families who settled the hill after the war, and the wider Asian-American import scene that grew around them from the late 1980s. The city's first import shop is on Ridgeline and is still run by the family that opened it.
- **Discipline:** Drift, downhill and uphill.
- **Events:** Solo scored runs, tandem battles, downhill time runs, uphill sprints.
- **Entry rules:** Rear-wheel drive only; front- and all-wheel drive are refused at the gate. Classes B and A. Aero permitted but unfashionable.
- **The grudge they carry:** For twenty years the rest of Nitroit would not take a four-cylinder import seriously, and Dockline in particular would not race one. Ridge Union stopped asking, built its own scene on a road nobody else wanted, and scores it by its own criteria. They are polite about this and have not forgotten it.
- **Philosophy:** Style is the score. A fast but flat run scores below a slower run held at angle. The only crew in the city that judges rather than times.

### Ringrunners — Expressway Ring

*Elevated multi-lane loop around the city. Continuous, no exits, no stopping.*

- **Lineage:** None, and they are proud of it. Founded fifteen years ago by second-generation kids from every other district who were tired of inheriting somebody's rules. The most mixed membership in Nitroit; the only common ground is the road.
- **Discipline:** Top speed.
- **Events:** Speed traps, sustained average-speed runs, full-loop time attacks, rolling standoffs at licence-losing velocity.
- **Entry rules:** Classes A and S. A minimum top-speed threshold is verified before entry. Aero mandatory above a defined power figure.
- **Philosophy:** Nerve over craft. Events run at velocities where a lane change is a commitment and a truck in the wrong lane ends the run. They attract the most police attention in the city and do not care.

### The Yard — Industrial Flats

*Warehouse district, open lots, abandoned infrastructure, endless usable space.*

- **Lineage:** Everyone's. The Flats emptied when the plants closed; nobody owns them, so nobody's rules apply. Yard night meets are the only place where all six other scenes stand in the same car park, and the food, music and languages there are the whole city at once.
- **Discipline:** All of them.
- **Events:** Everything the other six crews run, plus the largest night meets in the city.
- **Entry rules:** None. Any class, any drivetrain, any build, any car.
- **Philosophy:** The city's common ground and the only district where the other crews mix. It is where a player finds out which discipline they are actually good at, and where rivalries get settled in front of an audience.

### Riverside — Riverside

*Residential, mid-speed, unpredictable civilian traffic, well-lit boulevards.*

- **Lineage:** Mexican-American families, and a lowrider and custom tradition older than street racing in Nitroit and never really about racing. The boulevard cruise on Riverside's main street has run on the same night of the week for fifty years; the racing grew up alongside it.
- **Discipline:** Build quality, expressed through racing.
- **Events:** Cruise runs judged on presentation, short sprints where entry depends on the car passing judgement, coordinated multi-car runs.
- **Entry rules:** No class restriction. The car must pass the **Riverside check** below.
- **Philosophy:** The only crew that does not care how fast the car is, and the only one that will refuse the player's best car outright. It forces a player who has been optimising numbers to make something on purpose.

**The Riverside check.** A car is admitted if it meets the event's Presentation threshold ([Presentation](../systems/cars-and-building.md#presentation)) and fails **none** of these:

| Condition | Fails if |
|---|---|
| **Finish** | Any panel is unpainted, primer, mismatched in shade, or still wearing a previous colour |
| **Kit integrity** | Front, rear and side bodywork come from different kits, or a kit is partially fitted |
| **Stance** | Wheel diameter, tyre profile and ride height are not set to one another — wheel gap, stretch or poke outside a stated band |
| **Damage** | Any unrepaired visible damage ([Damage](../systems/driving-and-damage.md#damage-in-the-city)) |
| **Interior** | The boot is built out but the interior is untouched, or the reverse |
| **Intent** | Fewer than three of the six customisation categories (body, finish, lighting, wheels, interior, boot) have been touched |

Every condition is shown at the gate with a pass or fail, and the failing item is named. A rejection is never mysterious and never a matter of taste. The bar is not quality: a cheap car finished cleanly in one colour, on wheels that suit it, with a worked interior, passes. A hypercar with three kits bolted together and a bare boot does not.

### Meridian — The Heights

*Millionaires' district. Gated streets, private estate roads, hillside switchbacks, underground garages beneath luxury towers.*

- **Lineage:** The children and grandchildren of the families who own the iron mines, the mills that process the ore, the shipping lines that move it and the plants that turn it into product. **Nobody in Meridian earned the money.** They inherited it, from businesses their families still run, employing the parents of half the racers in the rest of the city.
- **Discipline:** Street racing on the most expensive machinery in the city.
- **Events:** Night runs on estate roads, hill sprints through the gated streets, tunnel and garage circuits under the towers, head-to-head challenges for sums nobody else can cover.
- **Entry rules:** **Classes S and X only** — road-going supercars and hypercars such as Bugatti, Koenigsegg and Pagani. The entrant must own the car: no borrowed entries, no detuned cars, no exceptions. Ownership is verified before the gate opens.
- **Philosophy:** Membership is the car, and the car is the family. There is no application and no reputation requirement; the only way past the class rule is owning a hypercar — which, for everyone in Meridian, was a birthday.

**The friction.** This is the crew the rest of the city has a real problem with, and the only friction in Nitroit about something other than driving. A Dockline racer whose father works a Meridian family's terminal is not neutral about racing that family's son. The game does not resolve this, does not make Meridian villains, and does not make them stupid: several are genuinely quick. It notes only that they never had to be anything else.

**Meridian is illegal like everything else in the city.** No marshals, scrutineers, timing officials or rulebook — only a gate, a class restriction, and enough private money that the police in The Heights are slower to arrive and less interested when they do. It is the least policed district and the one with the most expensive consequences for being caught ([Impound](../systems/police.md#impound)).

**It is the last district in the campaign and the hardest wall in the city.** Hypercars are the most expensive thing the game sells, so taking The Heights is where street winnings stop being spending money and become capital.

**Meridian supplies no drivers and no mechanics to a festival team.** Its members have no festival discipline and no interest in working for anyone. A Loyal Meridian contact offers money instead: sponsorship of the team's salaries for a season ([Team](../festival/team.md#salaries)). It is the only district whose relationships convert into funding rather than personnel.

## Territorial rule

**In the city, the crew that controls a district sets the rules inside it.** The controlling crew decides:

- which event types run in the district;
- the buy-in and payout for events there;
- whether outsiders may enter at all;
- the entry rules — class ceilings and floors, drivetrain requirements, aspiration limits, aero and nitrous rules.

**Entry rules are checked at the gate, not enforced technically.** No one inspects the car. The crew looks at it, reads its class and build, and waves it through or does not. A player who wants into a restricted event brings a car that fits or detunes one that does ([Detuning](../systems/cars-and-building.md#vehicle-classes)).

### Taking a district

**No title is required to take a district.** The protagonist takes it by beating its hierarchy: he defeats every ranked member of the controlling crew, in any order, and that opens the district's final race against the crew's leader — the best driver in the district. Beating the leader takes the district.

What limits him is the car, not his reputation: every race in the hierarchy is held under the district's entry rules, so The Heights stays out of reach until he owns a hypercar.

### Leading a district

**Taking a district makes the protagonist its leader.** That gives him three things, and one obligation.

| | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Commission** | Everyone who races in the district pays the leader a cut: **10% of every buy-in** for every event held there, settled weekly. It is the city's only passive income, and it grows with how busy the district is. |
| **Buy-in level** | The leader sets the district's buy-in at one of three levels — low, standard or high. Higher buy-ins mean a bigger cut of each event and bigger purses, and fewer racers who can afford to enter. |
| **Recognition** | NPCs in the district treat him as its leader in dialogue. |
| **Challenges** — the obligation | Ranked racers of the district can challenge the leader for it ([Defending a district](#defending-a-district)). |

The district's other house rules — which event types run, its class, drivetrain, aspiration, aero and nitrous rules — stay as the crew set them. They are the district's identity, and Jake leads it without rewriting it. This is the authority the game deliberately does not grant him in the festival ([Who makes the rules](../festival/overview.md#who-makes-the-rules)).

### Defending a district

- **A challenge** comes from a ranked racer of the district's crew, delivered by phone. Challenges arrive about once every fourteen in-game days, and sooner — after seven — if Jake has not raced in the district in that time.
- **Jake has three in-game days to answer.** A challenge left unanswered forfeits the district.
- **The challenge is one race**, in the district's discipline and under its entry rules.
- **Winning keeps the district**; losing hands it to the challenger. Defending is REP-neutral either way ([REP changes](../systems/reputation.md#rep-changes)) — there is no gain, only loss avoided. Commission stops the moment the district is lost.

### Who leads a crew

- **A lost district goes to whoever took it from Jake.** The challenger becomes the crew's new leader, and its final race.
- **Retaking a district means beating its new leader.** The hierarchy does not have to be beaten again; Jake already beat it once.
- **The original leaders keep their place in the story.** Delia Ward, Tomás Ferreira and the others stay in their crews as ranked members after Jake takes their district. Each keeps their Invitational, their crew storyline and their place as a recruitable festival driver, whoever is leading the crew at the time ([Cast](../story/cast.md#crew-leaders)).

## Street culture

The city runs on **underground car culture**: unsanctioned, self-organised, and answerable only to the people inside it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who is in it** | Amateurs, self-taught drivers, mechanics, shop owners, people who build cars in home garages and driveways. Nobody in the city races for a living; they race because the scene is the only place their car means anything. |
| **What a car is for** | Identity first, performance second, and the two are not separated. A fast but anonymous car earns nothing at a night meet. The scene reads a build as it reads a person: stance, wheel fitment, paint, exhaust note, engine-swap choice, whether the work was done properly or bodged. |
| **What the rules are** | Whatever the controlling crew says. Rules are verbal, local, inconsistent between districts, and enforced socially — by refusal of entry, reputation damage, being run off the road. |
| **What legitimacy looks like** | Recognition. No certificate, licence or ranking body; a racer is what other racers say he is. |
| **What is technically permitted** | Everything a car can be made to do: cross-manufacturer engine swaps, nitrous, illegal-height suspension, straight-piped exhausts, removed catalytic converters, unhomologated aero, non-road-legal tyres. No organising body exists, so no build is ever rejected on grounds of legality. |
| **What the risk is** | Police, other racers, and money. Nothing else. |

The only restrictions are **entry conditions set by a controlling crew for its own events**. These are house rules, not regulations: they apply to one crew's events in one district, and nobody inspects the car. A racer who ignores them is not disqualified; he is simply not let in.
