# Ending

How the championship is reached and decided is in [Rank and championship](rank-and-championship.md). This file covers what happens once Jake wins.

## The shape of the ending

For most of the game Jake cannot choose. One of two doors is closed, and he is the one holding it shut. The festival is him opening it; the championship is him walking through. What he does on the other side — a professional career, the path his father was on — is the first decision of his adult life that fear did not make for him.

The point is not that the circuit was the right answer and the street the wrong one. The point is that a choice made out of terror was never a choice at all.

## Winning

He takes the flag on a closed circuit, in a multi-lap race, with a championship on it. It may be by four seconds or by a car's length; the game does not arrange which. What is fixed is the shape: he crosses the line at the end of the race his father did not finish.

There is no speech and no montage. The win is the statement.

## The invitation

In the paddock afterwards, a professional GT3 team approaches him with a real, specific offer: a seat, a contract, a season in GT3 — his father's category. Purpose-built race cars, works and customer teams, endurance grids, and a ladder that leads to the FIA World Endurance Championship — the grid his father died on.

It is the first time in his life he is offered a car he did not build and does not own, and the first purpose-built race car he has been offered since karting.

**It is not a reward for winning the festival. It is what the festival is for.** This is the level professional teams recruit from: a public, sanctioned, semi-professional series where results are on a board anyone can read. Scouts are in the paddock all season, and a driver who led his team through all five disciplines and then won the Circuit Final against every other team's leader is exactly the profile they are looking for.

Jake spent five acts calling this place the thing he was afraid of. It was a bridge the whole time. The team is among the few people who work out, once he has won, whose son he is ([The name](campaign.md#the-name)) — which is not why the offer comes, but is why it comes so fast.

**He accepts.**

## The final sequence

The third cutscene: Jake arriving at a professional circuit — not in Revada, not anywhere the game has been — as a contracted driver. Team transporter, garage, crew, race suit, a car he did not build and does not own.

Everything he spent the game surrounded by is gone: no Nitroit, no crew, no district, no reputation, no one who knows him. He is at the start of the career his father had, at the level his father started at, with the same thing at the end of it.

The credits run there. The sequence does not show him winning anything and does not promise that it goes well.

**What the final sequence does not do:**

- It does not present the streets as a mistake or a phase. Nothing in it disowns them.
- It does not have him announce that he has healed, moved on, or made peace.
- It does not close Nitroit. The player returns to a fully open game.

His father died with the Endurance Trophy won on the road and never awarded. Jake signs a contract for the first season of a career that might get him to the same place. The main game does not say whether it will. It says that he is going, and that fear is no longer the reason for anything he does.

## After the credits

**Years later.** A cemetery in the state Jake left at nineteen. Overcast, still, no music — the first time the game shows anywhere other than Nitroit, Revada, the road between them or a circuit.

He is older. The clothes are a driver's clothes, but there is no car in the shot and no engine on the audio. He carries two things.

1. **He goes to his mother first.** He leaves flowers on Grace Spaid's grave and stays a while. The camera does not move in and he does not speak.
2. **Then he goes to his father.** He carries a trophy: the FIA Endurance Trophy for LMGT3 Drivers — the prize John Spaid led into a final lap and never collected, won by his son at the level his father worked at. No speech. He sets it at the headstone, straightens it, and leaves it there.

He walks back the way he came. The shot holds on the two graves — flowers on one, a trophy on the other — and ends.

**What the scene withholds on purpose:**

- **The caption.** Nothing on screen names the trophy or the year it was won. The player knows what it is because they know what his father was denied; the scene does not say it.
- **Whether he is still racing.** The scene does not say whether this is the middle of a career or after it.
- **Dialogue.** He does not speak, and nobody else is in the scene.
- **Nitroit.** No crew member, teammate or mechanic appears or is mentioned. Whether he kept any of them is left with the player who decided what they were worth.

**Why the order matters.** Flowers for Grace first, trophy for John second, and Grace gets the longer beat and the greater silence. The game spends its length on the man who died in the lead and comparatively little on the woman who raised Jake through it; the last thing it does is correct that by an order of operations rather than a line of dialogue.

**The trophy is the only object that leaves Jake's possession permanently.** Cars, money and titles all stay in a save file the player can return to. This one he puts down on purpose and walks away from.

That is the end of *Need for Speed: Beyond*.

## After the championship

Once the Circuit Final is won, both halves of the game remain permanently open:

- Nitroit keeps all seven districts, all street events, all police pursuits, all wager events and all crew content.
- The festival can be re-entered for new editions whenever the player chooses to wait for one ([Between editions](time.md#between-editions)). Rank and Team Standings reset each season; cars and money — including every festival purse won — do not.
- Every NPC the player built a relationship with remains available in both locations, and rosters can be rebuilt season to season.
- No content is locked by completion.

A player who wants to run from the police at Heat 5 in the Docks at three in the morning can do so forever, and nothing in the game's structure, dialogue or scoring treats that as regression. The story is finished, and the world Jake built is still his.
