# Property and Storage

**Cars have to live somewhere, and where they live is a system.** Jake starts in a hostel bunk with no garage ([Campaign](campaign.md#act-1--the-unknown)). Everything after that is bought or rented with money he earned in Nitroit.

## Property types

| Type | Capacity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hostel bunk** | 1 | Starting position. Weekly fee. One car, kept outside. |
| **Rented room** | 1 | Cheap, immediate, available in any district in Act 1. Kept outside. |
| **Rented apartment** | 2 | Secure parking below the building. |
| **Rented house** | 3 | A real garage and a driveway. |
| **Owned apartment** | 3 | No rent; capital tied up. |
| **Owned house** | 4–6 | The standard mid-game home. |
| **Warehouse unit** | 8–12 | Industrial Flats and the Docks only. Bulk storage and workshop space. |
| **Private lot** | 6–8 | Open-air, fenced, cheapest capacity per car. Everything in it is kept outside. |

**A player can hold several properties at once**, and by the festival most will have to: five discipline cars plus a street garage do not fit in one house.

## Capacity is a hard cap

**Jake cannot own more cars than he has space for.** Capacity is checked at the point of acquisition, and there is no overflow state.

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| Buying a car with no space | The purchase is refused at the point of sale. The player is told the number and where their space is. |
| Winning a car in a pink slip or an Invitational with no space | Before accepting the wager, the player chooses which car they would give up to make room. The choice is made in advance, never after. |
| Reclaiming from impound with no space | Refused. The car stays in impound at no extra cost until there is room. |

**Nothing is ever lost to a capacity problem.** The game refuses the transaction instead of taking anything.

## Indoor and outdoor space

Capacity comes in two kinds; the difference is upkeep, not risk.

| | Indoor | Outdoor |
|---|---|---|
| **Where** | Apartments, houses, warehouse units | Hostel, rented rooms, private lots |
| **Cost** | Higher per space | Lowest per space |
| **Effect on the car** | None | Slow cosmetic degradation: paint dulls, trim fades, glass pits |
| **What that costs** | Nothing | [Presentation](cars-and-building.md#presentation), which cuts Riverside entry and magazine offers |

Outdoor degradation is gradual, entirely cosmetic, and reversible at any shop for money. **No car is ever stolen, towed, mechanically damaged or removed for being kept outside.** A twelve-car collection on a fenced lot in the Flats is a legitimate economic choice that produces a slightly tired-looking collection.

## Switching cars

**The player never walks between vehicles, because the player never walks.** Changing cars means driving to a property or shop with space in it: the car goes in, the screen transitions, another car comes out. Any owned property with capacity works, as does any shop the player has access to.

**So property location is a matter of reach as well as time.** A player whose whole garage is in a warehouse in the Docks faces a long drive every time they want a different car on the Ridge, which is why most players end up owning somewhere in the north as well.

## Rent, ownership and location

- **Rent** is charged weekly and deducted automatically.
- **Ownership** removes the weekly charge and locks up a large sum instead. This is the game's only long-horizon financial decision, placed against everything else the money is for: a house is a drag car, a mechanic's salary for a season, or a hypercar he cannot enter The Heights without.
- **Property can be sold** at market price minus a fee — no loss, no gain. It is a place to store money, not a way to make it.

**A property sits in a district, and living in a district makes that district cheaper in time** ([Time](time.md)):

| Effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Upkeep** | NPCs in the district you live in do not decay; you already see them ([Decay](relationships.md#decay)). |
| **Workshop** | Work at a shop in your own district finishes one in-game day sooner, to a minimum of one. |
| **Events** | No effect. Every event in Nitroit is available to everyone, always. |

**Where Jake lives becomes a statement about who he is keeping.** A player in Ridgeline holds their drift relationships almost for free and pays full price for everyone else. A player in the Flats is next to nobody in particular and everybody equally — which is what The Yard is. A warehouse in the Docks plus a house on the Ridge is a legitimate and common configuration: the player saying, in the only language the clock understands, who they intend to keep.

## What property never does

- It does not unlock events, districts, crews or content.
- It does not affect REP, Standing or any festival system directly.
- It does not appear in the story. No cutscene happens in Jake's house and no NPC visits it.
- There is no decorating, furniture or interior customisation. The garage is the point.

**Jake's living situation improves across the campaign and the game never remarks on it.** He goes from a hostel bunk to a house with six cars in it over five acts, entirely on money he earned. Nobody in Nitroit ever knows he could have skipped all of it.
