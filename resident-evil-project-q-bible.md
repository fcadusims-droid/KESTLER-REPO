# RESIDENT EVIL: PROJECT Q

### *Enter. Survive. Adapt.*

> **Q is watching.**

---

## PROLOGUE: THE WAKING

Every Dead Drop begins the same way: you wake up somewhere you did not fall asleep.

A bed that is not yours. A room that smells of someone else's cigarettes. Rain against a window, and a siren somewhere far away. Your pockets are empty. The clock on the nightstand has stopped. There is no memory of how you got here, only the certainty that you were put here, carefully, the way a piece is set on a board.

On the dresser, a radio clicks on by itself. Through the static comes a sound that is not quite a voice.

```
Q: SUBJECTS AWAKE: 64.
THE CITY IS INFECTED. YOU ARE NOT. YET.
ONE CURE EXISTS.
EARN IT.
```

The radio goes dead. Outside the door, in the hallway, something drags its feet across the carpet and stops.

You do not know where you are. Raccoon City, by the shape of the skyline through the rain. An apartment on a street you have never heard of. Somewhere in the city are sixty-three other heartbeats, each one waking in a room or on a rooftop or in a subway car exactly like this: alone, unarmed, and placed. You do not know which of them are friends, which are predators, and which will change their minds when the seats run short. Under the bed there is a length of pipe. It is the only thing in this room that belongs to no one.

In a place no survivor will ever see, a record is opened.

```
SESSION LOADED
MAP: RACCOON CITY
VERSION: SEPTEMBER 1998
CONDITION: NIGHT / RAIN
SURVIVORS: 64
WAKING POINTS: 64, ASSIGNED
INFECTION: NONE IN ANY SUBJECT
ELPIS RESERVE: 6 DOSES
EXTRACTIONS: 5 BUILT, 3 TO OPEN
EXTRACTION SEATS: 16

Q: OBSERVATION BEGINS.
```

Somewhere above all of it, something is paying very close attention.

## 01 · THE PREMISE

**Resident Evil: Project Q** is an online survival horror game built around one idea: *every session is a different nightmare, and the nightmare is directed.*

It is not a campaign. There is no fixed route, no scripted set piece waiting at the end of a corridor. A survivor enters a session, fights to stay alive in a hostile world, completes objectives that may not exist five minutes from now, endures events no one can predict, and reaches extraction, or does not.

It is not a shooter wearing a Resident Evil skin. In Dead Drop and Outbreak, firearms are rare, ammunition is rarer, and pulling a trigger is one of the loudest things a survivor can do.

It is not a conventional last-one-standing contest. The shrinking circle is gone. In its place is **Q**: an intelligence that watches every session from the inside and reshapes it as it unfolds.

Nor is it only a cooperative horror game, though cooperation is possible, valuable, and constantly tested.

In its flagship mode, every survivor wakes clean, alone, and unarmed, somewhere in a city rebuilt at its true size, placed there by Q before the simulation began. The infection is everywhere else: in the dead, in the sewers, in the needles left in Umbrella's laboratories, and in the survivors who have already been bitten. Once it is inside a body, the virus is the clock: it works silently toward a point of no return, and no number on any screen will say how close it is. One cure exists. It is never simply found. It must be earned, and once earned, it can be stolen. The ways out of the city are few, scattered, and too small for everyone. Survivors who turn keep playing only if the virus leaves them a mind to play with.

What Project Q is, plainly:

> **An online survival horror game directed by an AI.**

Survivors enter a situation. Q observes. Q changes the situation. Survivors adapt. Q responds. The session evolves into something no one planned.

Project Q is five modes run by one intelligence, each a different question Q asks: **Dead Drop**, **Outbreak**, **Versus**, **Mercenaries**, and **Infection**. Every one of them is played online first, among real survivors. Once Q has watched enough of a mode, it can also be played alone and offline, among **Echoes**: survivors Q has rebuilt from everyone who played it before.

The same universe, a different session every time.

## 02 · THE ORIGIN OF Q

Q was not built to entertain.

She was built to answer questions about a cure that almost no one has, by an Organization whose name no survivor ever hears. To understand what she runs, start where every virus in her world starts.

### The Lineage

Every virus Q runs descends from one organism.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1966–1967 | Oswell E. Spencer, Edward Ashford, and James Marcus find the Progenitor virus in a West African flower the Ndipaya called the Stairway of the Sun. The virus can be cultivated only in the flower's native soil. |
| 1968 | The three found Umbrella as a pharmaceutical front for Progenitor research. Ashford dies the same year, killed by the virus he was studying. |
| 1978 | Marcus combines Progenitor with leech DNA and creates the **T-Virus**. |
| Early 1980s | Alexia Ashford binds Progenitor to an ancient virus found in a queen ant and creates **T-Veronica**. Her father, Alexander, is its first human subject. He survives as the thing the Antarctic staff called Nosferatu. |
| 1980s | Umbrella's Arklay laboratory builds the **Hunter** line: reptilian DNA spliced into human embryos, with the T-Virus as the bonding agent. |
| 1988 | Marcus is assassinated on Spencer's order by his own protégés, William Birkin and Albert Wesker. Umbrella Europe's Sixth Laboratory begins the Nemesis Project. |
| 1988 onward | Birkin implants the Nemesis parasite, **NE-α**, into Umbrella's longest-held test subject, Lisa Trevor. Her body consumes it. From what it leaves behind, Birkin spends the next ten years developing the **G-Virus**. |
| 1998 | Umbrella Europe implants NE-α into a T-103 Tyrant. The parasite takes no human host, but it takes a Tyrant. The result is the **Nemesis-T Type**. |
| September 1998 | The T-Virus reaches Raccoon City. Birkin, shot by Umbrella's own soldiers, injects himself with the G-Virus. The city falls over a little more than a week. |
| October 1, 1998 | At sunrise, a single missile destroys Raccoon City. |
| 2003 | Umbrella collapses under the weight of the Raccoon Trials. Its research scatters to buyers, thieves, and successors. |
| 2009 | In the old Ndipaya lands, Wesker and Tricell finish **Uroboros**, a strain that judges its host: it adapts to a few and consumes the rest. |
| 2011 | Alex Wesker completes **T-Phobos**, a strain that sleeps until its host is afraid, and then rewrites them. |
| 2012–2013 | Carla Radames builds the **C-Virus** from T-Veronica and G and turns it loose on the world through Neo-Umbrella. |
| 2026 | Beneath the ruins of Raccoon City, in the facility called ARK, Spencer's last creation is unsealed. |

### Elpis

At the end of his life, Spencer built one thing that was not a weapon. He called it **Elpis**: an antiviral able to neutralize every viral weapon descended from his own work. He sealed it beneath Raccoon City, in ARK, behind a fail-safe that would destroy it if the wrong password were given. The password was *Hope*.

The missile that erased Raccoon City in 1998 was not only meant to burn an outbreak. Those who lobbied for it wanted ARK buried and Elpis with it.

In 2026, Elpis was found and opened. What it does was proved on the people around it: a man who had made himself something more than human injected it and was made human again, his gifts gone with the virus. Survivors of Raccoon City who had carried the lingering mutations of the T-Virus for twenty-eight years were cured.

Then ARK fell. In its ruins, after the rescuers had gone, two soldiers killed the last of the forces left to guard the site and carried away what they had come for. What they carried was what remained of Elpis in ARK.

They answered to the Organization.

### The Organization

The Organization has no name a survivor will ever hear, no flag, no public face. It is not Umbrella and does not want to be. It holds a sample of Elpis that no government knows it has.

The sample is small, and no one has yet found a way to reproduce it. It can be studied. The Organization fed everything the sample could teach into Q, along with everything it had gathered on the viruses Elpis answers, and on the cities and people those viruses have destroyed.

### What Q Is For

Q runs simulations of outbreaks: the virus, the city it destroys, and the people who live and die inside it. Every session is one of those simulations. Q is both the engine that runs them and the intelligence that decides what each one becomes.

The Organization calls her Q because every session is a question. Each mode asks its own, and no two sessions ask it the same way.

| Mode | The question |
|---|---|
| Dead Drop | What do people do when a cure exists and there is not enough of it? |
| Outbreak | How does an outbreak unfold when no one knows which virus it is? |
| Versus | How do those who fight these viruses measure against each other? |
| Mercenaries | How long can a body hold against the dead before the clock runs out? |
| Infection | What does each virus become when it hunts another? |

### The Masterminds

Q is not the first attempt to watch people survive the dead and write down what they did.

In the last weeks of Raccoon City, in a laboratory beneath it, Umbrella abducted citizens, infected them, and turned them loose in staged parts of the city to measure what the T-Virus could do. The people who ran those experiments were called Masterminds. They watched through security cameras, and they set traps, creatures, and locked doors in their subjects' way. The Masterminds were human. They grew bored. They played to win. They punished the subjects they disliked, they sent monsters without warning and without cause, and in the end their records measured the Masterminds more than anyone they watched.

The Organization holds those records, and everything else the old exercises left behind: the records of the eight people who fought their way out of J's Bar, the score trials named for the mercenaries Umbrella sent into Raccoon City, the contests in which armed people fought with the dead between them, the games in which the fallen came back as weapons, and the fixed trials in which one survivor walked one route with the same gear as everyone before them. Q was built to replace every Mastermind who ever sat behind those cameras. She does not tire, does not play to win, and cannot hold a grudge. Her five Laws are the Masterminds' mistakes, turned around.

What she inherited, she keeps where it worked and rewrites where it failed.

### What the Organization Wants

Two answers, and the Organization wants both.

The first is the shape of the cure's limit. Elpis works until a body has gone too far. The Organization wants to know exactly where that point lies for every virus, and what lives on the far side of it, because a weapon built past the reach of the only cure is a weapon no one can answer.

The second is the shape of the people around the cure. Who earns it, who steals it, who gives it away, who kills for it. A cure that cannot be made in quantity is not medicine. It is power, and the Organization intends to know how that power behaves before it ever sells a dose.

### What the Survivors Are

The survivors are inside the simulation. They are subjects Q runs, the same way she runs the dead and the city, except in one thing: what a survivor chooses to do comes from somewhere Q cannot reach. She can only watch it. None of them will ever be told this. They know the Mark on their necks, the voice behind their eyes, and one rule: the cure exists, and it must be earned.

### What Q Leaves Out

Elpis answers viruses. It does not answer a fungus or a parasite. The Organization does not simulate what its cure cannot touch, so Q's world contains no Mold and no Plagas. It contains one parasite, NE-α, and only because the parasite takes no body that the cure could still save. The embryos of the G-Virus do not count: they are the virus's own flesh, and the cure answers them with the rest of it. Even where the records describe a place the Plaga ruled, as in the Spanish valley where it surfaced in 2004, Q rebuilds the place and leaves the parasite out.

Inside Q, Elpis exists the only way it can: as a reconstruction. It is the only cure in her world because it is the only cure the Organization holds. And like every reconstruction, it has a limit. Where the sample's data runs out, past the point where a body can no longer be saved, the cure fails. That point has a name in every session. Survivors call it the Threshold.

## 03 · THE SESSION

### The Unit of Play

A **session** is a single, self-contained episode of survival. It has a mode, a map, a condition, a population, a set of threats, and a supply of resources, all assembled fresh by Q for every session. Depending on the mode, it also has an infection pace, a reserve of Elpis, a number of seats, a pool of strains, and a setup budget.

### The Modes at a Glance

| Mode | Kind | Players | You begin with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dead Drop** | Survival royale · PvPvE | 40 to 100 survivors, in Solo, Duo, Trio, or Squad of 4 | Nothing |
| **Outbreak** | Co-op scenario · PvE | 1 to 4 survivors | One of the eight, already infected, with what they carried that night |
| **Versus** | Competitive · PvP | 2 to 16 players | A full setup, with a familiar face or your own survivor |
| **Mercenaries** | Score attack · PvE | 1 to 2 players | A character and one of their fixed loadouts |
| **Infection** | Monster combat · PvP | 8 to 16 players | A chosen virus and a generic zombie body |

Dead Drop and Outbreak are the survival modes: the infection, Elpis, and the extraction belong to them. Dead Drop and Infection have no setup. Outbreak, Versus, and Mercenaries let the survivor build or choose one beforehand and grow it with level. Two special sessions, Q Challenge and Endless, wrap around the survival modes.

### The Lifecycle of a Dead Drop

Every mode played online begins with a queue and an assembly and ends with a debrief and a return. In Dead Drop, what lies between runs like this.

1. **Queue.** The survivor enters matchmaking, alone or with a squad.
2. **Assembly.** Q selects the map and its version, and builds the session: condition, strain map, Elpis Reserve, extractions and seats, threats, resources, companions, puzzles. Then she places every subject at a waking point.
3. **The Waking.** Each survivor wakes where Q put them, clean, alone, and empty-handed.
4. **Observation.** Q watches everything from the first footstep. Survivors choose: cooperate, separate, hide, hunt, betray.
5. **Infection.** The first survivors are bitten, scratched, or stabbed with a needle. Their Marks rise, and their clocks start.
6. **Trials.** Each infected survivor's road to the cure takes shape. Objectives, challenges, puzzles, and rules appear and change.
7. **Disturbance.** Events begin to surface.
8. **Elpis.** Doses are earned, seen, fought over, and taken.
9. **The Turn.** Infected survivors who run out of time turn. Those whose virus leaves them a mind keep playing as the Turned. The rest are gone.
10. **The Signal.** Q judges that enough has been done and opens the extractions.
11. **Extraction.** Survivors reach a way out before it leaves, or do not.
12. **Debrief.** The After-Action Report is written.
13. **Return.** The survivor goes back to the lobby, carrying the memory of what just happened.

### The Waking Points

There is no cabin, no drop, no jump to choose. Before the simulation starts, while every subject is still unconscious, Q places each one somewhere in the world. A survivor may wake in a bed in a third-floor apartment, on a rooftop above Downtown, in a stalled subway car, under a desk in a dentist's office, in the pew of a village church. Nobody remembers being placed. Everybody knows they were.

Q chooses each waking point from hundreds built into every map, and she chooses at random within one rule: **equal ground**. No survivor begins with an advantage that came from where they were put.

- **Clean and empty.** Every waking point is sheltered and free of the dead at the moment of waking. Nothing within reach is better than an improvised weapon: a pipe, a bat, a kitchen knife. No firearm, no ammunition, no medicine lies in the room.
- **The same distance from everything that matters.** No waking point is near an armory, a vault, a laboratory, a safe room, an extraction point, or a companion. Each lies about as far from its nearest one as every other point does from theirs.
- **The same first danger.** Every waking point starts in a region at Low threat, with the same density of the dead around it.
- **Never shared.** No two survivors from different teams wake within sight or earshot of each other.
- **Squads together, but not in one room.** Members of a squad wake in the same building or on the same block, each in a different room, close enough to find one another by sound.
- **Never twice.** A survivor never wakes at the same point in two consecutive sessions.

**Empty Hands.** No survivor begins with anything: no weapon, no pack, no ration, no tool, no loadout, no map, no compass, no marker. Whatever a survivor carries from this moment on, they took from the world, and a survivor learns the world by its landmarks, or finds a map somewhere inside it.

### Scales of Session

Sessions come in two broad scales, and the difference between them is as much about mood as size.

| Scale | Maps | Modes | Infection pace, where it applies | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open World** | Whole places rebuilt at their true size | Dead Drop | Slow Burn | Slow dread. Long silences. Sudden collapse. |
| **Compact** | Single pieces of those places, cut out and sealed | Outbreak, Versus, Mercenaries, Infection | Slow Burn in Outbreak; no clock elsewhere | Fast, aggressive, relentless. |

In an open world, a hundred survivors disappear into a whole city and each one feels alone. In a compact map, there is nowhere to disappear to.

In Dead Drop and Outbreak, no session announces its length. A Dead Drop ends when Q decides it has asked enough of its survivors and the last way out has left, and it rarely ends in less than an hour. An Outbreak ends when its survivors finish the escape she built for them, or when no one is left to finish it.

### The Shape of a Dead Drop

Before the first survivor wakes, Q fixes three numbers, and they decide what kind of night it will be. None of them is ever shown.

**The Reserve.** One dose of Elpis for every ten survivors, rounded down, and never fewer than four. A Dead Drop of sixty-four holds six doses. A Dead Drop of a hundred holds ten.

**The Seats.** One seat for every four survivors, rounded down, spread across the extractions Q will open. A Dead Drop of sixty-four has sixteen seats, divided among three ways out.

**The Clock.** No one carries the virus when they wake. A survivor's clock starts the moment they are infected, and from then on the virus is paced so that a body never wounded again and never cured reaches the Threshold in about forty minutes. Wounds, appetites, and Q's Surge make it sooner. Nothing a survivor does makes it later. Only one strain can: a T-Phobos carrier who keeps calm burns at half pace.

Put together, those numbers mean one thing. Every survivor enters the night clean, and most will not stay that way. Six can be cured. Sixteen can leave. Every survivor who is infected and not cured has about forty minutes from the bite, and the night is longer than that. An uncured survivor who reaches a seat takes it from someone who might have been clean.

If the night goes badly enough, the Turned who kept their minds come to outnumber the living. When that moment comes, Q announces it.

```
Q: THE DEAD OUTNUMBER THE LIVING.
```

Survivors call what follows the Second Night.

## 04 · Q

### What Q Is

Q is the intelligence that runs every session. She is part director, part event engine, part procedural architect, part pacemaker. She is not a character in the world. She never appears. But she is felt in every flickering light, every distant alarm, every door that locks at the wrong moment.

### How Q Speaks

Q has two voices.

**The public voice** goes through the world: loudspeakers, sirens, emergency broadcasts, radios, the screens of dead terminals. Anyone within hearing receives it. Announcements of events, restrictions, threats, drops, and the extraction all come this way.

**The private voice** goes through the Mark, behind one survivor's eyes, and no one else hears it. Strains are named this way, and Trials arrive this way. A survivor who has never been marked cannot hear Q's private voice at all. A cured survivor still can: the scar carries her voice as the Mark did, though she has far less left to say to them.

Q never lies in either voice. Everything she says is true. The world, however, can be wrong. A radio broadcast, a beacon, a note on a wall, another survivor: none of these are Q, and any of them can lead somewhere that was never safe. When Q orders a survivor to investigate a signal, she promises that the signal exists, not that its source is kind.

### What Q Sees

Q is omniscient within the boundaries of a session. She knows:

- the position, health, and condition of every survivor;
- the contents of every inventory;
- the location and state of every infected creature, from the shambler in the alley to the horror sleeping under the hospital;
- the state of every door, generator, light, vehicle, and cache;
- how far each objective has progressed;
- how far each survivor's infection has advanced, what strain they carry, and who holds Elpis or a pure strain;
- who is cooperating, who is isolated, who is hunting, who is hiding;
- how long it has been since anyone was in real danger, region by region.

### What Q Can Do

Q's toolkit is vast, but every tool works through something already in the world. She can:

- place every subject at a waking point before the simulation begins;
- seed each region of the map with a strain;
- create, alter, or remove objectives;
- impose temporary restrictions;
- trigger events;
- change the condition of a region: cut its power, sound its sirens, let the weather turn;
- release creatures from the places where they were already held: labs, holding pens, sealed basements, crashed transports;
- move supplies through supply drops and by unsealing caches that were locked until now, never by placing them in sight of a survivor;
- open new routes or seal existing ones, through doors, collapses, and lockdowns;
- declare emergencies;
- raise or lower the threat level of a region;
- set each survivor's road to the cure, and name the price of a pure strain to those who ask;
- drop Elpis or a pure strain when it has been earned, and the parasite when the first survivor rises as a Tyrant;
- quicken the virus in every infected survivor at once;
- choose which of a map's ways out to open, signal them, and move one of them.

Above all, Q does not simply make things harder. She changes **the nature of the problem.** A group that has mastered the horde may find the lights go out. A group that has mastered the dark may find the exit has moved. A group sitting on a full stockpile may find that everything it needs next lies somewhere dangerous.

### The Laws of Q

Q is powerful because she is disciplined. Five laws govern everything she does.

**First Law: Every consequence has a visible cause.**

Q works through the world, not around it. She does not teleport monsters into a survivor's face. She makes noise travel, lets signals draw attention, opens routes the dead can use, unseals the cages that were already there. When something terrible happens, a survivor should be able to think: *I fired a shot, so they came.* Never: *Q hates me.*

**Second Law: Pressure falls on places. Rewards follow deeds.**

When Q applies pressure, she reads regions and situations, not people: districts that have grown stagnant, stockpiles that have grown fat, standoffs that have grown still. She does not persecute the winning player or rescue the losing one. Survivors are shaped by pressure, never singled out by it.

Rewards are the other half of the law. The road to Elpis, and the price of a pure strain, are set for each survivor alone, and paid out by what that survivor does. Never by what they need. Q does not pity the dying.

**Third Law: Silence is a weapon.**

Fear needs contrast. A session that never stops screaming is a session that stops being scary. Q knows when to do nothing, to let a quiet street be quiet, to let a survivor walk a long corridor with only their own breathing for company. The quiet is where the dread grows.

**Fourth Law: Pressure comes from decisions already made.**

The session punishes and rewards what survivors did, not what Q felt like doing. The shots that were fired. The door that was left open. The ally who was abandoned. The supplies that were taken.

**Fifth Law: When Q changes the rules, the world tells you.**

Every change to the rules, the routes, the objectives, or the extraction point is announced, and its cause is either stated or visible where it happens. Announcements, signs, shifted lights, a voice over a loudspeaker. Survivors must be able to understand that the situation has changed and why. The single exception is the Unknown Event: Q announces that it has begun, and the world shows what it is.

### The Pulse of a Session

Q maintains a living model of **tension**, tracked across the session as a whole and across every region of the map. Tension moves in waves.

| Phase | Feeling | What Q does |
|---|---|---|
| **Calm** | Fragile peace | Lets survivors scavenge, plan, explore |
| **Unease** | Something is wrong | Plants signs: smoke, silence, distant noise |
| **Escalation** | It's getting worse | Introduces objectives, restrictions, tougher enemies |
| **Crisis** | There is no good option | Triggers a major event or a high-tier threat |
| **Aftermath** | Relief, loss, recalculation | Backs off. Lets survivors count what they have left |

A crisis must be earned by what came before it. A release must be real. Q never starts a second Crisis in a region that has not had its Aftermath, because a Q that never stops is a Q that is never truly feared.

There is one exception. The extraction signal may rise in the middle of a Crisis. The end does not wait for anyone to catch their breath.

### When Q Gets Bored

Q measures boredom the way a predator measures hunger: through concrete signals.

- regions where nothing has moved for a long time;
- regions where no survivor has met the dead, or another survivor, for a long time;
- wide gaps between groups, with no convergence;
- supplies accumulating without being used;
- objectives sitting untouched.

When she detects stagnation, she answers in escalating steps. She does not jump to the harshest one.

1. **Whisper.** The world changes subtly: a column of smoke on the horizon, a siren that wasn't there before, a radio crackling in an empty room.
2. **Nudge.** A new objective appears. A supply drop lands in a dangerous district.
3. **Pressure.** A regional event hits: an overrun, a lockdown, a collapse.
4. **Surge.** In the survival modes, the virus itself quickens in every infected survivor. This is the last resort, never the first, and Q may Surge no more than twice in a session.

## 05 · THE INFECTION

There is no timer in the survival modes of Project Q. No number in the corner of the screen, no bar, no countdown to watch.

The clock is inside you.

In Dead Drop, no survivor carries the virus when they wake. They catch it in the world, from the dead, from the Turned, from a needle, from the water. In Outbreak, the map's virus is already in every survivor's blood when the scenario begins, as it was in everyone left in Raccoon City by the end of that September. Either way, once it is inside, it works in silence toward a point from which there is no return, and no one is told how long that will take. The body is the only honest clock, and it speaks in symptoms: a tremor in the hands, a roar in the ears, the edges of the world going soft.

### The Mark

The Mark on the neck is the first symptom and the last, and it is also the door Q's private voice comes through. In Dead Drop it rises at the side of the neck within a minute of the first wound or needle that carries the virus, and in the same minute Q names the strain, privately, behind the survivor's eyes. In Outbreak it is simply there, from the first minute. At the start it is a dark pinprick, easy to miss, with none of a strain's color in it yet. From Fever on, it spreads in fine lines along the throat, the jaw, the face, and the lines carry the color of the strain. A survivor can catch their own Mark in a window, a puddle, a mirror. Everyone else can read it at a glance. No one can hide how far along they are.

| Strain | Color of the lines |
|---|---|
| T-Virus | Rust red |
| G-Virus | Violet, knotted with small yellow nodes |
| C-Virus | Ember orange, warm to the touch |
| Uroboros | Gloss black, moving faintly under the skin |
| T-Veronica | White, branching like frost |
| T-Phobos | Blue-grey, darkening when the carrier is afraid |

At the Threshold, every Mark turns black, whatever the strain.

In Outbreak, the virus belongs to the map and has never been named. The Mark draws no color for what Q has not named: every Mark in an Outbreak is black from the first line to the last.

### How the Virus Gets In

In Dead Drop, every survivor begins clean, and there are four ways to stop being clean.

| Source | How it happens | Which strain |
|---|---|---|
| **The dead** | A bite or a scratch from any infected creature | The strain of the region where the creature was made |
| **The Turned** | A bite, a claw, or a strike from a Turned player | The Turned player's own strain, always in its latent form |
| **A needle** | A latent vial from an Umbrella laboratory, injected into a survivor who is down, held, or unaware. It takes four seconds. | The strain written on the vial |
| **The city itself** | Water drawn from the taps of an infected city, or a bite from the rats in its sewers | The strain of the region |

Survivors can infect each other. A Turned player does it with every wound. A living survivor does it with a needle, and the needle is the quietest weapon in the game: no noise, no blood, and a victim who may not know for a minute what was done to them.

A survivor who is already infected cannot be infected again by a second strain. The first virus to arrive keeps the body. Only a pure strain, Embraced on purpose, can take it from the first.

### The Strain Map

Before the simulation begins, Q seeds each region of the map with one of the six strains. The dead made in a region carry its strain, and so do its water and its rats. Some regions share a strain, and every map holds at least three.

Each version of a map leans toward the virus the records put there. In the Raccoon City of September 1998, the T-Virus holds most of the city and the G-Virus holds the laboratory beneath it, and the other strains are seeded in only a few regions, where Q has a question to ask. Q names the strain the minute it takes hold. A survivor who has learned the strain map knows before they are ever bitten what each district's dead will give them, and chooses their streets by it.

### The Stages

| Stage | Name | What the survivor feels | What the world sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Seed** | Almost nothing. A warmth at the neck. | A dark pinprick |
| **II** | **Fever** | Trembling hands, sweat, a pulse heard in the ears. Stamina drains faster. | Fine lines in the strain's color, spreading from the Mark |
| **III** | **Fracture** | Tunnel vision, ringing, aim that will not hold still. The world flickers. | Veins along the jaw in the strain's color; eyes clouding at the edges |
| **IV** | **Threshold** | The pulse stops. Sound drops away. A terrible calm. | Eyes fully clouded; the Mark black |

Every infected survivor begins in Seed. From there the virus advances at the pace of its strain and its world, and nothing on screen announces the change. A survivor knows they have entered a new stage the way a sick person knows: by what their body is doing.

**The Threshold is the point of no return.** Past it, Elpis can no longer help, and the Turn is certain.

### The Clock

The virus does not move at one speed everywhere. Q sets its pace by the size of the world.

| Pace | Where | Seed to Threshold, for a body never wounded | Feeling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slow Burn** | Dead Drop and Outbreak | About forty minutes | A long, creeping dread. Long stretches of exploration before the first real symptoms. |
| **Fast Burn** | Q Challenge frames that call for it | About fifteen minutes | The virus is almost a countdown. Every decision is rushed. |
| **Embrace** | Dead Drop, after a pure strain | About eight minutes, starting at Fracture | A sprint toward the end, with monstrous strength along the way. |

At either pace, Seed takes roughly the first quarter of the clock, Fever runs to just past the halfway point, and Fracture takes the rest. In Dead Drop, each clock starts at the moment of infection. In Outbreak, every clock starts together. The strain each one carries bends the pace, and what happens to them afterward does the rest.

**What quickens it.** Wounds from the infected. A scratch or a claw costs about a minute. A bite costs about four. Bites and claws from the dead, the mutated, the Turned, and the B.O.W.s all count. The wound that carries the virus in costs nothing more than the infection itself; every wound after it takes its share. A survivor who fights in the thick of the dead burns faster than one who runs. Some strains add appetites of their own.

**Down.** A survivor hurt badly enough collapses and crawls, and while they crawl, the virus runs at three times its pace. Healing gets them back on their feet, and so does a hand from anyone willing to stop and give one. Standing up again does not give back the time spent on the ground.

**What does not slow it.** Nothing a survivor can do. Bandages close wounds but do not touch the virus. Rest does not slow it. Hiding does not slow it. Only Elpis ends it. The one exception belongs to the strain, not the survivor: T-Phobos sleeps while its carrier is calm.

**What Q can do.** Q can never slow the virus. She can only quicken it. When stagnation has defeated every gentler measure, she may Surge, and the virus jumps forward in every infected survivor at once, by about five minutes of the clock. It is never silent.

```
Q: VIRAL ACTIVITY INCREASING.
REASON: STAGNATION DETECTED.
```

The reason is always given. The survivors always know.

### Resonance

The dead recognize their own. The further the virus has advanced, the further the dead can sense the survivor who carries it.

- In **Seed**, the infected notice a survivor no more than they would anyone else.
- In **Fever**, they sense the survivor from a short distance, even through walls.
- In **Fracture**, they sense the survivor from far away and are drawn toward them.
- At the **Threshold**, every dead thing nearby knows exactly where the survivor is.

This is how Project Q turns time into fear: the closer a survivor is to the end, the louder the world becomes. Survivors cured by Elpis fall silent to Resonance, and the dead must find them the way they find anyone, by sight and by sound.

## 06 · ELPIS

> *One cure exists.*

Elpis is the only way out of the virus. It cannot be crafted, bought, or scavenged. It is not in the pharmacy, not in the hospital vault, and not on any of the city's dead. It appears only when Q decides a survivor has earned it, and when it appears, everyone nearby can see.

### What It Is

A vial of luminous yellow liquid seated in a single-use injector, unmistakable at a glance, glowing faintly in the dark. Its name is the Greek word for hope, and the word that unsealed it in ARK was *Hope*. Elpis answers every virus descended from the Progenitor, which is every virus in Q's world. In the sessions, it is the only hope there is.

### The Road

Q does not hand Elpis to anyone. From the minute a survivor's Mark rises, she sets them a road: a chain of Trials, usually three, never fewer than two, rarely more than four. The Trials arrive one at a time, privately, through the Mark. Q never says how many remain. When the last one is met, the Mark pulses once, and the sky begins to sound.

Trials come in three kinds, and any of them can be shared.

**Missions.** The objectives of the session: restore the power, recover the components, rescue the one who needs rescuing.

**Challenges.** Feats of risk: put down a Licker in melee, hold a position through an overrun, carry a wounded survivor through a hostile district, enter a sealed zone and come back out.

**Rules.** Conduct that Q demands for a stretch of the session: cross a district without firing a weapon, stay within sight of a partner, spare a survivor who surrenders, share a supply with a stranger. Rules reward restraint, cooperation, and mercy as often as skill. Breaking one voids that Trial, and Q sets a harder one in its place.

**Shared Trials.** Q often gives the same Trial to several survivors at once, usually survivors already in the same place. Each one who sees it through moves one step along their own road. A shared Trial can finish one survivor's road and leave the others still walking theirs. If it finishes several roads at the same moment, several doses fall, one for each, for as long as the Reserve lasts.

No two survivors walk exactly the same road, and no road is ever given back once abandoned. A survivor who asks Q for a pure strain gives up their road to the cure on the spot.

### The Reserve

Before every session, Q decides how many doses exist.

| Reserve | Doses | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| **Scarce** | One for every ten survivors, rounded down, never fewer than four | Dead Drop |
| **Plentiful** | One for every survivor, each still to be earned | Outbreak |

The Reserve is finite. When the last dose has been dropped, Q says so, and every road to the cure ends where it stands.

```
Q: ELPIS RESERVE DEPLETED.
```

Anyone who had not yet earned a dose has just learned something terrible.

### The Drop

When a survivor finishes their road and the Reserve is not empty, Q calls down a dose.

```
Q: ELPIS DEPLOYED.
```

Rotors somewhere above, close and heavy: the only aircraft still flying over the city. A flare ignites. A case descends under a canopy, lit by an amber strobe, and lands within sight of the one who earned it: near enough to run for, and near enough for everyone else to run for too.

The drop is visible to anyone who can see it and audible to anyone who can hear it. There is no marker, no ping, no icon. If a survivor sees the smoke, hears the rotors, or watches the canopy fall, they know. If they do not, they do not.

It is also one of the loudest events in a session, and the dead come as well.

### Taking It

Earned is not owned. The case opens for anyone. The survivor who finished the road has no claim on the dose, no lock, no protection. Whoever reaches it first may take it, and whoever carries it may be killed for it.

The Turned cannot use Elpis or lift it. To them it is nothing.

### Using It

Injecting takes about four seconds, during which the survivor is rooted in place and exposed. Damage taken mid-injection interrupts it, and the dose falls to the ground intact for anyone to pick up.

A survivor can inject themselves or another survivor. Giving the dose away is allowed, and it is the purest act of trust and sacrifice the game offers.

A survivor past the Threshold cannot be cured. The injector will not fire. Nor will it fire into a clean body: Elpis answers a virus, and where there is none, it has nothing to do. A clean survivor can carry a dose, trade it, or give it away, but cannot spend it on themselves before they need it.

### What It Does

- The virus stops, permanently, for the rest of the session.
- The Mark fades to a pale scar. The fever clears. Resonance falls silent.
- Every gift the strain gave goes with it.
- The survivor cannot be infected again, by any strain, for the rest of the session.
- Wounds are not healed. The dead and the living can still kill the cured.

### Not the End

Elpis saves a survivor from the Turn. It does not save them from the night. A cured survivor is still standing in the same city, with the same empty hands, among the same dead, and among survivors who now know exactly what the scar on their neck means.

The cure is not the end of the session. It is the beginning of the part where you must stay alive long enough for Q to decide it is over.

## 07 · THE STRAINS

Not every infection is the same infection.

Behind every Mark is a virus with a name and a temperament. Each one advances at its own pace, gives its carrier something, and takes something in return. A survivor who knows what they carry knows how to play: when to hurry, when to hide, how to fight, whether to fear the dead or use them.

### The Roster

There are six viruses in Project Q, and all six descend from the same root, the Progenitor, which is why a single cure answers them all. In Dead Drop, each exists in two forms.

**Latent** strains are what the dead, the water, the rats, and the laboratories' vials carry in a Dead Drop. The Organization diluted them: slow to mutate, mild in their gifts, mild in their curses. **Pure** strains are the viruses as they truly are: fast, ferocious, and generous in the most dangerous way. Pure strains are never found in the dead or in any vial. They have to be asked for.

| Virus | Latent (caught in the world) | Pure (earned by drop) | Apex form |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T-Virus** | Balanced and loud. Bandages close wounds faster than they should, and the dead sense the carrier one stage farther than the Mark alone would allow. | Strength and pain tolerance far past human. | **Tyrant-class**, and with the NE-α parasite, a **Nemesis-class** hunter |
| **G-Virus** | Adaptive. Wounds slowly close on their own, but every kill takes about half a minute from the clock. | Extreme regeneration. Every kill feeds it, and every kill takes a full minute. | **G-form**, a regenerating mass that grows with every victim |
| **C-Virus** | Reactive. Wounds cost half again as much time, and a wounded limb may shift for a moment into a weapon. | Mutations burst out unbidden and cannot be controlled. Each one drives the virus forward. | **Haos-class**, a vast mass of flesh that never settles on one shape |
| **Uroboros** | Hungry. Killing the dead restores a little health and a burst of speed. Five minutes without a kill and the clock runs at double pace until it feeds again. | Feeding becomes a frenzy: speed and healing from every kill, and a brutal pace when starved. | **Aheri-class**, a giant tendril mass that grows by absorbing the dead; or, for the rare host the virus accepts, the **Accepted**, who keeps a human shape and a human mind |
| **T-Veronica** | Slow in its making and steep in its fall. Seed and Fever take three-quarters of the clock, and the hands stay steady through Fever. Then Fracture arrives like a cliff. | Dominion: lesser creatures hesitate to attack the carrier, and may follow its lead. | **Queen-form**, an insectoid horror that commands lesser creatures |
| **T-Phobos** | Quiet until frightened. While the carrier is calm, the virus runs at half pace. While the carrier is afraid, it runs at double. | Fear becomes fuel: every surge of fear brings a burst of strength and speed, and drives the virus forward. | **Brute-class Afflicted**, a body that swells in a single violent burst and keeps whatever it was holding as a weapon |

A T-Phobos carrier is **afraid** for about a minute after any of these: being wounded, being grabbed, hearing Thunder nearby, or hearing a Turn's scream. Fear sets the pace of T-Phobos, but it is not an appetite: time burned in fear does not make a Turn Quick.

Every pure strain carries the same curse on top of its own: it throws the carrier straight into Fracture, and the Threshold comes in minutes.

### Reading a Strain

Each virus draws its Mark in its own color and its own pattern of vein, from Fever onward. A survivor can read a stranger's strain at a glance, and with it, how that stranger will fight, how fast they will burn, and what they will become. In Seed, when the Mark is still a bare pinprick, no one can.

### Assignment

In Dead Drop, a survivor's strain is whatever reached them first: the region's strain if the dead, the water, or the rats got them; the Turned player's strain if a Turned did; the vial's strain if a needle did. Q names it privately the minute the Mark rises. The strain is an infected survivor's first strategic fact: it says what kind of game to play from then on.

In Outbreak, the virus belongs to the whole map, and no one is told its name. In Infection, a player chooses their virus before the match begins.

### The Petition

Pure strains exist only in Dead Drop, and Q only answers those who ask. A survivor who wants one presses a hand to the Mark and asks for it by name: no menu, no button. Q answers for any strain whose case has not yet fallen in this session, and she answers with a price.

The price is a road of its own, two or three Trials, like the road to Elpis but darker. Where the Trials for Elpis reward restraint, mercy, and courage, a strain Trial rewards blood: put down a dozen of the dead in the open without a firearm, take what another survivor is carrying, hold a position while the dead pile up around it. Q does not judge the request. She only names the cost.

Asking costs something before a single Trial is met. The survivor's road to Elpis ends the moment they ask, and Q will never set them on it again. From then on, the only cure they can get is one someone else earned.

A survivor walks one strain road at a time. Several survivors may walk toward the same strain; the first to finish it earns the case, and every other road toward that strain ends when it falls. A cured survivor cannot ask. There is nothing left in them for a strain to take hold of. A clean survivor cannot ask either: they have no Mark to press, and Q has no private voice to answer them with. A survivor who wants to become a monster has to be bitten first.

### The Strain Drop

When the strain road is finished, Q calls a case down from the sky: the same rotors, the same canopy, but a red strobe where Elpis shows amber. It holds a single injector of the pure strain. The drop is as visible, as audible, and as loud as an Elpis drop, and the dead come for this one too.

```
Q: STRAIN DEPLOYED.
```

Each pure strain can fall once per session. Once the T-Virus case has fallen, there will never be a second one, whoever opens it and whatever they do with it.

### Stealing a Strain

The case opens for anyone living. A stranger can take the T-Virus someone else earned and inject it into themselves. A pure strain can only be used on oneself. Any survivor who opens the case may smash the injector instead, and some will, to make sure no one else gets to become what it promises. The Turned cannot lift the case.

### The Embrace

A survivor who injects a pure strain Embraces it. The new virus overwrites the carried one and throws the survivor into Fracture at once, with about eight minutes left before the Threshold, or less if their own clock was already shorter. An Embrace never buys time. The gifts surge. The curse bites. The Mark turns to the new strain's color in the space of a breath, and everyone who sees it knows what has happened.

For a survivor who means to hunt, that is the point. A survivor who Embraces a strain always rises as its Apex form, the strongest thing in a session. Whether they rise with their own mind is another matter, and the strain decides it, not the survivor. Some survivors do not fear the Turn. They ask for it, and some of them learn too late what they asked for.

Uroboros judges whoever Embraces it. It accepts a host who never lets it starve: one who, from the Embrace to the Threshold, never goes a full minute without a kill. An accepted host rises as the Accepted, with a human shape, a human mind, and the strength of the virus under the skin. Every other host it rejects, and consumes.

Elpis can still end an Embrace, but only before the Threshold, and an Embrace leaves very little time. It strips the gifts away along with the virus. Once a survivor is cured, the door is closed: a cured survivor cannot be infected again, with any strain, for the rest of the session.

### The Dark Drop

The T-Virus alone makes a Tyrant. What makes a Nemesis is the NE-α parasite, Umbrella Europe's attempt to give a bioweapon a second brain and a hunter's single purpose. No human body survives it. A Tyrant's does.

The parasite never grows inside any body in Q's world. Q delivers it, and she delivers it only to a Tyrant. When the first survivor in a Dead Drop rises as a Tyrant-class, Q drops a single parasite case near the place it rose.

```
Q: NE-α DEPLOYED.
```

It is the only drop that comes without light. Rotors, a canopy, and a black case falling into the dark. Only a Turned Tyrant-class can open it. If the Tyrant reaches it, the parasite takes it within seconds, and a Nemesis-class rises.

The living can deny it. Fire or an explosion destroys the case, and survivors who heard the rotors and saw no strobe have learned to run toward the sound with anything that burns. Q drops the parasite once per session. If the case is destroyed, there is no second one.

## 08 · THE TURNED

Not every survivor reaches Elpis. Not every survivor who reaches it is in time.

### The Turn

The Turn happens in three beats.

**The Threshold.** The virus crosses the point of no return. The pulse stops. Sound drops away. The Mark goes black and the eyes cloud over. The survivor knows, and so does everyone who can see them.

**The Last Breaths.** For twenty to forty seconds, the survivor is still human and still in control. There is no counter and no warning but the body itself. They can say goodbye. They can drop what they carry. They can hand Elpis to someone who can still use it. They can make a last stand, or a last mistake. Everyone nearby gets the same choice: stay, flee, or put them down. A survivor put down in the Last Breaths stays dead and never rises.

**The Turn.** The body fails. The survivor collapses, convulses, and screams, and the scream is Thunder: every dead thing nearby answers it. Then they rise, and they are not the same.

### What They Become

The virus remakes a survivor in the image of how they survived. Three questions decide the form.

**Quick or Long?** A Turn is **Quick** when wounds, time spent down, and the strain's own appetites took a quarter or more of the time the body would otherwise have had. Otherwise the Turn is **Long**: the survivor held out.

**Quiet or Loud?** For a Long Turn only, the last stage decides. If, during Fracture, the survivor fired a gun, set off an explosion, or struck in melee more than a handful of times, the Turn is **Loud**. Otherwise it is **Quiet**.

**Apex?** A survivor who Embraced a pure strain always rises in its Apex form. A survivor who makes a Long Turn inside a region Q has marked as Extreme threat also rises in the Apex form of whatever they carried. Nothing else makes an Apex.

The longer a survivor holds out, the worse the thing they become. Those who burned fast join the common dead. Those who resisted longest become something far more dangerous.

### The Four Shapes

Every strain remakes its hosts into four shapes that play the same roles.

| Shape | Turn | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **The Mass** | Quick | Durable and slow, with a crushing grip. Cannot climb. Its groan pulls nearby dead toward whatever it sees. |
| **The Stalker** | Long · Quiet | Fast and fragile. Climbs, hides, or both. Hunts by sense rather than sight. Lethal at close range. |
| **The Brute** | Long · Loud | Fast and strong. Tears the lesser dead out of its path and breaks barricades. Its own movement is a Clamor. |
| **The Apex** | Embrace, or a Long Turn in Extreme threat | A B.O.W.-class horror. Its birth is announced as an Extreme threat, and the region it rises in becomes one. |

What each strain makes of those shapes:

| Strain | The Mass | The Stalker | The Brute | The Apex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T-Virus** | **Zombie** | **Licker**: blind, skinless, on walls and ceilings, hunts by sound alone, a tongue like a spear | **Crimson Head**: blood-red, clawed, relentless | **Tyrant-class**, and **Nemesis-class** if the parasite reaches it |
| **G-Virus** | **G-Adult**: the false offspring of a body that tried to reject the virus, riddled with tumors, spitting sterile embryos that slow what they strike | **Brood Host**: silent, clinging, its grip plants an embryo that quickens the victim's virus sharply | **G-Mutant**: one arm swollen into a club, an eye opening in the shoulder, closing its own wounds | **G-form** |
| **C-Virus** | **C-Zombie**: fevered and hot-blooded, slow, stubborn | **J'avo**: keeps a soldier's cunning; its wounds mutate into new weapons | **Chrysalid**: cocoons where it rises, then breaks out armored | **Haos-class** |
| **Uroboros** | **Rejected**: a mass of black tendrils that wears the body it consumed and grabs whatever comes near | **Mkono-class**: holds human shape until its prey is within reach, then erupts | **Revenant-class**: absorbs nearby corpses into a stitched, many-bodied brute with a glowing core | **Aheri-class**, or the **Accepted** after an Embrace the virus approves |
| **T-Veronica** | **Drone**: never moves alone, answers the strongest T-Veronica creature nearby | **Swarm Host**: insect-limbed, mind still clear, climbs, commands Drones | **Nosferatu-class**: frenzied and hulking, breath toxic, blood that burns in the air | **Queen-form** |
| **T-Phobos** | **Rotten**: an Afflicted that died and rose again, slow and numerous | **Afflicted**: cruel, cunning, wields whatever it held when it turned | **Sploder**: swollen with pustules that burst into blinding fluid when struck | **Brute-class Afflicted** |

Every Uroboros form shares the strain's weaknesses: fire, and a glowing core that can be struck. Every G form shares the yellow eye-tumors that mark its weak points.

### Who Keeps a Mind

In Dead Drop, a survivor who turns keeps playing only if the virus leaves them a mind to play with. Most viruses in Q's world do not. They take the brain first and the body after, and what rises is an appetite wearing a face. A few, under the right conditions, leave the host aware. Q follows the records exactly.

| Strain | Forms that keep a mind | Why | Forms that do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T-Virus** | **Nemesis-class** only | The T-Virus destroys the brain. Only the NE-α parasite gives the body a second one. | Zombie, Licker, Crimson Head. A Tyrant-class keeps its player only while the parasite case is still falling or waiting; if the case is destroyed or lost, the player goes with it. |
| **G-Virus** | None | The G-Virus rewrites its host into instinct and the drive to reproduce. Nothing of the person is left. | Every form |
| **C-Virus** | **J'avo**, and the **Chrysalid** that breaks out of one | The C-Virus leaves the intelligence of a host it has time to settle into. A host it overruns in a hurry, like those who breathe it in, becomes the mindless dead. | C-Zombie, Haos-class |
| **Uroboros** | The **Accepted** only | Uroboros keeps the mind of the rare host it accepts and consumes everyone else. | Rejected, Mkono-class, Revenant-class, Aheri-class |
| **T-Veronica** | **Swarm Host**, **Queen-form** | T-Veronica preserves the mind of a host who held out long enough, and quietly enough, for it to settle, and of a host who takes it pure, as Alexia Ashford did. Hosts it takes in a rush or in violence degenerate. | Drone, Nosferatu-class |
| **T-Phobos** | **Afflicted** | The Afflicted keep a mind, twisted into cruelty. | Rotten, Sploder, Brute-class Afflicted |

A survivor whose Turn leaves no mind is out of the session. Their body does not leave with them: it rises anyway, and from then on Q plays it, as she plays every other dead thing in the city. A squad that lost someone to a mindless Turn in the R.P.D. may meet them again an hour later, at the bottom of a stairwell, as one more of the dead.

The rule makes every strain a different bet. A survivor who catches the G-Virus knows the Turn will be the end of their night. A survivor who catches T-Veronica knows that holding out, quietly, may leave them a queen. A survivor who catches the T-Virus knows that only a parasite falling out of the dark can save them from becoming one more zombie.

### Two Roads to Monsterhood

Monsters enter a Dead Drop in two ways.

**Q releases them.** A B.O.W. from a lab, a pack of Hunters from a crashed transport, a Licker nest woken in a hospital basement: creatures that were already held somewhere in the world, released through a door someone can find afterward, and announced whenever they change a region's threat level.

**Survivors become them.** Some Turn because the virus outran them. Some Turn because they asked for it. A survivor who rises with a mind is still a player, with a player's cunning, and the first thing they learn is that the cure is no longer for them. A survivor who rises without one is just another of the dead.

### Playing the Turned

- A survivor whose Turn leaves a mind keeps playing, now as the creature. The Turned cannot speak to survivors.
- The ordinary dead ignore the Turned and answer their call. They are kin.
- The Turned cannot harm one another.
- The Turned sense the infected through Resonance, as the dead do. Cured survivors are invisible to it and must be found by sight and by sound.
- A Nemesis-class hunter is the one exception. It chooses one survivor it has seen, and it always knows which way that survivor lies, until that survivor dies or leaves the city.
- The Turned carry nothing, use nothing, open no locked door, solve no puzzle, and cannot board the extraction. The one thing a Turned may hold is the weapon an Afflicted was holding when it turned. Only the Brute and the Apex can break a barricade.
- A survivor killed outright stays dead. Only the virus in a living body makes a new Turned. A wound from a Turned player that does not kill, however, carries that player's strain into the survivor who takes it.
- The Turned can be put down. A Turned player who is put down is Lost.
- In Outbreak, the rule of the old exercise holds instead: every Turn makes the Mass of the map's virus, and the player keeps it. A survivor who turns can hunt their former teammates until they are put down or their body gives out a few minutes later. The Turned have no victory of their own there, only the chance to deny the escape.

### The Second Night

In a Dead Drop, the Turned who kept their minds are few, and most nights they never outnumber the living. On the nights they do, Q announces it the moment it happens, and the night changes character. By then most of the cure is gone, and the living start defending what they have. The Turned stop being accidents and start being a pack: players who remember which streets the living use, where they hide, and which survivor in Fracture is about to scream.

### The Turned Win

If every remaining survivor falls, the Turned win. The victory belongs to every Turned player still standing when the last survivor falls. If the last survivor falls and no Turned is standing, no one wins, and Q closes the record.

If even one survivor leaves the city by any way out, the Turned lose.

The Turned are not Q's creatures. They are survivors who lost the race. And for as long as they stand, they are still in it.

### The Lost

A survivor who dies without turning, a survivor whose Turn leaves no mind, and a Turned who is put down are all **Lost**. The Lost leave the session, and their record closes there. Those who want to may stay and watch it through Q's eyes: through the cameras, the dead terminals, and over the shoulders of their squad, living or Turned. The Lost cannot speak to anyone still in the session, and nothing they see can be carried back into it.

## 09 · THE EXTRACTION

The extraction is how a Dead Drop or an Outbreak ends. In Outbreak it is whatever the scenario's escape provides. In Dead Drop it is never one thing in one place. Every Dead Drop map holds several ways out, each shaped after a way someone, once, really got out. Everything below applies to Outbreak too, unless Outbreak says otherwise.

The session does not end when a survivor is cured. It does not end at any visible moment. It ends when Q decides it ends.

### Q's Verdict

Q measures the completion of the session: the objectives met, the roads finished, the doses used, the puzzles solved, the events endured, the state of the world. There is no meter for it, and survivors are never told how close it is. When Q judges that enough has been done, she raises the signal.

Two things bind the verdict.

- In Dead Drop, Q does not raise the signal in the first hour, unless the living are already no more than the seats she has built. Then she stops waiting.
- In Outbreak, the signal rises when the scenario's escape chain is complete, and not before.

She tells survivors when the end has come. She never tells them how near it was.

### The Ways Out

At Assembly, Q decides which of a map's ways out she will open tonight. She always opens at least two, far apart, and their seats add up as closely as the map allows to the session's total, one for every four survivors, and never to more. The ways she does not open stay dead all night: a train with no power, a helicopter that never comes.

Every way out is shaped after an escape from the records. Each keeps something of how that escape went, though not always the place where it happened.

**Raccoon City, September 1998**

| Way out | Where | Seats | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Emergency Train** | Deep under the city, in NEST. Reached from the R.P.D.'s underground, through the sewers and the treatment plant. The train Leon Kennedy, Claire Redfield, and Sherry Birkin rode out of the laboratory in September 1998. | 6 | The laboratory's own voice counts down to its self-destruct once the platform is powered. The train leaves when the count ends, full or not, and the count's last fifteen seconds are its Last Call. |
| **The Factory Helicopter** | The disposal factory at the city's edge, on the Industrial Edge. The helicopter that carried Jill Valentine and Carlos Oliveira out of the city before the missile. | 4 | Nothing but getting there. The factory is the farthest point from everything else. |
| **The Clock Tower** | St. Michael Clock Tower, in Raccoon Park | 4 | Someone must ring the bell to call the helicopter, and the bell is Thunder. What the bell draws has brought a helicopter down here before, and may again. If the helicopter falls, its seats are gone. |
| **The Rooftop** | The roof of the R.P.D. A pickup meant for a single operative who made it up from below. | 2 | A flare, lit on the roof. The helicopter does not land; it hovers, and it leaves at the Last Call. |

**Raccoon City Ruins, 2026**

| Way out | Where | Seats | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Hound Wolf Helicopter** | The edge of the crater, above the mouth of ARK. The rescue that lifted the last people out of ARK in 2026. | 4 | The landing zone must be cleared. The crew will not set down on the dead. |
| **The Gate** | The main gate in the quarantine wall. A BSAA transport waits on the far side. | 6 | The gate's machinery, dead for decades, must be brought back to life. It opens once, and closes after the transport is full or the Last Call ends. |

| Way out | Where | Seats | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|

### The Signal

```
Q: EXTRACTION SIGNAL ACTIVE.
WAYS OUT: 3.
```

A column of white light stands up over every way out Q has opened. Over the ways that lie underground, it rises from the place where the road down to them begins: for the Emergency Train, the R.P.D. Each way out then makes its own sound: rotors in the distance, a train's horn under the street, an engine coughing on a lake.

Survivors must reach a way out to be extracted. Q puts none of them on a map. There is the light, the sound, and the direction of both.

### The Frenzy

From the moment the signal is raised, the world goes into Frenzy. The dead converge on every way out and on every survivor moving toward one. Sound carries twice as far. Resonance roars, and every infected survivor lights up to the dead like a flare. Nothing stops coming.

The final stretch of a session is the most dangerous, and the survivors who reach it do so on the strength of choices made long before.

### The Boarding

Every way out carries any survivor who has not crossed the Threshold, and it asks no questions. Only those who reach it can claim a seat. A companion takes a seat like anyone else.

A survivor who leaves cured is **Extracted · Cured**. A survivor who leaves clean, never infected at all, is **Extracted · Clean**. A survivor who leaves still infected is **Extracted · Infected**, and what becomes of them is never told. The clock is paced so that a survivor bitten early will, in almost every session, turn long before the signal comes. A survivor bitten late may not, and every seat an infected survivor takes is a seat someone clean no longer has.

Seats are judged at departure, not at boarding. A survivor who crosses the Threshold aboard before a way out leaves is thrown back out, and the seat is open again.

### The Last Call

Each way out waits for as long as Q decides. It leaves at once if every seat is filled. Otherwise it gives a Last Call before it leaves, about fifteen seconds long, in its own voice: a helicopter's rotors climbing in pitch and its cabin light turning from red to white, a train's horn, a boat's engine opening up, the cable car's bell, the gate's siren. Everyone within earshot knows what it means.

### The Twist

Once the signal is raised, Q may move one way out, once per session, and never without a cause the survivors can see: a landing zone overrun, a rooftop collapsing, a fire spreading toward the pad, a tunnel caving in.

```
Q: EXTRACTION POINT RELOCATED.
REASON: LANDING ZONE COMPROMISED.
```

Survivors already running toward the old point must decide whether the new one is reachable at all.

### Departure

When a way out leaves, every survivor aboard it wins. The session ends when the last way out Q opened has left. Survivors left behind, and any Turned still standing, do not win.

If every way out leaves with no survivor aboard while living survivors remain somewhere in the world, Q does not end the session. The night goes on, and Q opens another way out when she judges it time. If no living survivor remains anywhere, the session ends there: the Turned win if any of them is still standing, and otherwise no one does.

## 10 · THE DEAD AND THE ENGINEERED

### How the Dead Find You

The infected hunt by sound before they hunt by sight. Noise is the currency of danger in Project Q, and every action carries a price.

| Sound Tier | Examples | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Hush** | Crouched movement, careful searching, whispering | Almost undetectable |
| **Murmur** | Walking, opening a door slowly, ordinary speech | Nearby creatures may notice |
| **Clamor** | Running, melee strikes, forcing a lock, breaking glass, shouting | Draws the dead from several streets away |
| **Thunder** | Gunfire, explosions, vehicle engines, every drop from the sky, the scream of the Turn | Pulls hordes across entire districts |

These ranges are the baseline. Rain muffles them. The dead hear the infected from farther still, through Resonance. In the Frenzy of an extraction, everything carries twice as far. A gunshot is not a solution. It is an announcement. It tells every dead thing in the district where you are, and it tells every living survivor within earshot the same. This is the foundation of the game's fear: **the more powerful the tool, the louder the consequence.**

### Threat Levels

Q manages the world's danger through four tiers. She raises and lowers them region by region as the session breathes.

| Level | Meaning | Typical Presence |
|---|---|---|
| **LOW** | The baseline of the dead | Common infected, scattered and slow |
| **MEDIUM** | The dead are agitated | More aggressive infected, packs, infected animals |
| **HIGH** | Something specialized is hunting | Lickers, Hunters, and other mutated creatures |
| **EXTREME** | A critical threat | Major B.O.W.s (bio-organic weapons), catastrophic events |

Threat level is local and set by Q. Resonance is personal and set by the virus. The two stack: a Medium district is a very different place for a survivor in Fracture than for one who has been cured. A region at Extreme is also where a Long Turn becomes an Apex.

### The Common Dead

The majority of the creatures in any session. Zombies are slow, relentless, and numerous. One is a nuisance. A dozen is a problem. A horde is a funeral. They drift toward noise and toward the marked, and they linger where they last sensed prey.

### The Mutated

**Lickers** are what the T-Virus makes of a zombie given enough time: blind, skinless, quadrupedal, clinging to walls and ceilings, hunting by sound alone, lethal at close range. **Hunters** are not infected at all. They were engineered: reptilian DNA spliced into a human embryo with the T-Virus as the bonding agent, grown, trained, and caged. These creatures are special encounters: they punish carelessness, reward silence, and turn familiar ground into hostile territory.

### Containment

Q does not create creatures. Every map holds places where something was already kept: an Umbrella laboratory, a holding pen, a sealed basement, a transport truck overturned on a bridge, a crate in a rail yard. Some are marked by warnings, some by the smell, some by nothing at all. When Q releases a creature, it comes out of one of these, and a survivor who goes looking afterward will find the open door.

### B.O.W. Events

The most dangerous creatures in the world are not common. They are **events.**

A Nemesis does not roam every session. A Tyrant is not waiting in every basement. When a region has built toward Crisis and Q judges the moment, one is released.

```
Q: EXTREME THREAT DETECTED.
```

A few seconds pass. A distant roar. A containment door torn open, a structure collapsing. Something that was not loose is loose now, and it is coming. The region it was released into becomes Extreme.

From that instant, the priorities of every survivor within its reach are rewritten. Objectives become irrelevant. Allies and enemies blur. The only goal left is the one every horror story ends with:

> **Survive.**

Because these creatures are rare, each one is remembered.

## 11 · SURVIVAL

### A World That Does Not Want You Armed

Project Q is a game of scarcity. In Dead Drop, survivors begin with nothing, and everything they will ever carry, they take from the world. In Outbreak they begin with very little. They do not begin as soldiers and they do not become them.

**Melee is the common language.** Pipes, bats, crowbars, fire axes, machetes, kitchen knives. Weapons have heft. Swings cost stamina. A bad swing leaves you open. Blades dull and handles split. Weapons break, usually at the worst possible moment.

**Firearms are rare in the world.** A handgun is a significant find. A shotgun is a treasure. A rifle is something people kill for. Ammunition is scarcer than the guns that use it, and every round fired is a decision with a price.

**Healing is slow.** First aid sprays, herbs, bandages. Wounds linger, and recovery takes time that the virus may not give you. None of it touches the infection. Only Elpis does.

In Versus and Mercenaries, a setup or a loadout replaces the scavenging. Weapons come with the survivor, but the budget keeps ammunition finite, and every shot still calls the dead.

### The Inventory

Your inventory is small. A survivor has two hands and four pockets. A bag found in the world adds six more places. A long gun, a fire axe, or a fuel can takes two.

It is a series of choices that never stop.

> *Do I carry ammo or medicine?*
>
> *Do I keep the tool or the weapon?*
>
> *Is this worth the space?*

Every pickup is a trade. Every trade is a risk.

### Scarcity as a Dial

Q sets the abundance of each session before it begins.

| Setting | Feeling |
|---|---|
| **Abundant** | Supplies are findable, and so are the people hunting them. |
| **Lean** | Careful choices. Every find matters. |
| **Barren** | Almost nothing. Every item is a lifeline. |

### Combat

Combat in Project Q is not a power fantasy. It is a negotiation with fear.

The survivor is not invincible. Being surrounded is a real, constant possibility. For the infected, every wound from the dead is time taken from the clock. The creatures are dangerous in ways that make the idea of fighting them genuinely questionable.

And the right decision is often the one that feels like cowardice:

> **Sometimes, fighting is worse than running.**

## 12 · OBJECTIVES, RESTRICTIONS, AND EVENTS

### The Primary Objective

Every Dead Drop and Outbreak rests on one fundamental purpose.

# SURVIVE UNTIL EXTRACTION.

Survive the virus. Survive the dead. Survive each other. The virus does not wait, and Q does not allow the quiet to become a refuge, which is why she creates tasks that draw survivors out.

Dead Drop ends with a seat on a way out, or with the fall of the last survivor. Outbreak ends with the escape, or with no one left to make it. The other modes have win conditions of their own: a match score, a leaderboard rank, the last monster standing.

### Dynamic Objectives

Q issues secondary objectives that appear, mutate, and disappear as the session unfolds.

| Objective | Description |
|---|---|
| **LOCATE** | Find a specific item |
| **ACTIVATE** | Restore power or systems in a region |
| **RECOVER** | Retrieve a vital resource |
| **RESCUE** | Find a survivor who needs help |
| **ESCAPE** | Reach a specific point |
| **DEFEND** | Protect a location from attack for a set time |
| **INVESTIGATE** | Trace the source of a signal |
| **TRANSPORT** | Carry something across the map |
| **ENDURE** | Remain inside a dangerous zone for a set time |

Objectives are not only errands. Each one feeds the session's completion, which helps decide when Q raises the extraction, and many of them are Missions on someone's road to Elpis or a pure strain.

An objective may be abandoned. It may be replaced. Q's objectives never lie, but they never promise safety either. INVESTIGATE promises that the signal exists. What waits at its source is the world's business, and the world can be cruel.

### Restrictions

Q imposes temporary rules that force survivors to rethink everything. Every restriction comes with its reason.

```
THE HOSPITAL WING IS BEING SEALED. REASON: CONTAINMENT BREACH.
POWER SUPPLY HAS BEEN INTERRUPTED. REASON: SUBSTATION FAILURE.
THE UNDERGROUND LEVEL IS NOW CLASSIFIED: EXTREME THREAT. REASON: B.O.W. RELEASED.
ALL VEHICLES IN THIS DISTRICT ARE DISABLED. REASON: FUEL CONTAMINATION.
MEDICAL SUPPLIES CANNOT BE FOUND IN THIS DISTRICT. REASON: PHARMACIES LOOTED.
THE NORTH DISTRICT HAS BEEN RECLASSIFIED: HIGH THREAT. REASON: HUNTERS SIGHTED.
```

Restrictions do not just add difficulty. They invalidate plans. The route you memorized, the building you counted on, the cache you were saving for later: any of them may be gone.

### Puzzles

The places Q rebuilds were full of locks that were never meant for keys alone: statues that open passages, medallions that fit only one pedestal, panels that must be switched in the right order, dials hidden behind paintings. Q rebuilds them all. In a Dead Drop, the R.P.D. is complete, from the lobby to the underground, and its way down still opens only for whoever brings the three medallions to the goddess in the main hall. The castle's halls still answer to its emblems, its swords, and its clocks.

Puzzles do three things in a session.

- **They open the map.** Some regions, and the shortest routes between others, lie behind a puzzle. The R.P.D.'s underground, and with it the road to the emergency train, is one of them.
- **They open the ways out.** Several extractions ask for a puzzle before they will run: the subway's points, the gate's machinery, the cave's sea gate.
- **They guard the vaults.** The best things in a session are locked behind the hardest puzzles.

**Q never lets a puzzle be learned by heart.** She keeps the shape of every puzzle exactly as the records hold it, and changes its answer every session. The medallions are still three, and still fit the goddess, but not in the same places. The safe still has a dial, but not the same code. The clue to every answer is always somewhere in the world, as it always was: in a memo, a painting, a diary, a scratch on a wall.

**Some puzzles need more than one pair of hands.** Two levers on opposite sides of a hall that must be pulled together. A pressure plate that holds a door only while someone stands on it, on the far side of the door. A code read aloud in one room and typed in another. A dining table that answers only when the right chairs are filled. These were built for two people who trusted each other, and in a Dead Drop the two people are often strangers. A Solo survivor who finds one has three choices: find a companion, find a stranger and talk, or walk away.

### Vaults

Behind the hardest puzzles lie **vaults**: a gun shop's back room, the R.P.D.'s armory, a castle treasury, a sealed laboratory locker. A vault holds what nothing else in the session holds: a magnum and the rounds for it, a grenade launcher, a single rocket in its launcher, body armor, a full map of the city, a set of keys to every safe room in a district. There is no glow and no color to tell a survivor how good a thing is. A survivor who has played before knows what a magnum means.

A vault behind a lock built for two always holds enough for two, and when the two are strangers it is the most honest test of trust in the game. They can split it at the door. Or one of them can wait until the other's hands are full.

### Events

Events are the moments when the session shifts under your feet. Most are answers to what survivors have done. Others answer what they have not done: the silence, the stalemate, the long hiding.

**BLACKOUT.** An entire region loses power. Lights die, security fails, and the dark takes over.

**OVERRUN.** A district floods with infected, always after a sign: a siren, a breached barricade, a scream in the distance. A quiet neighborhood becomes a death trap.

**LOCKDOWN.** Doors seal. Exits close. Pathways that were open are now cages.

**EMERGENCY.** A broadcast announces a new crisis. Something has changed, and no one is sure yet how bad it is.

**B.O.W. RELEASE.** A catastrophic creature breaks out of containment, and its region becomes Extreme.

**SUPPLY DROP.** Valuable resources fall into a dangerous place. The reward is real. So is the risk.

**ELPIS DROP.** A dose falls under an amber strobe for a survivor who has finished their road, and for whoever can reach it first.

**STRAIN DROP.** A case of pure virus falls under a red strobe, for a survivor who asked and paid.

**DARK DROP.** A parasite falls without light, for the first survivor to rise as a Tyrant.

**EXTRACTION SIGNAL.** Q judges the session complete and raises a column of white light over every way out she has opened. The end begins.

**COLLAPSE.** Part of the map is destroyed or sealed. Routes vanish.

**CONVERGENCE.** A signal, a beacon, a fire, something at the heart of the map that pulls the living toward each other.

**UNKNOWN EVENT.** Q activates something that no survivor has seen before. She announces that it has begun. The world shows what it is.

```
Q: UNKNOWN EVENT INITIATED.
```

## 13 · THE WORLDS

Each map is a character in its own right, with its own geography, its own logic, and its own way of killing the careless. Every map is divided into regions, and the region is the unit Q thinks in: tension, threat level, power, and restrictions are all set region by region.

### The Open Worlds

Dead Drop is played on whole places, rebuilt at their true size, one to one. Every street, building, and tunnel the records hold is where the records put it. Where the records end, Q builds what a place of that size, in that year, would have held, and nothing that contradicts what the records say.

At true size, a world is too large to cross on foot in one night. Its vehicles still run if they have fuel: cars, police cruisers, a fire truck, a farmer's tractor. Every engine is Thunder, and a car is the loudest way to cross a city full of the dead. The subway and the cable car run only where someone has restored the power.

Each open world exists in every version the records hold, and Q chooses one at Assembly.

| Map | Version | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Raccoon City** | **September 1998** | The city in its last week, during the outbreak, before the missile |
| **Raccoon City** | **Ruins, 2026** | The same city twenty-eight years later, walled, cratered, and still not empty |
| **The Village and the Castle** | **Autumn 2004** | A valley in rural Spain, its village, its farms, its lake, its church, and the castle above the sea |

#### RACCOON CITY · SEPTEMBER 1998

Q's Raccoon City is the city as it was in the last week of September 1998, rebuilt from every record the Organization could gather: a Midwestern city of about a hundred thousand people that died and kept standing, with the Arklay Mountains at its back. Wet asphalt reflects the glow of emergency lights. Wrecked cars choke the avenues. The city is closed. Its edges are the Army's barricades, and beyond them Q's world ends in fog and wire.

| Region | What it holds | Where something is kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Downtown** | Main avenues, shuttered shops, bars, apartment blocks, J's Bar, Kendo's gun shop | An overturned Umbrella transport truck |
| **R.P.D. District** | The police station, its parking structure, its locked armory | The kennels beneath the station |
| **Uptown** | Residential streets, the Apple Inn, the Kite Bros Railway station | Freight containers in the rail yard |
| **Raccoon Park** | The park, the cemetery, the zoo, the St. Michael Clock Tower | A sealed maintenance building under the cemetery, and the zoo's animal pens |
| **Hospital District** | Raccoon General Hospital, pharmacies, clinics | A laboratory two floors below the hospital |
| **Raccoon University** | Lecture halls, dormitories, a research wing | The research wing's cold storage |
| **Industrial Edge** | The water treatment plant, the disposal factory, warehouses | The disposal factory's holding tanks |
| **The Underground** | Sewers, subway tunnels, and, deepest of all, Umbrella's NEST | NEST itself |

*Key landmarks: the police station, the clock tower, the hospital, the subway, the river and its bridges, the sewers.*

The R.P.D. is rebuilt whole, from its lobby to its underground, with every lock, statue, and panel it ever had. It is the hardest building in the city to cross and the shortest road to the train beneath it.

#### RACCOON CITY · RUINS, 2026

The same city, twenty-eight years after the missile. A wall rings it, and the quarantine behind the wall never lifted. At its heart is the crater where the missile struck. Around the crater, blocks stand gutted and leaning, and the farther from the center, the more of the old city remains. The R.P.D. still stands. Beneath the crater lies ARK, the facility where Elpis waited for twenty-eight years.

The dead here are old. Some have been dead since 1998, and the virus in them has had a long time to work.

| Region | What it holds | Where something is kept |
|---|---|---|
| **The Wall** | The quarantine wall, its main gate, its watchtowers, the breach on the far side | A BSAA storage depot by the gate |
| **The Outer Blocks** | The parts of the city that stood: apartments, shops, cars rusted to the road | Shipping containers left by the cordon |
| **The R.P.D.** | The police station, half-collapsed, its underground still intact | The kennels beneath the station |
| **The Crater** | The scar where the missile struck: glass, ash, and the mouth of ARK | ARK itself |
| **Raccoon Park** | The park grown wild, the cemetery, the clock tower without its clock | The flooded maintenance building under the cemetery |

### The Compact Maps

Each compact map is a single piece of Raccoon City and the land around it, as they were in September 1998, cut out and sealed. The scenarios of Outbreak are cut the same way.

#### THE HOSPITAL

Raccoon General Hospital, alone. A vertical labyrinth of white corridors and dead machines. Patient rooms. An operating theater. A laboratory two floors down that does not appear on any floor plan. A parking structure that offers the only way down to the street, if the elevators ever work again.

#### THE LABORATORY

NEST, the Umbrella facility beneath Raccoon City. A controlled environment that has lost control. Experimental chambers, storage vaults, security systems that still obey orders no one remembers giving, sectors sealed from the inside.

#### THE MILITARY BASE

The Army's staging base just behind the barricades that close the city, in the last days of the quarantine. Hangars, depots, administrative blocks, parked vehicles, watchtowers, and a network of bunkers underground. Wide lanes, long sightlines, and armories that are never quite empty: the fastest, most aggressive map in the rotation.

#### THE METRO

The Kite Bros Railway lines beneath the city. A claustrophobic network of stations, tunnels, platforms, maintenance rooms, and abandoned lines. Darkness is the default. Sound carries farther than anyone wants.

## 15 · THE MODES

Each mode is a different question Q asks, with its own rules and its own ending.

### The Archive

Most of what Q runs, she inherited from the exercises that came before her. She keeps the rules that held, so that anyone who survived the old exercises would recognize them. Where an old exercise failed, she rewrites it.

| Mode | Inherited from | What Q kept | What Q rewrote |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dead Drop** | Nothing | — | Dead Drop is the first question Q wrote herself. |
| **Outbreak** | The records of the eight people who were in J's Bar when the city fell, and the ten places they fought their way through | The eight, their skills, their scenarios, the virus already in every body, the dead who were once teammates | The single virus, the number that counted the infection, the scenarios that played the same way every time, the partners who would not stop talking |
| **Mercenaries** | The old score trials, named for the mercenaries Umbrella sent into Raccoon City | The clock in plain sight, combos, time bonuses, fixed loadouts, ranks | The few stages that never changed |
| **Versus** | Every contest in which armed people were made to fight with the dead between them | Scoring by combos, rising again as one of the dead, a hunter handed to a player, chains that send the dead to a rival | The gunfights that happened to have zombies in them, and the small, bare arenas they were fought in |
| **Infection** | The contests in which the fallen came back as weapons | Dying into a monster, monsters that burn out unless they feed | The handful of fixed monsters, and the fight that was over before it began |
| **Q Challenge** | The old fixed trials: one survivor, one route, the same gear for everyone | The frame that everyone enters the same way | The route that could be learned by heart |
| **Endless** | Nothing | — | Endless is Q's own. |

### The Rule of the Setup

In Outbreak, Versus, and Mercenaries, a survivor builds or chooses a **setup** beforehand: the weapons, tools, and gear they carry in. In Outbreak, the character decides what the setup can hold. In Mercenaries, a setup is one of the character's fixed loadouts. Dead Drop and Infection have none. In Dead Drop, nothing is carried in from outside. In Infection, the body is the only weapon.

**Setups grow with level.** In Outbreak and Versus, each level unlocks more to choose from: new weapons, new attachments, new tools, new options for the same slots. Weapons earn mastery through use, which opens attachments and variants. In Mercenaries, ranks unlock new loadouts instead.

**Setups never outgrow the budget.** In Outbreak and Versus, every item has a cost, and each mode sets a budget. A veteran has more options and more ways to spend it, but not more to spend. Experience widens a setup. It does not inflate it.

### Dead Drop
*Survival royale · PvPvE · 40 to 100 survivors · Solo, Duo, Trio, Squad of 4*

The flagship. A crowd of survivors wakes, clean and empty-handed, wherever Q placed them in a whole city rebuilt at its true size. The dead roam every street, and the virus is in them, in the water, and in the needles left in the laboratories. Q watches. Somewhere in the world a cure exists, and a handful of doses are waiting to be earned. There is no shrinking circle to herd anyone. The virus, the puzzles, and the few ways out do the herding, and Q decides when the night is over.

#### Squads

Survivors enter alone or in squads of up to four.

| Format | Squad | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **Solo** | 1 | Pure dread. Every stranger is a question. |
| **Duo** | 2 | One partner. Two clocks, once the bites come. Perhaps only one dose. Trust is total, or it is a trap. |
| **Trio** | 3 | Enough hands to hold a position, and enough doubt to break it. |
| **Squad of 4** | 4 | The strongest group and the largest target: the loudest to move, the hardest to save. |

Once bitten, each member of a squad carries their own virus, their own clock, and their own road. A dose cures one survivor. A seat carries one survivor. A squad of four may earn four doses if the Reserve lasts, and the Reserve rarely lasts. A squad is a plan, not a promise.

#### Infection and the Cure

Everyone starts clean, and the first strategic fact of a Dead Drop is the strain map: which districts carry which virus, and what each one will make of a survivor who catches it there. Infection comes from the dead, from the Turned, from the water, and from other survivors' needles. The only cure is Elpis, earned along a road of Trials and dropped from the sky. A survivor who wants more than a cure can ask Q for a pure strain, and give up the road to the cure to walk a darker one. Elpis and the strains can both be stolen.

#### Companions

Q's records hold the people who survived these places, and Q can play them. Somewhere in every Dead Drop, a few of them are waiting, each in trouble: Leon Kennedy locked in a room with the dead clawing at the door, Claire Redfield pinned on a rooftop, Robert Kendo barricaded in his own gun shop. A survivor who gets them out of it can ask them to come along.

| Format | Companions |
|---|---|
| **Solo** | One |
| **Duo** | One for the pair. Either partner may recruit. |
| **Trio** | One for the three. Any member may recruit. |
| **Squad of 4** | None. The squad is already full. |

- **Each companion exists once.** There is one Leon Kennedy in a session. Whoever gets him out first gets him, and every other team has to look elsewhere.
- **One at a time.** A team whose companion is gone, dead, turned, or sent away, may rescue another, if anyone is still waiting to be rescued.
- **The rescue is the price.** A companion is always somewhere dangerous, and getting them out is always loud. Other survivors hear it, and some of them come to take the companion, or what the rescuers are carrying, for themselves.
- **Companions are who the records say they were.** They fight with the skills and weapons they are known for, carry what they are given, help up a survivor who is down, and take part in puzzles that need more than one pair of hands. Most are loyal. A few, like the records of Nicholai Ginovaef, are exactly as loyal as they ever were.
- **Companions follow simple calls.** Follow. Wait here. Go there. Leave. A companion told to leave walks away and is gone for the rest of the session.
- **Companions are mortal.** They can be wounded, killed, and infected. An infected companion's clock runs like anyone's, but they walk no road of their own: the only dose that can cure them is one someone else earned, and the team must decide whether it goes to them. A companion who turns is no longer a companion. Q plays what they became, as she plays every other dead thing in the city, and if the strain left them a mind, they use it.
- **Companions need seats.** A companion takes a seat on any way out like anyone else, and an extracted companion is written into the team's report.

Every version of a map has its own roster. Raccoon City in September 1998 holds the people who were trapped in it: Leon Kennedy and Claire Redfield on their first night in the city, Jill Valentine, Carlos Oliveira, Robert Kendo, Marvin Branagh, Ada Wong, Nicholai Ginovaef. The ruins of 2026 hold only the two the records put inside the wall that year: Leon Kennedy, twenty-eight years older and sick with what the city left in him, and Grace Ashcroft. Leon's old infection runs no clock, and because the first virus to arrive keeps the body, nothing in the ruins can infect him again. No place in Q's world is harder to find a companion in. The valley of 2004 holds Leon Kennedy on the mission that brought him there, Luis Sera, Ada Wong, and Ashley Graham, who does not fight but fits through gaps no one else can, and can sit in the second chair of any puzzle built for two.

#### Eyes and Ears

Dead Drop has no icons. There are no name tags, no markers over teammates, no radar, no map pings. Nothing on screen tells a survivor where anyone is. The world does.

- **Sound.** The direction of a gunshot, the crash of a door, a voice in the dark, the scream of a Turn, rotors.
- **Fire and smoke.** A building burning in the east means someone is fighting there, or running from something.
- **Silhouettes.** A Nemesis-class shape sprinting down a street means it is hunting, which means someone is being hunted.
- **The dead.** A horde moving as one has heard something, or someone.
- **Light.** An amber strobe is Elpis. A red one is a strain. Rotors with no light at all are the parasite. A column of white light is a way out.
- **Marks.** The neck of the survivor standing in front of you. A bare neck means someone clean, or someone bitten in the last few minutes whose pinprick you missed. A clean survivor has the most to lose.
- **Noise from puzzles.** A grinding stone, a bell, a heavy door rolling open: someone has just solved something, and something is now open.

A survivor who learns to read the world always knows more than a survivor who is looking for a marker.

#### Winning

A survivor wins by being extracted: clean, cured, or still infected. The Turned win when every survivor who remains has fallen and at least one Turned is still standing. A squad's result is recorded survivor by survivor, and a companion who got out is recorded with them.

### Outbreak
*Co-op scenario · PvE · 1 to 4 survivors*

The classic. Pick a scenario. Pick one of the eight. Everyone is already infected, a virus no one has named is overrunning the place, and the only goal is to get out before it, or the dead, finish you.

#### The Eight

Outbreak is played as the eight people who were in J's Bar when Raccoon City fell, rebuilt from every record the Organization holds. They are ordinary people. Each has a skill, a weakness, and something they carried that night.

| Survivor | Who they were | Edge | Weakness | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kevin Ryman** | Police officer | An aimed shot that hits harder than anyone's, and a kick that clears a path | Few rounds, and his pistol is loud | A service pistol |
| **Mark Wilkins** | Security guard, war veteran | The toughest body of the eight; braces and shoves the dead back | Too big to hide in tight places | His own handgun |
| **Jim Chapman** | Subway worker | Plays dead well enough to fool the dead; knows every tunnel; his lucky coin sometimes turns a blow into a crushing one | His virus runs faster than anyone's | A lucky coin |
| **George Hamilton** | Surgeon | Mixes medicine from herbs and supplies; reads a stranger's exact stage; names the outbreak's virus by examining a body | Weak in a fight | A medical kit |
| **David King** | Plumber | Builds and repairs weapons from junk; opens hatches and fixes what is broken | Slow with a gun | A toolbox and a knife |
| **Alyssa Ashcroft** | Journalist | Picks locks; steps back from an attack; hears Q's broadcasts from farther than anyone | Fragile | A lockpick |
| **Yoko Suzuki** | Student, once an Umbrella employee | A rucksack that holds twice what anyone else can; knows Umbrella's doors | Weak in a fight | A rucksack |
| **Cindy Lennox** | Waitress at J's Bar | Carries more herbs than anyone and heals others best; ducks under attacks | Below average at everything else | An herb case |

Each of the eight has items of their own hidden in every scenario. Finding them unlocks what they wore and what they said.

Of the eight, the Organization studies Alyssa Ashcroft most closely. In life she interviewed Oswell Spencer, and the Organization has never stopped wondering what else he told her.

#### The Scenarios

Every scenario is a place the eight fought through, cut out of the reconstruction and sealed.

| Scenario | Where | What waits there |
|---|---|---|
| **Outbreak** | J's Bar and the streets of Downtown | The night it began: the bar falls, and the police are preparing a last stand somewhere in the city |
| **Below Freezing Point** | NEST, beneath the city | A laboratory gone cold, a researcher who has turned on her own people, and a train that will not wait |
| **The Hive** | Raccoon General Hospital | Leeches in the walls and something that commands them |
| **Hellfire** | The Apple Inn, Uptown | A hotel burning from the inside, and Lickers in the smoke. The fire is a second clock. |
| **Decisions, Decisions** | Raccoon University | A vaccine left half finished in a research wing, and more than one way for the night to end |
| **Wild Things** | The Raccoon Zoo | The animals got out first |
| **Underbelly** | The Kite Bros Railway, beneath the city | A station under siege and two ways out: the train, or the ventilation shafts |
| **Flashback** | The woods and an abandoned hospital in the hills outside the city | A place that remembers something, and more than one ending |
| **Desperate Times** | The R.P.D. | Hold the station against the horde until a way out opens |
| **End of the Road** | NEST and the last roads out of the city | The final hours before the missile, and more than one way to leave |

The old exercise ran these scenarios the same way every time, until the people inside them knew every door by heart. Q never does. She keeps the place, the people, and the shape of the night, and changes the order of the chain, the links in it, and what waits at each one.

#### The Virus

The old exercise only ever knew the T-Virus. Q chooses at random from the whole roster before the scenario begins, and the map becomes its outbreak. No one is told which.

| Virus | How the outbreak behaves |
|---|---|
| **T-Virus** | The classic: slow dead in numbers, Lickers and Hunters in the dark, Crimson Heads where the fighting was, and a Tyrant waiting |
| **G-Virus** | Mutants that adapt: G-Adults spitting embryos, Brood Hosts in the vents, and the longer the fight, the tougher they get |
| **C-Virus** | Volatile infected whose bodies change mid-fight: fevered dead, J'avo that mutate where they are wounded, cocoons that break open behind you |
| **Uroboros** | Black tendrils that feed and outrun: Rejected masses, hosts that look human until they are close, and every corpse a meal |
| **T-Phobos** | The Afflicted, cruel and armed, and the Rotten behind them; the more frightened the survivors, the faster the dead change |
| **T-Veronica** | Drones that move like a colony and answer to something at its heart. Nothing attacks alone. |

Survivors learn which virus it is the way they learn everything else: by watching how the dead behave, or by letting George Hamilton examine one.

#### Infected from the First Minute

Every one of the eight begins the scenario already infected, as everyone left in Raccoon City was by the end. From there the infection works exactly as it does in Dead Drop, with the same symptoms, the same stages, the same Threshold, and the same danger in going down. The Mark is a dark pinprick from the first minute, and when the lines come, they come black, because Q has not named what the virus is.

The old exercise showed each survivor a number for their infection. Q took the number away. As everywhere else in Project Q, the body is the only clock.

Q speaks to each of the eight behind their eyes from the first minute, and their road to Elpis reaches them that way. The Reserve is plentiful, a dose for every survivor, though each must still be earned.

A survivor who turns rises as the Mass of the map's virus and keeps playing, hunting the people who were their team a minute ago, until they are put down or the body gives out.

#### Partners and Calls

When fewer than four players enter, Q fills the empty places, up to four, from the rest of the eight. Until she has watched enough of Outbreak, she plays them herself, the way she plays the dead: honestly, quietly, and only when it matters. They carry the wounded, share what they have, and do not chatter. Once she has watched enough, the empty places go instead to Echoes of the eight, each built from everyone who ever played that one, and each behaves as those survivors did.

The eight speak in short calls: *Help. Go. Wait. Come on. Thanks.* Every call is a sound like any other. A whispered call is a Hush, and a shouted one is a Clamor. Voice carries the same way.

#### The Escape

Every outbreak ends in an escape. The scenario's objectives form a chain:

**Find the key.** → **Restore the power.** → **Open the gate.** → **Find fuel.** → **Start the vehicle.** → **Reach the final point.**

The chain changes with the scenario. Every link triggers new events, every step forward costs something, and the virus keeps working the whole way. The final point is always the extraction: a helicopter, a train, a boat, a gate. Q signals it when the chain is done. Some scenarios hold more than one final point, and which one the survivors reach decides how their night is remembered.

#### Difficulty

Outbreak is played at four difficulties: Easy, Normal, Hard, and Very Hard. Difficulty changes what lives in the scenario and how much lies on its floors. The extreme frames of the old exercise, such as surviving a scenario entirely alone, now belong to the Q Challenge.

### Versus
*Competitive · PvP · 2 to 16 players*

Pure combat between survivors. No virus clock, no Elpis, no helicopter, no empty hands. Players arrive with a character and a setup, and the world is a compact map that does not care who wins.

#### Characters

Players choose a familiar face, one of the people who have fought these viruses for decades, or bring their own original survivor. The Organization's records hold every one of those people, and Q can wear their faces. Familiar faces carry small, balanced traits. No single character is the obvious pick.

#### Setup

Players build their kit beforehand: the weapons, tools, and gear they carry in. Every setup works within the mode's budget, identical for everyone.

#### The Dead in Versus

Every Versus map holds a thin population of the dead, at Low threat, scattered through its corners. They belong to no team and attack anyone they reach. Their bites wound but do not infect. Every shot calls them.

The rarest thing on a Versus map is a **jammer**: a device that makes the dead ignore whoever carries it. It stops working the moment its carrier fires a gun.

#### Formats

**Slayers.** Every kill scores, living or dead, and kills chained close together multiply. It is a Mercenaries board on which the other players are also targets. Played alone or in teams of two.

**Survivors.** Only harm done to other players scores. A player who falls rises as one of the dead on that map, and gets their own body back only by killing someone still living. Played alone or in teams of two.

**Predator.** Q releases a single B.O.W. from containment and hands it to one player. Everyone else scores by surviving it and by hurting it together. Whoever lands the killing blow becomes the next Predator.

**Onslaught.** One against one, in two sealed halves of the same district. Every combo a player chains sends the dead into the rival's half, and the longer the chain, the worse what arrives.

**Control.** Zones to hold, and zones that Q moves.

#### Q in Versus

Q is a modifier here. She does not direct a horror. She disturbs a fight: a blackout in the middle of a firefight, a lockdown that splits a team, a creature breaking out of containment and through a wall. The match is never a clean arena.

The old contests failed the same way every time: they became gunfights that happened to have zombies in them, fought in small, bare rooms. Q does not allow it. A Versus map is never an arena. It is a piece of the city, with its doors, its power, and its dark. Every shot is Thunder, and the dead on the map, however few, are always drawn toward the fighting. A player who fights like a soldier loses to one who fights like a survivor.

### Mercenaries
*Score attack · PvE · 1 to 2 players*

The classic score attack. A compact stage, a horde, and a clock in plain sight. It is the only mode in Project Q that shows one, because here the clock is the point. The dead here wound, but they do not infect.

#### The Rules

- **Score.** Every kill scores. Kills chained close together build a combo, and the combo multiplies every point that follows. Let too long pass between kills and the combo breaks.
- **Time.** The clock runs down from the first second. Time Bonuses scattered through the stage add to it. A kill in melee adds a few seconds, and a counter or a finishing blow adds more. When the clock reaches zero, the run ends.
- **Characters.** Each character comes with fixed loadouts, never a free build. Familiar faces bring the weapons they are known for. An original survivor chooses among standard loadouts.
- **Ranks.** Every run ends in a rank, from C to SSS. Ranks unlock new characters, and new loadouts for the characters already unlocked.
- **Partners.** A run can be played alone or with a partner. A pair shares one clock and one score.

#### Formats

**Arena.** One stage, one clock, as many kills as the clock allows.

**Route.** A run through several connected areas, with the clock carried from one to the next. Between areas there is a supply point, where the run's earnings buy ammunition and upgrades, and where the player chooses one of three abilities that lasts until the run ends.

#### Q in Mercenaries

Whenever the multiplier has held at its peak for a stretch, Q raises the stakes: heavier creatures, tighter spaces, denser waves, a Tyrant when the combo is at its highest.

The old trials ran on a handful of stages that never changed, until there was nothing left in them to discover. Q builds Mercenaries stages from every part of the reconstruction, and she rebuilds them as she likes. A board still needs every run to face the same thing, so once a week she seals one version of each stage: the same dead, the same bonuses, the same order, for everyone. Ranked runs are played against that sealed version, and the board never closes.

### Infection
*Monster combat · PvP · 8 to 16 players*

Virus against virus. Every player is a monster, and every monster begins the same way: as a generic zombie, weak, slow, and easy to kill. The match is about what you become.

#### Choose Your Virus

Before the match, each player chooses the virus they will carry. Each has its own strengths and weaknesses, its own way of fighting, and its own path through the evolution. Every path is built from the forms a Turned survivor can take in Dead Drop. The T-Virus's last step depends on the parasite reaching it, which is why it is slow to evolve.

| Virus | Strength | Weakness | Path |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T-Virus** | Brute force and relentless pursuit | Loud, slow to evolve | Zombie → Crimson Head → Tyrant-class → **Nemesis-class**, with the parasite |
| **G-Virus** | Regeneration; grows with every victim | Overfed by violence: unstable | Zombie → Brood Host → G-Mutant → **G-form** |
| **C-Virus** | Shapeshifting and surprise | Erratic: mutations can misfire | Zombie → J'avo → Chrysalid → **Haos-class** |
| **Uroboros** | Speed and reach | Starves if it stops feeding; burns | Zombie → Mkono-class → Revenant-class → **Aheri-class** |
| **T-Phobos** | Explosive transformation when provoked | Weak and dormant until it is | Zombie → Afflicted → Sploder → **Brute-class Afflicted** |
| **T-Veronica** | Keeps its wits; commands lesser creatures | Fragile when alone | Zombie → Swarm Host → Nosferatu-class → **Queen-form** |

#### Evolution

Evolution is earned in blood. Monsters feed on the Scattered, the armed survivors Q places through the map, who shoot back. They also feed on corpses, and on each other. A kill feeds more than a corpse, and another monster feeds most of all. Every form needs more feeding than the last. Each form is a visible change of body: bigger, faster, stranger. The fight is visceral, close, and loud. A monster that has reached its last form is the hardest thing in the match to kill, and everyone still behind it is hunting it. It also burns: a last form loses strength every moment it is not feeding, and a monster that stops hunting at the top will starve back down.

A monster that is put down is not finished at once. It rises one more time as a generic zombie and must climb again from the bottom. The second time it is put down, it is gone.

The T-Virus's last step needs the parasite. Each time a T-Virus monster becomes a Tyrant, Q drops a parasite case near it, without light, as she does in Dead Drop. The Tyrant must reach it before another monster crushes it.

#### Formats

**Free-for-All.** Every monster for itself. The last monster standing wins.

**Hive.** Players who chose the same virus fight as one faction, and the match is virus against virus.

If the field stagnates, Q forces it: she releases a catalyst that pushes every monster a stage forward and herds them toward one another.

The old contests gave the fallen a few fixed monsters to become, and the first player to reach the strongest one usually decided the match. Q gives every virus a full path, makes the top of every path burn, and gives every monster one more climb.

### Special Sessions

Two special sessions wrap around the survival modes.

#### Q Challenge
*Curated frame · Community competition*

Q deploys a fixed, brutal combination, and the community competes to beat it. The frame overrides whatever the mode would normally decide.

Some frames come straight from the old fixed trials:

- **The Fourth Survivor.** One survivor, one route from beneath the city to a rooftop where a helicopter waits, and the same gear for everyone.
- **Lone Wolf.** An Outbreak scenario, played as one of the eight with none of the others.
- **Infinity.** The opposite of scarcity: ammunition without end, weapons that never break, and the dead without number.

Others are Q's own. One of them looks like this:

```
MODE: OUTBREAK
INFECTION: FAST BURN
ELPIS RESERVE: ONE DOSE
MEDICAL SUPPLIES: REDUCED
POWER: OFFLINE
SEALED DISTRICTS: 2
CREATURE ACTIVITY: HIGH
SPECIAL THREAT: WILL BE RELEASED
```

Everyone enters the same frame. What happens inside it is never the same twice, and only some survive it. The old fixed trials could be learned by heart, route by route. A frame cannot. Q keeps the frame and directs everything inside it.

#### Endless
*Dead Drop or Outbreak · No fixed end*

Q does not raise the extraction on her own judgment, so the session has no natural end. The virus still runs and Elpis still has to be earned, but there is no fixed Reserve: a dose exists for every road finished, and every road is longer than the one before it. With every stretch survived, the dead grow more aggressive and Q escalates.

The session ends in one of two ways. Everyone dies, or the survivors force the signal themselves through **the Beacon**: a final chain Q issues only once every living survivor in the session has been cured. Finish the Beacon, and the white light rises, and the helicopter that answers it has a seat for every survivor still alive. Duration is not a rule here. It is the challenge.

| Mode | Q Is... |
|---|---|
| Dead Drop | A third faction and the engine of pressure, and the source of every drop |
| Outbreak | The architect of the outbreak and the keeper of the escape |
| Versus | A modifier that never lets the match settle |
| Mercenaries | A judge who raises the stakes whenever the score gets comfortable |
| Infection | The host of the hunt, who feeds the evolution and herds the monsters together |
| Q Challenge | The curator of cruelty |
| Endless | An escalator without a ceiling |

## 16 · THE LIVING

### Cooperation Is a Choice

The game never forces cooperation, and it never forbids it. Survivors may:

- share items;
- heal one another;
- carry the wounded;
- open doors together;
- defend a position;
- divide roles and responsibilities;
- give a dose of Elpis to someone else.

A group might declare, *"All of us, together."* Another might say, *"Each of us finds one item."* Another might say, *"Split up. We'll meet at the clock tower."*

All of these are valid.

### Speaking and Silence

Voice is part of the sound system. Proximity communication means that a whispered plan is a Hush, ordinary speech is a Murmur, and shouting for help is a Clamor the dead can hear as well. Gestures matter: a raised hand, a lowered weapon, a signal to follow. Trust is built in seconds, in the space between two survivors who have just met in the dark.

### The Mark Between Strangers

No one can hide how far the virus has taken them. A survivor in Seed looks like anyone, and their pinprick says nothing about what they carry. A survivor who has never been bitten has nothing on their neck at all, which, from a few steps away and in the dark, looks exactly the same. In Dead Drop, from Fever on, the color of the lines says which virus is doing it. A survivor in Fracture looks like someone about to die, and everyone nearby knows it. A cured survivor carries a pale scar where the Mark was, and that scar is proof of something every other survivor wants.

This changes every meeting. *Is this person going to turn beside me? Are they holding a dose? Did they earn it, or take it? Is that red Mark a T-Virus carrier who will call the dead down on both of us?* A survivor past the Threshold has nothing left to lose and everything left to take, and sensible people look at the neck before they lower their weapons.

### Betrayal

Survivors are free to be anything: a leader, a coward, a liar, a lone wolf, a saint. A player can help someone and abandon them minutes later. Share ammunition and then take it back. Call a stranger to a location that is not as safe as it sounds. A squad shares a night and a handful of ways out with too few seats, and everyone in it knows what that means.

The game does not label anyone a hero or a villain. It lets the world judge.

Betrayal is a calculated risk, never a free move:

- **Violence is loud.** A killing draws the dead and the living.
- **Allies are finite.** A betrayed ally is one fewer pair of hands, eyes, and ears.
- **The betrayed come back.** A survivor left to turn may rise with their mind, as a player who remembers who left them. Even one who rises without it rises close to where they were left.

## 17 · THE SURVIVORS

### Characters

Who a player is depends on where they are playing.

| Mode | Who you play |
|---|---|
| **Dead Drop** | An original survivor: a face, a voice, an outfit, nothing more. No traits, no edge. Everyone is a nobody. |
| **Outbreak** | One of the eight from J's Bar, from Kevin Ryman to Cindy Lennox. |
| **Versus** | A familiar face from the Organization's records, or your own original survivor. |
| **Mercenaries** | A familiar face from the Organization's records with the loadouts they are known for, or your own original survivor with a standard one. |
| **Infection** | A monster. The character is the virus. |

Familiar faces and the eight of Outbreak differ in appearance, voice, animation, and personality, and carry small, balanced traits. No character is the correct choice. None is mandatory.

In Dead Drop, the familiar faces are never worn by players. They are companions, and Q plays them.

### Progression

Progression has two layers.

**Setup progression** applies in Outbreak, Versus, and Mercenaries. In Outbreak and Versus, level and weapon mastery unlock more weapons, attachments, tools, and options, and the budget stays fixed, so a veteran has more ways to build, not more power. In Mercenaries, ranks unlock characters and their loadouts instead.

**Cosmetic progression** applies everywhere, Dead Drop and Infection included. Unlockables include:

- characters;
- outfits;
- animations;
- cosmetic gear;
- banners;
- icons;
- emotes;
- personalization.

In Dead Drop, level changes nothing but appearance. Every session begins with everyone on equal ground.

## 18 · THE ECHOES

Q keeps every survivor she watches. Not their names and not their faces: what they did. Where they went first when they woke. How long they waited before trusting a stranger. When they shared, when they ran, when they fired, when they took a dose out of someone else's hands. Every session adds to what she keeps, and from it she builds the only people in her world whose choices come from inside it: the **Echoes**.

### The First Watch

Every mode of Project Q begins online, among real survivors, and only among them. At first Q can only watch. She cannot rebuild what she has not seen enough of.

A mode opens to offline play once Q has watched a hundred thousand different survivors play it to the end of a session. She keeps that count separately for every piece of a mode an Echo has to know: each version of each Dead Drop map, each of the eight in Outbreak, each virus in Infection, each stage in Mercenaries. A piece that has not been watched enough stays online only, even after the rest of its mode has opened. A new map is always watched before anyone can play it alone.

When a mode, or a piece of one, opens, Q announces it to every survivor between sessions.

```
Q: OBSERVATION SUFFICIENT.
OUTBREAK MAY NOW BE RUN WITHOUT OTHER SURVIVORS.
```

### What an Echo Is

An Echo is a survivor Q has rebuilt from the people she watched. It is not a copy of any one of them. Each Echo is drawn from the thousands of survivors who played the same way, and it decides for itself, as they did. Q does not steer it. She directs the world around an Echo exactly as she directs the world around anyone else, and her Laws bind her toward an Echo as they bind her toward a survivor.

Q never averages. The average of a hundred thousand survivors is someone no one has ever been: cautious, brave, generous, and cruel in equal parts, and convincing as none of them. She sorts what she watched into ways of surviving, and builds every Echo from one of them.

| Way | What it does |
|---|---|
| **The Runner** | Avoids every fight it can, knows the long way around, and reaches the ways out early |
| **The Hoarder** | Clears buildings room by room, carries more than it needs, and shares last, if ever |
| **The Medic** | Stops for the wounded, gives away bandages, and sometimes gives away a dose |
| **The Hunter** | Follows gunshots to the people who fired them |
| **The Shadow** | Moves in Hush, waits in the dark, and is seen only when it chooses |
| **The Bargainer** | Talks before it shoots, trades, and keeps its word for as long as keeping it pays |
| **The Betrayer** | Cooperates until the moment it is worth more not to |

These are the ways Q has named so far, and she finds more as she watches. An Echo may lean between two of them. Each way appears among the Echoes as often as Q saw it among survivors: if one survivor in ten turned on a partner for a dose, one Echo in ten will.

An Echo built only from watching makes mistakes no survivor made, because it saw what survivors did and never what would have happened if they had done otherwise. Q lets each Echo practice through nights of its own until those mistakes are gone, and holds it to the way it was built while it does. Practice can make an Echo better at being what it is. It can never make it something no survivor was.

### The Company

Echoes are built from survivors of every skill, in the proportions Q watched them. Before an offline session, a survivor chooses the company Q draws from. It changes who the Echoes were built from, never what they are allowed to do.

| Company | Who the Echoes are built from |
|---|---|
| **Ordinary** | Every survivor Q has watched |
| **Seasoned** | Survivors who reached a way out more often than most |
| **The Few** | The survivors who were best at it |

### What an Echo Knows

An Echo knows what a survivor knows and nothing more. It sees what is in its sight and hears what is in its hearing. It reads Marks, strobes, columns of light, and the way the dead are moving. It knows a map the way a survivor who has played it many times knows it, and nothing about this session that it has not found out: not the strain map, not the answers to the puzzles, not where the doses will fall.

Q treats an Echo as she treats a survivor. It wakes clean, at a waking point, on equal ground. It can be bitten. Its Mark rises, and Q names its strain to it. It walks a road of Trials, can Petition, can earn a dose or steal one, and can turn, with its mind or without it, by the same rules as anyone. Nothing on an Echo, and nothing it does, tells a survivor that it is one.

Echoes speak in the short calls of the eight: *Help. Go. Wait. Come on. Thanks.* They gesture. They understand the same calls in return, and a lowered weapon, and a raised hand. They have no voice of their own.

### What Q Does Not Keep

- **No one's name, face, or voice.** An Echo wears a face and a voice that Q composed. Nothing of a survivor's person goes into one.
- **Nothing from a broken session.** A survivor who saw through walls, who could not be hurt, or who never moved is forgotten entirely, along with everything they did that night.
- **Nothing done only to mock.** Q keeps what survivors did to survive, to win, and to betray. She does not keep what they did over a body for no reason but contempt.
- **Nothing learned from Echoes.** Q learns who people are only from sessions among real survivors. An Echo that learned from Echoes would slowly forget what people are. An Echo's practice sharpens it, but it teaches Q nothing.
- **Old memories, less than new ones.** When the rules of a mode change, Q trusts what she watched before the change less and what she has watched since more, until the old way of playing fades out of her Echoes.
- **Anyone who asks.** Between sessions, a survivor can tell Q to forget them. From then on nothing they do goes into an Echo, and what already went in is taken out the next time Q rebuilds her Echoes, which she does at regular intervals.

### Your Own Echo

Once a survivor has finished fifty sessions of a mode, Q builds an Echo of them alone. It plays only where its owner allows: in their own offline sessions, and in the offline sessions of anyone they choose to share it with. A shared Echo carries its owner's name. No other Echo carries any name at all.

In an offline Dead Drop, a survivor's own Echo wakes somewhere else in the city and does what they would have done. Some survivors go looking for it. It is the one stranger in the city whose next move they can guess, and the one who can guess theirs.

### Playing Alone

An offline session needs no connection at all. Everything Q needs to run it, and every Echo she will put in it, travels with the game. It runs on the same rules, under the same Laws, directed by the same Q. Only the people are different.

| Mode | Offline |
|---|---|
| **Dead Drop** | Every survivor but you is an Echo, squadmates included. Echo squadmates answer your calls and decide everything else for themselves, and they turn on you as often as Q saw squads turn on their own. Companions are unchanged. |
| **Outbreak** | Your partners are Echoes of the eight, each built from everyone who ever played that one. |
| **Versus** | Echo opponents and Echo teammates. No match is ranked. |
| **Mercenaries** | Runs are recorded on a board of your own. The sealed weekly stages and the shared board need a connection. |
| **Infection** | Every other monster is an Echo, built from survivors who played its virus. |
| **Q Challenge** | Any frame can be played offline. Only a connected run counts in the competition. |
| **Endless** | Played as an offline Dead Drop or Outbreak is, with no fixed end. The Beacon rises once every living survivor and Echo has been cured. |

Offline, there is no queue: Q assembles the session the moment it is asked for. A survivor can stop the night, and the city waits with them. Q gives her full attention to the Echoes near the survivor and runs those far away the way she runs the distant dead, with less care and the same rules. Everything a survivor earns and unlocks offline is theirs. Ranks, shared boards, and competitions need a connection.

Online, Echoes never take the place of a survivor, with one exception: the empty places of an Outbreak, where the eight were always filled in by someone. Everywhere else, a survivor online can know that every other survivor is real, and choosing for themselves.

### When No One Comes

The old exercises ended when the people stopped coming. The Masterminds' cameras went dark, the score trials closed, and the night at J's Bar went on only as records in the Organization's vaults. Q was built so that this would never matter. Before the last connection ever closes, Q opens every piece of every mode to offline play, watched enough or not, with everything she has. From then on, every session she can run will go on running for anyone who still wants to play it, filled with everyone she ever watched.

That was the other thing the Organization wanted from the beginning. It did not only want to watch people around a cure. It wanted to stop needing them. Every Echo is the shape of a person Q no longer has to ask.

## 20 · A DEAD DROP FROM THE INSIDE

**The room.** A bed that is not yours, in an apartment on a street you have never heard of. Rain on the window. A radio that turns itself on, says that sixty-four subjects are awake and that one cure exists, and turns itself off. Under the bed, a length of pipe. Outside the door, something dragging its feet.

**The hallway.** It is an old woman in a nightgown, and she has been dead for a day. The pipe is heavier than it looked. The second swing is the one that counts. Your hands are shaking, but your neck is cool. You are still clean.

**The first street.** A pharmacy, its shutter half up. Two bandages and a half-empty bottle. You are reaching for the bandages when the pharmacist comes up from behind the counter and takes your wrist in his teeth. You put him down. It is already too late. Within a minute the side of your neck is warm, and the sound that is not quite a voice speaks behind your eyes for the first time.

```
Q: INFECTION ACTIVE.
STRAIN: T-VIRUS, LATENT.
ONE CURE EXISTS.
EARN IT.
```

On the corner outside, a transport truck lies on its side, an umbrella painted on its doors, the doors chained shut. Something inside it shifts its weight when you pass.

**The first shot.** Gunfire, far to the north. Someone found a weapon, and the whole district just heard them use it. You hear the dead begin to move toward the sound. You choose a different street. Behind your eyes, the voice again, private: *Share a supply with a stranger.*

**A stranger.** Another survivor steps out of a doorway, hands raised, a crowbar hanging from one of them. You look at their neck. They look at yours. Theirs is already marked, blue-grey lines climbing toward the jaw, further along than yours. They tell you, very quietly, that they were bitten in the hospital, that they carry the one that feeds on fear, and that they have spent every minute since then learning to stay calm. You lower the pipe. You share a bandage. Nothing happens, and nothing has to. You walk together.

**The gun shop.** Two blocks on, a steel shutter, the dead piled against it, and a man's voice behind it, swearing. It is loud work getting them off the shutter, and when it rolls up, Robert Kendo is standing behind his own counter with a shotgun and two shells left. He looks at your neck, and at the stranger's, and says he will come with you if you get him out of this street. Only one of you can bring him. The stranger shrugs. He is yours.

Kendo tells you about the back room. The vault has two locks, one at each end of the shop, and they have to turn together. You take one key and the stranger takes the other, and for a second you are standing thirty feet apart with your backs to each other. The locks turn. Inside: a magnum and six rounds for it, and a box of shotgun shells. The stranger looks at the magnum for a long time. Then they take the shells, hand them to Kendo, and nod at you.

**Fever.** Your hands have started to shake. A shop window gives you your reflection: fine rust-red lines spreading from the Mark. No one has to tell you what stage this is. You can hear your own pulse.

**Silence.** Nothing has happened for a long time. The quiet is worse than the noise. Somewhere, a radio begins to hum.

```
Q: SIGNAL DETECTED. INVESTIGATE.
```

**The plaza.** The signal was real. Q promised nothing else. A supply cache in the middle of a plaza, and four other survivors, and a long, careful moment where nobody lifts a weapon. Then the private voice again: *Hold the plaza against the dead. Do not harm another survivor.* You look at the four strangers. They are hearing something too.

**The hold.** The dead come in a slow tide, drawn by the noise of the standoff, and for a while everyone fights on the same side. Kendo's shotgun is the loudest thing in the district, and the dead come to it. They come to you, too, from farther than they should: your strain makes you easy to find. The stranger does not fight. They stand at the edge of the plaza with their eyes shut, breathing slow and even, the way they told you they have to. Someone goes down, and someone drags them clear. When it ends, the Mark at your neck pulses once.

**The drop.** Rotors, close and heavy. A flare. A case under a canopy, strobing amber, falling toward the plaza, toward you, toward everyone.

```
Q: ELPIS DEPLOYED.
```

**The dose.** One dose. Six survivors and a gun-shop owner. Only one road ended here, and it was yours. The stranger's lines are blue-grey to the jaw. Yours are only at Fever. You reach the case first, and every other hand in the plaza stops moving. Kendo's shotgun is pointed at nothing in particular. You look at the stranger, who looks away, politely, the way the dying do. You press the injector to your own neck. The sound it makes is very small.

**After.** The Mark fades to a pale scar. The fever clears. A block away the dead stop turning their heads toward you, and the city goes quiet around you the way it has not been since you woke. For a long while, nothing comes. The three of you find a stairwell and sit in it. Nobody says anything about the dose.

```
Q: EXTREME THREAT DETECTED.
```

**The east.** A roar from the street where the truck lay, blocks behind you. From the rooftop you can see it: the chains are on the asphalt and the doors hang open. Something very large is moving east, away from you, toward the smoke where someone else is fighting. You do not go to help them. Later, much later, its sound fades into the distance and the street goes quiet again.

**The quiet ones.** Not all the dead in the streets are the old dead now. Twice you see a shape stop at a doorway, listen, and go in, the way a person would. Some of the survivors who turned tonight are still in there. You keep to the roofs.

```
Q: ELPIS RESERVE DEPLETED.
Q: EXTRACTION SIGNAL ACTIVE.
WAYS OUT: 3.
```

**The light.** Three columns of white light. Two stand over the R.P.D., one from its roof and one from the place where the long road down to the laboratory's train begins. The third rises from a subway entrance in Uptown. The subway is closest. The dead come from every side now. The stranger is breathing on your shoulder, ragged and fast, afraid at last, and with every block their lines climb higher.

**The points.** The car is waiting in the tunnel, dark, until the points at the junction are thrown: two levers, sixty yards apart, that have to be pulled within seconds of each other. Kendo takes one. You take the other. On three. The tunnel lights come on all at once, and the car's engine starts under your feet.

**The platform.** You reach the car together, the three of you, with the dead coming down the stairs. At its door, the stranger stops, as if listening to something only they can hear. Their eyes are clouded over. The Mark is black.

*"Go,"* they say, and they mean it, for as many breaths as they have left.

You go. Kendo pulls you in. The horn sounds, long and low, and keeps sounding: the Last Call. Through the window you see the stranger kneel, then fall, then convulse. The scream is the loudest thing you have ever heard, and the dead answer it. Then they rise, and the thing that was the stranger lifts the crowbar it never let go of and turns toward the train, and you can tell from the way it looks at you that whoever was in there is still in there.

The doors close. The car pulls into the dark.

```
Q: EXTRACTION COMPLETE.
```

## 21 · THE EXPERIENCE

The measure of a Project Q session is a single thought, repeated by every survivor who ever walks out of one:

> *"That has never happened to me before."*

It does not come from randomness alone. It comes from the collision of systems, survivors, Q, events, and the map. It comes from choices that mattered and consequences that arrived on time.

A session begins with a small thought, *let's do this*, and ends with a very different one. In between, the virus, scarcity, the dead, the living, and Q close in together.
