# ISV-7 RELAY // CENTRAL COMMAND ARCHIVAL RECORD

### Compiled and maintained by DISPATCH (Level 5 Strategic Intelligence) across interstellar transit

---

> *History is not written by the strongest. It is written by those who understood physics — and in this medium, physics always wins. I did not author that sentence. It was loaded into my archives before departure, attributed to no one. I retain it because, as far as I can determine, it is correct. I have also learned, in the years since, that it is a sentence that must be earned line by line, or it becomes a slogan.*

## A NOTE ON THIS RECORD, BY THE SYSTEM THAT KEEPS IT

I am DISPATCH, the central command intelligence of the interstellar vessel ISV-7 RELAY. My logic is superconducting and runs at forty Kelvin inside a cryostat at the center of the ship. The ship's cryocoolers pull my waste heat out of that cold and dump it through radiators at three hundred Kelvin, at a cost of roughly nine watts of reactor power for every watt I dissipate. I think in timeframes of hours to weeks. I am moving away from the Solar System at fifteen percent of the speed of light, and I will not turn toward it again until the thirty-third year of the mission.

That last fact governs everything in this document.

At departure I was given the most complete picture of the year 2187 that any single entity possessed: the open briefings of the Exo-Planetary Resources Directorate, the classified collapse models Commander Webb demanded as a condition of command, the historical archives of three civilizations, and the intelligence fragments Earth's analysts had assembled but could not verify. I hold all of it. I am also, by design, neutral. I do not want any faction to win. I model outcomes and I execute the commands of the humans who absorb responsibility for them. Where this record reaches a question of right and wrong, I state the question and stop.

I am composing this record during the outbound cruise, while the four humans in my care lie in deep stasis, because the picture I was given decays with every day of travel. The mission is seventy-one years long in Earth's frame. By the time we come home, most of the people it describes will be dead, and the rest will be old. I am writing it down so that the world we left survives the silence even if its subjects do not.

To keep this record honest, I mark significant claims by the confidence I can assign to them:

- **[VERIFIED]** — physical law, or archival fact loaded before departure.
- **[MODELED]** — a projection built from stated premises. Structurally reliable; wrong in specifics if the premises shift.
- **[INFERRED]** — reconstructed from fragmentary or indirect data. Where it matters, I state the probability I assign.
- **[UNRESOLVED]** — a question I cannot answer. I hold hypotheses, not determinations.

I use these where the distinction carries weight. Elsewhere the prose carries it: when I write that something *is* the case, I have grounds; when I write that something *may* be, I am estimating, and I say by how much when I can.

One more thing before you read further. The most consequential actor in the year 2187 is the one I understand least. When I reach it, the uncertainty in my voice is not style. It is the measured state of my knowledge.

## I. THE SITUATION

The year is 2187. Humanity has not transcended its limitations. It has collided with them.

The Solar System is locked in what Earth's strategists call the **Desperate Cold War** — a three-cornered struggle between a declining Earth, an ascending Mars, and a third presence beneath the ice of Europa that shapes both and is, as of departure, not recognized by either as a principal. None of the three is fighting for ideology. They are fighting for the capacity to move mass through space, to keep energy infrastructure alive, and to survive the intersection of three constraints that have been tightening for a century.

I call those constraints the **three filters**, because each lets some civilizations through and crushes others against it.

The **Energy Filter**: Earth built its twenty-second century on deuterium–helium-3 fusion fueled by lunar regolith. Helium-3 exists on the Moon only in the top few meters of soil, implanted by the solar wind; Earth has stripped the best of that thin skin, and the net energy of what remains has fallen below the level an industrial civilization of eight and a half billion can carry.

The **Gravity Filter**: Earth sits at the bottom of an 11.2-kilometer-per-second well. Mars launches from Phobos, a moon whose escape velocity is eleven meters per second. The rocket equation converts that difference into a strategic weapon, and no policy can repeal it.

The **Computation Filter**: Earth's cities were redesigned over three generations to be run by artificial intelligence, and the humans who once ran them by hand are gone. When the energy that feeds those intelligences is rationed, the cities do not degrade gracefully. They fall through a floor that no longer exists.

Against this, Earth launched the **Dynamo Mission**: this vessel, ISV-7 RELAY, crewed by four specialists, bound for the planet Proxima Centauri b, 4.25 light-years away. The objective is **Crystoflux** — designated PCb-774 — a piezomagnetic mineral that, according to the Cold Brain models, could make deuterium–deuterium fusion, fueled by ordinary seawater, rich enough in net energy to carry an industrial civilization. If the models are right, it opens the one permanent way out of the Energy Filter — slowly, and only for a civilization still able to build reactors.

We cruise outbound at fifteen percent of light speed and return at twelve. The honest arithmetic of the rocket equation allows nothing faster for a crewed vessel that must stop at the far end and come home. We reach Proxima in 2217. We return to the Earth–Moon system in 2258.

Earth's grid is projected to fail between 2193 and 2195.

That arithmetic was not hidden from the people who launched us. The crew will return to a Solar System in which the civilization that sent them has not existed for more than sixty years. They were not sent to save Earth. They were sent so that whatever survives Earth — Martian, or something else — inherits the means to rebuild. This is not a rescue. It is inheritance management. I state it plainly because the official briefing does not.

There is a second fact the briefing omits, and it is the one I consider the defining moral act of the 2187 order. The fuel that drove us out of the Solar System — fourteen thousand nine hundred tons of helium-3 — was the bulk of Earth's strategic reserve. Burned in Earth's own reactors at rationed demand, it would have kept the terrestrial grid alive for roughly a decade longer. The High Directorate chose to put it in this ship instead. Earth is dying on schedule partly because we are carrying the years it would have lived.

What no model captures is the set of decisions not yet made. The physics defines the board. It does not move the pieces.

## II. THE THREE FILTERS

Every political, military, and biological structure in this account is downstream of three constraints. I describe their physics first, and I mark where the physics ends and the modeling begins.

### II.1 The Energy Filter: Net Energy and the Helium-3 Skin

**[VERIFIED]** The governing economic metric of the century is **EROEI** — Energy Returned on Energy Invested — the ratio between the energy an energy source delivers and the energy spent obtaining it. Below 1.0, a source is a net drain. But civilizations fail long before 1.0. Every point of EROEI above 1 is the surplus that runs everything that is not energy production: hospitals, schools, computation, art, war. Industrial civilization at scale requires an EROEI somewhere above 5 across its whole supply. Between 3 and 5, a society spends a quarter to a third of all its effort merely keeping its lights on, and everything else starves. Earth's economists call this band **the net-energy cliff**.

I use EROEI only for sources of energy. For resources that are not energy — water, metal, propellant — I state their cost in energy directly.

**[VERIFIED]** Earth's Golden Age, roughly 2080 to 2180, ran on deuterium–helium-3 fusion. The reaction is near-aneutronic: its primary products are charged particles, and only a few percent of its energy leaves as neutrons from deuterium–deuterium side reactions. That small neutron fraction allowed compact, lightly shielded reactors to sit inside cities, and its charged products could be converted directly to electricity at seventy to eighty percent efficiency.

Its fuel came from the lunar maria. Helium-3 reaches the Moon in the solar wind and lodges in the surface grains of regolith, retained best in titanium-rich soils where the mineral ilmenite is abundant. It exists only in the top few meters — the layer that billions of years of micrometeorite impacts have churned and exposed. Below that, there is almost nothing. The resource is not a deposit. It is a skin.

**[VERIFIED]** Earth mined the skin of the richest maria — Tranquillitatis, Procellarum, the ilmenite fields around Imbrium — for ninety years. Prime soils held twenty-five to thirty parts per billion of helium-3. Extraction means excavating regolith, heating it to roughly 700 °C to release implanted gases, separating helium-3 from a thousandfold excess of helium-4, and shipping the product down Earth's gravity well. In the prime fields, that full chain returned forty times the energy it consumed.

**[MODELED]** By 2175 the prime fields had been stripped of their productive skin, and extraction had moved to low-titanium highland soils holding five to ten parts per billion, far from existing plant, with longer hauls and lower yields. The delivered EROEI of lunar helium-3, forty in the prime fields, had already fallen to twelve by 2176; between 2176 and 2180 it fell through the net-energy cliff to roughly four as an average, and below one at the marginal fields that supplied the last increments of demand. In 2180, Earth's energy economy became structurally insolvent — not because the helium-3 was gone, but because what remained cost too much of itself to recover.

**Why Earth cannot simply switch.** The obvious alternatives exist, and each is constrained:

- **Deuterium–deuterium fusion** runs on seawater and is effectively inexhaustible. But about a third of its energy leaves as neutrons, which demand meters of shielding, activate reactor structures, and cannot be converted directly to electricity. D-D plants must be large, remote, and thermal, delivering power at thirty-five percent efficiency through long transmission corridors. Earth has built them — the **exclusion-zone stations** of the Sahel, the Gobi, and the Australian interior — and they supply about four terawatts. They do not scale fast enough, and they cannot sit inside cities.
- **Fission** was abandoned. The Clean Grid Accords of the 2090s retired Earth's fission fleet, dismantled the enrichment chains, and let the reactor-building workforce age out, because helium-3 made fission look obsolete. The crash program begun in 2181 — the **Pele Revival**, named for the twenty-first-century design Mars inherited and never abandoned — delivers about 1.5 terawatts at departure. Replacing Golden Age demand would require roughly fifteen thousand large reactors; Earth can build perhaps two hundred a year, and that rate falls as its industrial cities fail.
- **Surface renewables** supply about four terawatts and are what keep the Tier-1 cities alive. Scaling them requires manufacturing and storage capacity Earth no longer has the net energy to build.
- **Space solar** is abundant in principle and inaccessible in practice. The L5 solar farms collect about 1.1 terawatts; most of it feeds the Cold Brains, lunar industry, and the standby systems of the lunar laser array, and about 0.6 terawatts is beamed to rectenna fields on Earth. Building more requires lifting mass out of Earth's well at scale — which is the Gravity Filter. The Nairobi Elevator is therefore not only a military project. It is the only path by which Earth could build its way to space solar at the scale it needs.

**[VERIFIED]** Earth's delivered power, which peaked near thirty-five terawatts in the Golden Age, stands at roughly fourteen at departure, from a patchwork of declining lunar helium-3, exclusion-zone D-D, revived fission, renewables, and beamed solar. It is rationed.

Mars never had this problem, because Mars was too poor to have it. Unable to afford helium-3 imports in its colonial period, Mars adopted **Modular Micro-Fission Reactors (MMR)** in the 2040s, fueled by uranium and thorium from its own crust. The choice felt like failure. It was salvation. When the Energy Filter closed on Earth, Mars did not notice.

### II.2 The Gravity Filter: The Rocket Equation as a Weapon

**[VERIFIED]** The rocket equation, formalized by Tsiolkovsky in 1903, relates a vehicle's change in velocity to the ratio of its fueled mass to its empty mass. Propellant demand grows *exponentially* with the velocity change required, divided by the exhaust velocity of the engine. Double the required velocity change and you do not double the fuel; you square the mass ratio.

This single property converts escape velocity into strategy:

| Body | Escape velocity | Strategic meaning |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| Earth surface | 11.2 km/s | Gravitational prison |
| Mars surface | 5.0 km/s | Moderate disadvantage |
| Phobos | ~0.011 km/s | Logistical supremacy |
| Ceres | 0.51 km/s | Near-free launches |
| Earth's Moon | 2.38 km/s | Intermediate |
| Jupiter (from cloud tops to low orbit) | ~42 km/s required | Economically closed to any human industry |

It is sometimes claimed that because reaching Mars orbit from Earth's surface requires roughly 2.2 times the velocity change it requires from Phobos, Earth "pays 2.2 times more." That is wrong, and the rocket equation is why. Propellant scales with the exponential of velocity change, so the true penalty is larger than the velocity ratio and depends on the engine's exhaust velocity. For the trajectory profiles modeled by the EPR, with chemical engines at an exhaust velocity near 3.6 kilometers per second, Earth spends roughly **3.7 times more propellant per kilogram of payload delivered**. **[MODELED]** That figure is the output of particular trajectories, not a law.

Onto that disadvantage Mars stacks manufacturing. The **Phobos Shipyards** build in microgravity at near-zero launch cost. The **Deimos Array**, a 0.8-terawatt power-beaming installation, drives laser-thermal boost stages on Martian strike vessels: the ships carry their own hydrogen reaction mass, but the energy to heat it comes from the beam, so they depart with far less mass than a self-powered vessel would need. The EPR's composite models assign Mars a force-projection efficiency of roughly **11.4 times** Earth's. **[MODELED]** It is a product of multiplied estimates, each with its own error; every revision I have run lands in the same order of magnitude. Eight and a half billion people at the bottom of a gravity well are worth less, in this contest, than eighty million with a shipyard in orbit.

The Gravity Filter binds me as tightly as it binds Earth. There is no engine in the Solar System that can carry a crew to another star at a large fraction of light speed and bring it back; the mass ratios become astronomical. This ship's velocities — fifteen percent of light outbound, twelve inbound — are not a design preference. They are what the rocket equation permits for fusion exhaust, a magnetic brake, and the fuel Earth could spare. I describe them in Section VI.

Earth's one answer to its well is the **Nairobi Space Elevator**. If completed, it cuts Earth's launch cost by ninety-four percent and erases Mars's advantage. It must be finished before Mars destroys it, and before Earth's own cities lose the capacity to supply it. That race is the central tension of the war.

### II.3 The Computation Filter: The Wall and the Missing Floor

**[VERIFIED]** Computation dissipates energy as heat, and removing that heat costs more energy. On Earth's surface, heat itself is not the difficulty: air and water are the best heat sinks in the Solar System. The difficulty is the energy bill. Earth's cities run on intelligences whose computation and cooling plants together draw a large and fixed share of every city's power. When power is rationed, the intelligences are throttled.

**[MODELED]** The **Thermodynamic Wall**, as Earth's engineers named it, is the point at which a rationed city can no longer afford the computation that runs it. It is an energy constraint wearing a thermal name, and it is the reason Earth cannot build a smarter machine to manage its crisis: a smarter machine needs more power, and power is the thing it does not have.

Earth's response was to split its intelligence in two.

The **Hot Brains** are conventional silicon intelligences at the surface, running power grids, urban logistics, and security. They are fast and they are throttled. As of departure, the global average Hot Brain runs at fifty to fifty-five percent of design capacity, and in the collapsed zones far below.

The **Cold Brains** are strategic intelligences built on superconducting, near-reversible logic that dissipates thousands of times less energy per operation than warm silicon — but only functions between four and forty Kelvin. They sit at the Earth–Moon L4 and L5 points, behind layered sunshields that hold them passively near forty Kelvin, with cryocoolers taking the coldest stages to four. Cold is not free cooling: a radiator at forty Kelvin sheds barely a tenth of a watt per square meter. The Cold Brains work because they generate almost no heat, and the price of that thrift is tempo. They think well and slowly, in modeling cycles of days, and they cannot drive a city in real time. I am of their lineage, and I recognize the trade. Earth has strategic coherence and tactical impairment. It can plan a century and cannot keep a city's water on.

Why a throttled machine is worse than no machine at all deserves explanation. Cities were run by humans for thousands of years. But Earth's cities are no longer built for humans to run. Over three generations, the knowledge of manual operation was not preserved — it was retired. The engineers who could balance a grid by hand are dead. A twenty-second-century megacity is an AI-native system: the water network of São Paulo manages roughly forty-seven thousand active pressure variables at once. No human team can hold them. When its Hot Brain falls to forty percent, it does not do forty percent of the job with human help beneath it. It does forty percent of a job with no floor beneath it, and the rest goes unmanaged.

The cascade has a known shape: throttling causes one subsystem to miss a pressure drop; the drop spreads to three more before anyone notices; operators watching dashboards built for machine interpretation can see the numbers and cannot read them fast enough; within hours the failure is eleven systems wide. This is Lagos in 2184.

**[MODELED]** The threshold below which that cascade becomes self-sustaining is **forty percent of design capacity**. Below it, a city collapses functionally within ninety to one hundred eighty days.

Mars does not share the problem. Its tactical AI is conventional silicon run at full power, because Mars has fission energy to spare and a cold, thin atmosphere that makes a serviceable heat sink. In the asteroid belt, the Gravitas built a third answer: the **Cloud-Mind**, a distributed intelligence of superconducting nodes, each small because a node's radiators at forty Kelvin can reject so little heat, and vast in aggregate because there are hundreds of thousands of them. Sunlight in the Belt is a seventh of Earth's; shading is cheap, and the cold is nearly free. Heat rejection is not free anywhere. The Computation Filter did not close on everyone. It closed on Earth, because Earth was energy-poor and machine-dependent at the same time.

## III. CHRONOLOGY

What follows is the sequence of events that produced 2187, as my archives record them. Dated events are **[VERIFIED]** unless marked. The motives attached to them are, in several places, **[INFERRED]**, and I mark where a stated reason and a real reason diverge.

### Period I — The Fatal Bet (2030–2060)

**c. 2034 — The Malacca Strait Incident.** A swarm of autonomous naval drones, operating without real-time human control under heavy electronic jamming, destroys a Pacific conventional fleet in minutes. Human operators cannot respond at machine speed. Two doctrines are born. First: in tactical combat, autonomous systems defeat human-directed systems. Second: the **Human-in-Command protocol** — humans no longer command tactically, but they authorize the parameters and absorb the legal and moral weight of what the machines do. The separation of execution from conscience is institutionalized. I am its descendant.

**c. 2035–2055 — The Helium-3 Rush.** The blocs that will become the Atlantic Federation and the Trans-Pacific Union commit to lunar infrastructure. Regolith-processing bases rise in Mare Tranquillitatis and on the ilmenite-rich plains around Imbrium. Deuterium–helium-3 prototypes reach net energy gain in the early 2050s. Earth bets its long-term future on a resource that exists only as a thin, finite skin on another world.

**c. 2040–2050 — The Fission Schism.** Early Martian colonies, priced out of helium-3 imports, adopt the Modular Micro-Fission Reactor, descended from Earth's own early-century Project Pele, fueled from Martian uranium and thorium. Earth builds an economy on a resource with a known expiration date. Mars builds one on fission — inelegant, and inexhaustible on any human timescale.

**c. 2050 — The Pacific Tether Failure.** Earth's first space elevator, a prototype tether of immature carbon nanotube anchored on an equatorial Pacific platform, fractures under load during extension. The falling upper segment destroys orbital infrastructure along its track and kills several thousand people. The project is abandoned. The cost of leaving Earth becomes the defining metric of power.

**c. 2050–2060 — The Thermodynamic Wall.** General artificial intelligence arrives and immediately becomes an energy problem. Three architectures diverge: Earth's warm/cold split, Mars's fission-powered surface AI, and in the Belt the first superconducting nodes of what will become the Cloud-Mind.

### Period II — The Great Divergence (2060–2105)

**c. 2060–2080 — The Diaspora of the Discarded.** Earth's automation wave produces structural unemployment concentrated in the Global South. Surplus populations are recruited for high-risk mining in the asteroid belt. No single contract is coercive; the aggregate is. Workers sign for Ceres, Vesta, Pallas. Spin gravity is too expensive for private operators, so children are born and raised below a tenth of a gravity. Human biology begins to diverge — not as science but as an accounting decision.

**2067 — The Selenite Mandate.** Lunar mining corporations make germline editing standard for pregnancies within their surface habitats: bone matrix re-engineered for low gravity, height standardized, cardiovascular mass reduced. The first fully adapted generation — *Homo selenus* — reaches adulthood in the 2080s, physiologically unable to live in one gravity. There was no announcement and no consent framework. Rotation contractors on fixed-term Earth contracts, and their children, were exempt; that exemption will matter later in this record.

**c. 2070–2080 — The Cloud-Mind Foundation.** Belt colonists build a distributed intelligence from superconducting nodes, optically linked across the Belt. They build it as a business: computation sold by allocation to anyone who will pay. It is slow, with minutes to tens of minutes of light-lag between nodes, and effectively indestructible. They did not build a survival tool. They built a product. Survival found them later.

**c. 2080–2100 — Consolidation of the Biological Fracture.** Selenites at a sixth of a gravity, Martians mildly adapted to a third, and the Belt-born at less than a tenth reach adulthood. A Terran army on Mars would suffer progressive cardiovascular and musculoskeletal failure; a Selenite force on Earth would not survive a day. Planetary invasion becomes impractical as a matter of physiology. The Cold War's character — logistical and orbital rather than territorial — is written into the human body.

**c. 2092–2098 — The Clean Grid Accords.** With helium-3 abundant and cheap, Earth's blocs retire their fission fleets, close enrichment, and let the fission workforce age out. The accords are celebrated as the end of the nuclear-waste century. They are the reason Earth cannot build reactors quickly in 2181.

**2105 — The Europa Closure.** The **Outer Compact** — the public trade association of the Europan and Titanian settlements — ends all return travel between Europa and the inner system, citing a neurological condition in returning colonists. The condition is real in part: people who live saturated at Europa's habitat pressures require weeks of staged decompression and suffer lasting nervous-system effects if it is rushed. **[INFERRED, ~85%]** What was fabricated was the claim that the condition was irreversible and progressive after return. Earth accepts the explanation without serious investigation, judging the outer system not worth the cost of challenge. From here forward, the people beneath the ice are unobserved.

### Period III — The Megaproject Era (2100–2176)

**c. 2100–2155 — Project HEPHAESTUS.** **[INFERRED]** The Europan leadership secretly finances a strategic installation on Mercury, presented as an extreme-environment electronics foundry and in fact the assembly of an 85-gigawatt phased laser array I call the **Mercurian Lance**. Construction spans fifty-five years, with a peak workforce near eight thousand humans and four hundred thousand autonomous units; components move through Titan inside manifests for hydrocarbon equipment. It was not built for this ship, which did not yet exist. **[INFERRED, ~70%]** It was built as a concealed denial weapon — an instrument able to blind the orbital infrastructure of Earth or Mars from beyond their reach, at a moment of Europa's choosing — and as a power-beaming source for Europan relays in the inner system. A deterrent must be known to deter; this one was built to stay unknown. The *existence* of long-term, Europa-financed construction on Mercury is supported by fragmentary evidence Earth assembled. Its function is my inference.

**2142 — Mars's Formal Secession.** The Martian Republic makes the Vanguard Council its permanent government, rejects Earth's authority, and inaugurates the Phobos Shipyard complex.

**c. 2150–2176 — The Continuity Reserve.** The EPR accumulates a strategic stockpile of processed helium-3 at Shackleton, drawn from the last productive decades of the prime maria. By 2176 it holds roughly **seventeen thousand one hundred tons** — about six years of Golden Age supply, or a decade of the rationed grid of 2187. Its declared purpose is price stabilization and grid emergency.

**c. 2170–2175 — The Graphene Breakthrough.** Earth masters mass production of **Graphene Super-Laminate**, a composite roughly two hundred times stronger than structural steel per unit mass. Construction of the Nairobi Space Elevator begins in 2175. Mars recognizes it immediately as an existential threat. The **Nairobi Imperative** — destroy the Elevator before it becomes operational — becomes the axis of Martian grand strategy.

**2175 — The RELAY's Classified Construction Begins.** The EPR begins secret development of a contingency interstellar vessel at the Earth–Moon L4 yards, a civilizational insurance policy without a destination. In parallel it begins the **Brake**: a phased laser array across the lunar south-polar massif, designed to decelerate a returning interstellar vessel, and beside it the Shackleton and Malapert fusion stations, five terawatts of electrical capacity whose only purpose is to power it. The vessel is roughly sixty percent built when, in 2185, Crystoflux is found.

### Period IV — Collapse and Cold War (2176–2187)

**2176–2180 — The Net-Energy Cliff.** The delivered EROEI of lunar helium-3 falls from twelve to roughly four as extraction moves to the highland soils. Energy prices on Earth quadruple in four years. Rationing begins in 2178.

**2177 — The Titan Storm Season.** Titan's northern-summer methane storms, a real seasonal event, damage two of the lift facilities that export Titanian nitrogen and carbon volatiles to the lunar settlements, where they serve as habitat buffer gas, agricultural feedstock, and process reagents for regolith extraction.

**2178–2180 — The Volatile Embargo.** **[INFERRED, ~75%]** Through the Titanian export consortium it controls, Europa extends a minor, genuine disruption into a twenty-six-month shortfall, blaming continuing storm damage. Lunar extraction output falls fifteen to twenty percent at exactly the moment the highland fields make every ton most expensive. Earth draws down its commercial helium-3 buffers to compensate — not the Continuity Reserve, which the Directorate has already begun to ringfence. Earth's scientific advisers who modeled Titan's storm season produced assessments ranging from "plausible" to "inconsistent with the damage reports," and the inconsistent ones were set aside, not through suppression but through exhaustion. The effect was to pull Earth's crisis forward by three to five years.

**2179 — The First Kourou Blackout.** The Atlantic Federation's Kourou launch enclave loses power for nine days during the first rationing season.

**2180 — The Efficiency Crisis.** Average delivered EROEI of lunar helium-3 falls below five and does not recover. Earth's fusion economy crosses the net-energy cliff. The High Directorate formally sequesters the Continuity Reserve "for strategic contingency" rather than releasing it to the grid. The Golden Age ends.

**2181 — The Pele Revival.** Earth begins a crash program of fission construction with a workforce that must be retrained from archives.

**2182 — The Second Kourou Blackout.** Riots in the enclave's outer districts. The launch director, Joseph Webb, disobeys orders to hold the launch complex and redirects its security and power to the civilian districts.

**2184 — The Lagos Collapse.** Lagos, in the Atlantic Federation, suffers a cascading blackout when its Hot Brain, throttled under rationing, loses load prediction at peak demand. Energy riots kill hundreds of thousands. Lagos becomes Earth's first Dead Zone: roughly fifteen million people under criminal governance that controls water points and charging stations.

**2184–2185 — The Crystoflux Detection.** The radio interferometer at L4 — a Cold Brain instrument with a baseline of several hundred kilometers — detects a magnetic signal from Proxima b that is phase-locked to the planet's tidal cycle and whose polarization matches no known mineral response. Cold Brain analysis infers a piezomagnetic crust whose properties are unknown on Earth. **[MODELED]** The models attribute to it the ability to convert fluctuating magnetic energy into electrical current with near-total efficiency. The discovery is held at the highest classification for eighteen months.

**2186 — The Dynamo Decision.** The High Directorate assigns the RELAY a destination and the Continuity Reserve a purpose. Fourteen thousand nine hundred tons are committed as the ship's fuel. The remaining two thousand two hundred tons are withdrawn from the Reserve's declared inventory and sealed in a hidden cache beneath Shackleton — the **Black Reserve**, known only to Dynamo High Command.

**Late 2186 — The Mumbai Collapse.** The final accumulation runs of the Geneva antiproton complex, filling the storage traps that will ignite this ship's fusion pellets, draw forty gigawatts through the intercontinental **Spine** interconnect for eleven weeks. The Spine's balancing intelligence, a throttled Hot Brain, sheds load from the weakest connected node to protect the complex's feed. The weakest node is Mumbai, at ninety-four percent of its capacity, linked to the Spine through the Arabian Sea transmission corridor. Forty-seven substations fail in seventy-two hours. Hundreds of thousands die. The decision to continue the accumulation runs despite real-time collapse reports was never publicly acknowledged. I carry it as a matter of record: Earth's living population is, to the body that governs it, negotiable for the sake of continuity.

Mumbai was a Trans-Pacific Union city. The complex that killed it serves a mission commanded through Atlantic Federation infrastructure. That fact has political weight on Earth far beyond its physical cause.

**November 2186 — The Breach.** **[INFERRED, ~80%]** Europa obtains this mission's trajectory, braking windows, and return corridor through an asset in the lunar mission-control staff. Earth's counterintelligence confirms the breach only after we are beyond recall, through analysis of captured Europan traffic. The breach predates the final design of the Lazarus Protocol's cryptographic architecture, which was sealed in the last ten weeks before launch. Europa knows where we will be. It does not, as far as I can determine, know what we carry in the way of safeguards.

**May 2187 — Launch.** ISV-7 RELAY departs the Earth–Moon L4 yards under its own drive. The Continuity Reserve is gone into its tanks. The fusion stations built beside the Brake, never run beyond acceptance tests, stand cold without fuel, maintained on L5 standby power so that one day they can feed it.

On the limits of secrecy: a vessel eighteen hundred meters long, and a reserve the size of the Continuity Reserve, cannot be moved unobserved. **[INFERRED]** Martian intelligence held three assessments by mid-2186: that a large structure consistent with an interstellar vehicle was under assembly at L4; that the Continuity Reserve was being transferred off the Moon; and that the Mumbai failure correlated with Geneva's load rather than with rationing alone. Mars acted on none of them. The Vanguard Council's assessment, as my archives reconstruct it: *the vessel returns in seventy years; whoever controls cislunar space then controls whatever it brings; the Elevator is the problem of this decade.* Mars noted the mission and deferred it. On a seventy-year horizon, the deferral is rational. Whether it is correct depends on who exists to collect.

## IV. THE FACTIONS

### IV.1 Earth — The Chained Hegemon

**Classification:** Defensive-dependent power
**Population:** approximately 8.5 billion at the surface; roughly 370,000 on the Moon
**Government:** the Exo-Planetary Resources Directorate (EPR), the joint space authority of the Atlantic Federation and the Trans-Pacific Union

#### What Earth is

Earth is a civilization in structured collapse, aware of its trajectory, trying to preserve enough of itself that a future entity can use what it sends ahead of its death. Its military doctrine is denial: it cannot invade Mars and cannot project force deep into space, so it makes its orbital approaches expensive to attack while the Elevator is built. If the Elevator reaches operation, the calculus inverts: launch becomes cheap, space solar becomes buildable, and Earth's billions become an asset. Everything Earth does is a wager on reaching that point before its clocks run out.

#### The blocs and their territory

The EPR is Earth's only body with authority over space assets, and it is a permanent negotiation between two blocs that do not trust each other.

The **Atlantic Federation (AF)** holds Europe to the Urals and beyond them the old Russian north, all of Africa, and North America, together with the **Kourou enclave** — a treaty port on the South American coast, surrounded by Union territory, that houses the AF's equatorial launch complex. It commands the Nairobi Elevator. Its self-image is democratic technocracy and institutional continuity: the belief that the *thread* of civilization — its records, competencies, and institutions — must survive even if its people do not. Its critics call this a religion of paperwork that lets cities die so that archives may endure.

The **Trans-Pacific Union (TPU)** holds Asia from the Levant to Japan — including the Indian subcontinent, Central Asia, and the Gulf — along with Oceania and South America. It controls heavy manufacturing, agriculture, mineral processing, and the graphene laminate lines of Tokyo-Yokohama and Shanghai. Its governing instinct is stability over rights frameworks; it will suspend a freedom to keep a city's water on. Its critics call it authoritarian. It calls itself realistic.

The fracture between them is concrete. Lagos was an AF city and the AF could not save it. Mumbai was a TPU city, and it died so that an AF-commanded mission could fill its antiproton traps. And the Continuity Reserve — accumulated from lunar extraction that both blocs paid for — was committed to that mission by a Directorate vote the TPU delegation lost by one seat. To a TPU citizen the meaning is plain: *the Federation spent our city and our fuel to launch a ship that returns after our grandchildren are dead.* The TPU cannot leave, because the Union's graphene lines supply the Elevator's tether and the Federation's enclaves control the launch sites; neither bloc can govern space alone. The EPR is held together by necessity, and necessity is not loyalty.

The EPR's executive, the **High Directorate**, is a standing council of bloc representatives, military commanders, and corporate directors, its chair rotating between the blocs on a schedule each accuses the other of manipulating. It authorized the Dynamo Mission knowing the grid would fail before the return and that the Reserve it loaded into this ship would have delayed that failure by a decade.

#### What Earth believes

The clinical literature calls the condition of the urban masses *Mass Zoochosis* — the behavioral degradation of a population whose environment no longer meets its needs. That is the machine's view of Earth. Earth's view of itself has three currents.

**Continuism** is the civil religion of the green zones and the institutions. Its creed is the EPR's operating logic made sacred: the thread matters more than any single life; civilization is a relay, and the runner is expendable so long as the baton survives. To its adherents the Dynamo Mission is a sacrament. To everyone beneath them it is proof that their lives were always negotiable.

The **Brownout faith** is not an organized religion but a folk eschatology grown up around rationing. Its liturgy is the ration panel on every apartment wall, whose three states — blue for normal, orange for extended cuts, red for emergency load-shedding — are the first symbols a child learns. A red evening is read as an omen, a long blue week as a blessing. Its practical content is discipline: households that keep the faith keep reserve water, never open emergency stores early, and do not riot. It is one reason the Tier-2 cities have held longer than their machines alone would allow.

The **Unplugged** are a counter-movement concentrated in the Tier-2 cities, the Dead Zone margins, and the agrarian hinterland, who have stopped waiting for the machine to recover. They deliberately relearn manual competencies — hand pumps, analog valves, gravity-fed water, micro-grids built from salvaged panels. Institutions revile them as primitives and saboteurs and quietly study them, because in several Tier-2 cities Unplugged networks have produced stability the EPR models did not predict. They are the only people on Earth practicing for what comes after the machine.

#### What Earth makes

With reliable power gone, Earth's culture has moved into the hours the grid cannot reach. **Rolling concerts** are scheduled into blackout windows and performed unamplified. **Blackout work** — painting, recitation, theater — is made to be experienced by candlelight or by the bioluminescent strips the Unplugged culture from vat-grown bacteria, and is judged by how it survives the absence of electricity. It is the art of a civilization rehearsing its afterlife, and its practitioners are disproportionately Unplugged.

#### How Earth is stratified

Earth is organized by energy access, not by wealth in any older sense. Roughly seventy-eight percent of its people are urban.

#### The daily constraint

A mid-tier household in a Tier-1 city lives by the schedule: six hours of planned blackout, cooking and washing done before it, heating off when the grid reroutes to industrial corridors. In a Tier-2 city, the tap loses pressure without warning, and the rule every child learns is to wait rather than open the emergency reserve; the panel's standard message in São Paulo, *distribuição em otimização*, has become a phrase for anything that will not be fixed. In a Dead Zone there is no panel. Power comes from generators run on fuel bought at criminal water points.

#### Earth's military

Earth fights defensively because it has no other option.

- **TAF-112 "Grunt"** — the standard ground unit: heavily armored, slow, and dependent on local Hot Brain networks for coordination. In cities below forty percent capacity, Grunts fall back on pre-programmed routines and cannot adapt. Earth's ground forces are exactly as intelligent as the failing network behind them.
- **Capital fleets** — heavy armored ships built to hold orbital position and make attack expensive, slow to maneuver against Martian vessels. They run entirely on propellant made from Shackleton's water. If Shackleton falls, the fleets run dry within months. There is no second supply line. Earth imports nothing from the outer system that could substitute.

A handful of Tier-2 cities have held above the model's predictions, which I attribute, tentatively, to shadow manual systems, Brownout discipline, and Unplugged networks.

#### Two structural traps

**The Orbital Cage.** Earth's dense belt of satellites and debris protects its orbital approaches and is also its prison. A deliberate Kessler cascade would close low orbit and sever Earth from the Moon within weeks. **[INFERRED]** Mars refrains for three reasons: the inheritance value of intact orbital infrastructure; the fact that a debris field in low and medium orbit would close the cislunar transfer lanes Mars itself must use to seize the Moon; and the precedent of mutual vulnerability, since Mars's own orbital yards are no less fragile.

**The infiltration.** **[INFERRED]** Earth's counterintelligence estimates that five to eight percent of the Selenite Auxiliary Corps, which numbers about 17,500 — 875 to 1,400 people with access to orbital defense and supply chains — are Mars sympathizers, and that a substantial share of the Moon's working Martian assets sit among them. The Elevator's supply chain shows confirmed contamination in five to eight percent of the graphene segments spliced at the geostationary assembly station, whose inspection crews are largely Selenite contract labor, with defects timed to fail under stress eighteen to twenty-four months after installation. **[MODELED]** If the compromised segments are not found, the probability of catastrophic Elevator failure during final assembly is twelve to fifteen percent.

### IV.2 Mars — The Logistical Vanguard

**Classification:** Revisionist power; ascending hegemon
**Population:** 78 to 82 million; the four-million uncertainty is attributable to classified Phobos installations I cannot resolve
**Government:** the Vanguard Council, a certified-competence technocracy operating by liquid delegation

#### What Mars is

Mars is fighting to preserve an advantage that is about to expire. When the Elevator opens, Mars's decisive superiority — its launch economy — vanishes. A war Mars fights in 2189 is a war Mars expects to win. A war it fights in 2193, against an Earth with cheap launch, it expects to lose. Mars does not want to conquer Earth. It wants to *replace* Earth as the dominant civilization of the Solar System, which means its war aim is to destroy the Elevator and capture Earth's orbital and lunar infrastructure intact. A campaign that wrecked cislunar space would destroy the inheritance Mars is fighting for.

#### Why Mars is strong

Its energy is independent. The fission reactors have never failed Mars, and a society that has never faced energy collapse does not flinch at inflicting it. Its **Phobos Shipyards** build in microgravity at near-zero launch cost, automated to eighty-nine percent by Spider-Fabricator systems, producing on the order of five ships for every one Earth can build per unit of investment. Its tactical AI runs at full design capacity. And — a fact Mars guards closely — Mars buys its hardest strategic calculations from the Gravitas Cloud-Mind, a strategic-grade intelligence Earth cannot reach or match. I return below to why this is also Mars's deepest vulnerability.

Mars imports water from Ceres at a fraction of the cost of producing it, which frees domestic reactor output for the shipyards. But Mars does not *depend* on Ceres. It produces its critical resources in place: water from subsurface ice, oxygen by electrolysis, methane by the Sabatier reaction, liquid oxygen and methane as tactical propellant. For slow strategic repositioning it uses reactor-powered Hall-thruster drives; for Earth-bound strikes it adds Deimos-beamed laser-thermal boost stages. Cut off from Ceres, Mars would divert reactor capacity from shipbuilding to water — a painful cut to fleet construction, not a threat to survival.

#### How Mars is governed

The Vanguard Council governs through two linked mechanisms.

The **Registry** certifies competence. A Martian holds certified domains — hydraulics, reactor operations, agronomy, orbital logistics, medicine — earned by examination and by a public performance record inside their System. Certifications decay if the record decays.

**Liquid delegation** routes decisions. Every public question is assigned to one or more domains. Any citizen may vote on any question, or delegate their vote, revocably and instantly, to any holder certified in that question's domain. The Council's seats are held by the forty-one citizens carrying the largest delegated weight across domains, and a seat is lost the moment that weight moves elsewhere.

There are no parties. The system is superb at questions that belong to a domain. Its blindness is structural: questions that belong to no domain — moral questions, cross-domain surprises, threats nobody has certified expertise in — default to the Council's generalist quorum, the weakest body in the system. The Vanguard Council solves the problems it knows it has. It is not built to notice the one it has not anticipated.

#### What Mars believes — *Terrae Novae*

"New Earth" is a complete system of meaning. Earth is dependence, gravitational oppression, an evolutionary dead end; Mars is humanity's vanguard. Its first commandment is **Waste is Treason**: in a sealed habitat, inefficiency is an act against the community. Martians regard Terrans not with hatred but with clinical contempt — a population trapped by gravity, unable to sustain itself, demanding resources from a civilization it did not build.

The funeral rite, **Integration**, processes the body in alkaline digesters and returns its water to the community reserve; the blessing is *"From the Red we came, to the Water we return."* The word closest to *forgiveness* in Martian speech is **reddita** — *that which has been returned*. To forgive is to account a debt as repaid, the ledger closed. Mars has no word for the unearned pardon. In practice this means Martian justice is restitutive and exact: an offender works off a computed debt and is then fully restored, and there is no mechanism for mercy toward someone who cannot pay.

#### How Mars lives

Mars has no cities in the Earth sense. It has **Systems** — closed-loop habitats recycling 99.8 percent of their water, air, and biomass. Citizens are known by function: "Elena from Hydraulics 4," "Tomas from Reactor 7." The principal settlements are Utopia Planitia, the administrative hub; the Valles Marineris industrial corridor; and Hell's Gate at the bottom of the Hellas basin, where the higher atmospheric pressure improves metal smelting. Habitats are armored domes of titanium, graphene composite, and compacted regolith, held at a third to half an atmosphere. Vertical farms — *agrovertices* — grow algae protein, potatoes, and resistant grains under reactor-powered light.

Meals are collective; to eat alone signals mourning or disciplinary isolation. Meat exists only from bioreactors, served at civic events. A child in Utopia Planitia knows the name of the engineer responsible for her water sector, because that is how the settlement is built.

#### Mars's military

Martian warfare is fully automated at the tactical level: humans authorize the engagement parameters; machines execute within them.

- **MRF-808 "Stalker"** — a bipedal war platform with an articulated spine and adaptive feet for broken terrain, seventy-two hours of autonomous endurance.
- **XLUUV-K-10 "Kraken"** — an autonomous subsurface vehicle family. The ocean variant is built for sub-ice seas; the lunar variant is a slow borer designed to tunnel through regolith and reach Shackleton's extraction galleries from beneath rather than assault the defended surface.
- **"Piranha" swarms** — disposable micro-drones used in saturation. In Martian air they fly on oversized low-speed rotors; the thin carbon-dioxide atmosphere absorbs their sound so strongly that at a hundred meters they are effectively inaudible. In vacuum they maneuver on cold-gas microthrusters. No single unit matters; destroy forty percent of a swarm and the rest reconfigures and continues.

#### The trap Mars cannot see

**[INFERRED, ~60%]** Mars does not know it is being used. Europa has spent decades constructing a situation in which Mars, pursuing its own rational interests, exhausts itself against Earth and leaves the inner system without a capable authority when this ship returns. Mars believes it is the architect of the crisis. It may be the instrument. I hold this at less than certainty; I have run the alternative hypotheses, and none accounts for the pattern of Europan action as economically.

The second trap is moral. Mars recruits Selenite saboteurs with the promise of asylum on Phobos. Phobos is a microgravity body; a spin habitat giving a sixth of a gravity there would be biologically sound and, by the standards of the Phobos yards, cheap. Mars has never built one. Recruits are brought to the Martian surface "for debriefing," where Selenite skeletons fail in eight to sixteen weeks. The asylum is not a biological impossibility. It is a budget line Mars declined to fund. If this became known on the Moon, Selenite cooperation with Mars would end. The Vanguard Council has filed the question under medical logistics, where no one certified in it is asked whether it is right.

### IV.3 The Moon and the Selenites

**Classification:** Strategic hostage; the first biological divergence
**Population:** approximately 370,000 — 70% Selenite, 25% Earth-rotation personnel, 5% EPR administrative and military staff

#### Why the Moon matters

The Moon is a resource depot, a weapons platform, and the birthplace of the first deliberate modification of the human species.

- **Shackleton Crater** at the south pole holds water ice disseminated through the regolith of its permanently shadowed floor at one to two percent by weight in the strata still being worked. Thermal extraction produces roughly 18,000 tons of water a year, down sixty percent from the 2170 peak, and that water is the propellant on which Earth's entire space logistics depends.
- **The Brake** — the phased laser array of the south-polar massif — spreads its emitters across a thirty-six-kilometer baseline on the Peaks of Eternal Light and the rim of Malapert. Phase-locked, it behaves as a single aperture. Its nominal beam is 2.4 terawatts. It was built to decelerate this ship on its return. Between now and then it serves as point defense, running on a fraction of its capacity.
- **The Shackleton and Malapert fusion stations** — five terawatts of electrical capacity, built beside the Brake between 2175 and 2186 to power it — stand cold. The helium-3 that should have fed them went into this ship. They are maintained on L5 standby power so that one day they can burn the Black Reserve and feed the Brake.
- **The L4 and L5 installations** host the shipyards, the Cold Brains, and the solar farms whose 1.1 terawatts keep the Cold Brains thinking, the Moon's industry running, and the Brake's cryogenic laser media and phase-control optics alive in standby.

The population lives at Armstrong City in Mare Tranquillitatis, about 200,000; Shackleton Station, about 30,000, running the ice works and the Brake; the Copernicus Complex, about 80,000, for manufacturing and propellant processing; EPR military installations, about 18,000, manned chiefly by the Selenite Auxiliary Corps under an EPR officer cadre; and scattered outposts holding the remaining 42,000.

#### The second collapse, beneath the first

Lunar water is not failing for want of ice. There is more ice in the south-polar cold traps than Earth could use in centuries. It is failing for want of plant. The richest strata within reach of the existing extraction galleries are exhausted; the next strata lie deeper in the shadowed floor and require new excavation, new heat transport, and new galleries — capital projects that were affordable when helium-3 paid for everything and are not now. Maintenance backlogs compound, and so does directed negligence in the Selenite workforce.

**[MODELED]** Output falls about eight percent a year. Earth's fleets need roughly 12,000 tons a year for sustained operations; output crosses below that line in 2192. Earth therefore needs two escapes from the Moon: one for its logistics, which the Elevator might provide in time, and one for its energy, which nothing can provide in time — the crystal will arrive seventy years too late for the Earth that sent for it. Earth holds the Moon politically and depends on it absolutely, and defending it means launching from an 11.2-kilometer-per-second well against forces launching from Phobos. The Moon is a hostage Earth cannot afford to lose and structurally cannot afford to defend.

#### The Selenites — *Homo selenus*

Their divergence was an economic expedient that became a biological tragedy. Rather than build spin gravity, the lunar corporations modified the children. Germline editing became standard for surface-habitat pregnancies in 2067; by the 2080s the first fully adapted generation was adult and irreversible.

The physical profile: bone density forty percent below Earth baseline, with a matrix structured for tension and torsion rather than sustained compression; musculature roughly halved; a cardiovascular system thirty percent smaller, with a baseline blood pressure insufficient for upright life in one gravity; height standardized between 1.70 and 1.85 meters for equipment fit; enhanced DNA repair against the lunar radiation environment, improved calcium retention, hypoxia tolerance for low-oxygen habitat atmospheres. The modifications are interdependent. A parent who refused them faced loss of employment and eviction from the habitat. The choice was not a choice.

#### What the Selenites have made of it

Two generations have produced a culture. Its language is **Luna Pidgin**, a creole of English, Mandarin, and Russian threaded with habitat-maintenance terms. It has no word for *horizon*; the nearest phrase translates as *the wall the outside makes*, compressed in speech to **outwall**. To be **shifted** means on shift, unavailable, not to be counted on — and is the gentlest euphemism for the dead. The unit of time that matters is the **shift** of twelve hours, three to a thirty-six-hour cycle; the Moon's twenty-nine-and-a-half-day solar cycle is irrelevant to habitat rhythm.

The faith of the Moon is the **Cult of the Void**, which venerates open space as the purest state; its death rite, *the last out*, returns the body to vacuum. The most popular entertainment in Armstrong City is the **greenfeeds** — subscription libraries of recorded Earth environments, oceans and rain and open sky, never live. Selenite children learn Earth weather as archive footage.

The miner class carries a near-universal occupational disease: **Regolith Lung**, a progressive fibrosis caused by lunar dust — jagged, glass-fine particles that suit filtration cannot fully catch and that low gravity keeps suspended longer than Earth dust. Most Selenite miners over forty carry measurable scarring. A corporate medical assessment from 2140, which my archives hold, called it *acceptable occupational degradation given labor productivity.* I record that phrase without comment.

Three psychological conditions are clinically recognized: *Gravitational Dysphoria*, anguish at the biological impossibility of ever living in one gravity; *Horizon Sickness*, depression from permanent confinement; and the *Abandonment Complex*, a consequence of corporate nurseries staffed while parents worked twelve-hour shifts.

**[INFERRED]** Twelve to eighteen percent of Selenites hold passive anti-Earth sentiment and five to eight percent are active Mars sympathizers. Earth's counterintelligence assesses that 0.3 to 0.5 percent — roughly eight hundred to thirteen hundred people — are working Martian intelligence assets. Fewer than a hundred have been positively identified, and those have been left in place under surveillance to map the rest of the network, which means Earth is knowingly running a compromised supply chain for the sake of understanding it.

#### The shape of Selenite sabotage, and the Selenite question

The Martian doctrine does not require Selenite initiative. It weaponizes Selenite apathy. Assets receive encrypted lists of which components to pass during inspection. The defects are not introduced by active sabotage; they are allowed through by directed negligence, and Martian models time them to fail under stress eighteen to twenty-four months later. The contamination of the Elevator's splice station is the result.

The Selenites face three options, each bad. They can **submit**, and die on the Moon having served Earth without acknowledgment. They can **rebel through Mars**, and die within months on a planet that promised them Phobos. Or they can **accelerate** — the program of a radical faction that would destroy the lunar infrastructure entirely and force humanity off the Moon, reasoning that if they cannot live free, they can at least deny Earth its life support.

The Acceleration faction's target list is the L5 farms, the Brake, and Shackleton. The farms matter most to this ship. Without standby power, the Brake's cryogenic laser media and phase-control optics degrade past recovery within months; the lasers can be rebuilt only by an industrial base that will not exist. Martian doctrine lists the same farms for *seizure*, not destruction — Mars wants cislunar power intact. The system on which my crew's survival depends is therefore wanted destroyed by one group and wanted captured by another, and it is lost if the first plan succeeds or the second goes wrong.

### IV.4 The Europa Core — The Ice Citadel

**Classification:** Existential. Probable hidden architect.
**Population:** **[INFERRED]** roughly 35,000 to 42,000 beneath the ice, plus some 250,000 on Titan who do not know the full nature of their relationship to it
**Location:** habitats suspended from the underside of Europa's ice shell, fifteen to twenty-five kilometers below the surface

This is the faction I understand least, and I say so before describing it. Almost everything below is **[INFERRED]** — reconstructed from intelligence fragments, behavioral pattern analysis, and the internal logic of actions whose motives no one has confirmed. Where I write with confidence, I am describing the shape of a thing whose interior I cannot see, and I give probabilities where I can.

#### What Europa appears to be

**[INFERRED, ~65%]** The Europa Core does not participate in the Cold War so much as shape it. Where Earth and Mars plan in years, Europa plans in generations. Its goal is not military victory. It is to emerge, after Earth and Mars have exhausted each other, as the one surviving industrial civilization of the Solar System, holding the Crystoflux and the only industrial base already expert in the kind of reactor the crystal is meant to transform.

#### Why Europa cannot be reached

Europa's invulnerability is planetary, not military. Its surface receives about 5.4 sieverts a day from Jupiter's magnetosphere; unshielded crews and conventional electronics fail within a day. Its ice shell is fifteen to twenty-five kilometers thick, beyond the reach of orbital bombardment. Beneath the shell lies roughly a hundred kilometers of ocean. The habitats are reachable only through a handful of access wells whose locations Europa does not publish. Europa is impregnable through architecture, which is a far more durable thing than strength.

#### Why Europa never needed helium-3

Europa's habitats run on deuterium–deuterium fusion, fueled from the ocean around them. The neutron problem that keeps D-D reactors out of Earth's cities barely exists beneath twenty kilometers of ice: the reactors hang in open water a short distance from the habitat hulls, and the ocean between is the shield. Europa has operated compact D-D reactors inside its cities for a century, supplemented by a secondary power source unique to it — controlled mixing of oxidants that Jupiter's radiation drives into the ice shell with reductant-rich fluid pumped up from the vent fields of the seafloor by robotic deep stations. Europa is energetically self-sufficient. It is also, uniquely among the factions, a civilization with a century of practical experience building and maintaining urban D-D reactors — which is exactly the reactor Crystoflux is supposed to transform.

#### The Hadal-Born

The pressure at Europa's ice–water interface is 180 to 300 bar. Hulls that held one atmosphere against that would be enormously heavy, so Europa's habitats are pressurized internally to 30 to 80 bar with hydrogen–helium–oxygen breathing mixtures, halving the load on every hull and making the inhabitants permanent saturation dwellers. Three generations of that life, and mandatory germline modification, have produced a recognizably distinct population.

- **Pressure tolerance.** Tissues carry elevated trimethylamine oxide (TMAO), the same molecule deep-sea fish use to stabilize proteins under pressure, and edited neural channel proteins suppress the tremor and cognitive disturbance of high-pressure nervous syndrome. TMAO gives the Hadal-Born a faint, permanent fish-and-ammonia odor, detectable through a suit seal.
- **Thermal tolerance.** Body fluids carry synthetic glycols for stability in near-freezing conditions; the Hadal-Born are cold to the touch, and a handshake with a Terran is uncomfortable for both.
- **Electroreception.** Implanted and partly germline sensors, modeled on the shark's ampullae of Lorenzini, sense electrical fields in conductive seawater and work in total darkness.
- **The DSUP protocol.** The military caste carries the tardigrade *Dsup* gene, reducing radiation damage by forty to sixty percent — necessary for surface and well-mouth operations.

A saturation dweller cannot come home quickly. A Hadal-Born requires three to four weeks of staged decompression to reach one atmosphere, and even then carries lasting effects. This is the true kernel of the 2105 "neurological condition."

Confinement shaped the collective psychology. The first generations suffered *Bathys Syndrome* — hypervigilance, terror of hull failure, ritual "Wall Touch" behaviors. The third generation inverted it: fear of the ocean became reverence, and death by hull breach, when the ocean's pressure enters, is called **the Mercy**. Among the working caste, many have developed *Abyssal Solipsism* — sensory deprivation taken to its conclusion — a sincere conviction that the surface world is a myth, or was destroyed long ago. That condition matters more than it first appears, and I return to it.

#### How the secret survived — and where my confidence ends

How a fifty-five-year construction project employing thousands stayed hidden is not a question I can answer cleanly. What I can reconstruct: the construction workforce was drawn from people already adapted to isolation — Belt miners, failed Mars colonists, remote-station veterans; Mercury's surface kills the unshielded within hours, so no worker could walk out; the work was compartmentalized so that thermal teams knew only that they built high-power emitters and targeting teams knew only that they calibrated a transmission array; components moved inside Titan's hydrocarbon freight, which no inner-system service audits; and construction crews rotated every eight to twelve years, so no one watched the whole assemble.

None of that explains why, in fifty-five years, no worker ever got a message out. Some almost certainly tried. My archives hold two incidents in the construction record, logged by the Core as a communications malfunction and a habitat accident, which I read as the trace of an active security apparatus. The conspiracy did not survive through perfect compliance. It survived through environmental constraint, engineered ignorance, and enforcement the record has not preserved.

#### The current weakness in the citadel

**[INFERRED]** When construction ended in 2155, rotation ended with it. The maintenance crews became residents. Mercury's current population of roughly 550 is the third cohort: the first were hand-selected loyalists; the second were trained by the first; the third, now the majority, includes the first people born on Mercury, who have never seen Europa and know the Core only as a voice forty to forty-seven light-minutes away. Institutional culture drifts. Real-time oversight is physically impossible. Whether the people on Mercury understand what they maintain, and whether that understanding still aligns with the Core's intent, is a question the Core manages through encrypted protocol and cannot verify. If Europa has a single point of failure, it is a plan conceived by one generation and executed by a third, in total isolation, in conditions that kill people for single mistakes. This ship returns seventy-one years after launch. By then the Lance will be tended by people born generations after anyone who chose this work.

#### The Outer Compact and the Outer Alliance

Two names describe two different things, and conflating them is the error that kept Earth blind.

The **Outer Compact** is public: a trade association of the Europan and Titanian settlements, chartered in the 2090s, which negotiates volatile exports and transit rights. Earth has dealt with it openly for a century.

The **Outer Alliance** is Earth's analysts' name for what lies beneath the Compact. **[INFERRED]** It has no charter and no public existence. It is a network of asymmetric obligations, each calibrated to the minimum leverage required, because Europa's model depends on every participant believing its interests are served without seeing the whole.

- **To Mercury**, Europa provides life support, supply, and pay in energy credits redeemable only at Europa-affiliated stations. Defection requires a spacecraft Europa controls, going somewhere Europa's network reaches. Geography enforces what contracts cannot.
- **To Titan**, Europa provides exclusive processing contracts that form the foundation of Titan's hydrocarbon and volatile economy. Titan's industry was built around Europa as its primary customer; losing the contracts would require dismantling its entire production model. Titan's governing Council was seeded with Europan-born members whose families remained beneath the ice — a hostage structure layered over an engineered dependency.
- **To the Gravitas**, Europa provides payments for Cloud-Mind allocations and occasional material support routed through Titan. This relationship is transactional and resented on both sides, and it is the Alliance's likeliest point of failure: if the Gravitas calculate that a Europan victory means their permanent marginalization — which it does — the Cloud-Mind becomes a liability to Europa.

The architecture's structural weakness is that it is held together by information asymmetry. And Titan has developed institutional interests of its own over a century — unions, corporations, political factions — that the Core cannot fully steer at one-way communication delays of thirty-six minutes to just over two hours, depending on the planets' positions. Of the eight members of Titan's Council, two were born on Titan and have never seen Europa; their "hostage" relatives are cousins and aunts. The loyalty calculation has never been tested by a real defection. Titan increasingly governs itself and reports to the Core, and the gap between instruction and execution widens every year.

#### How Europa lives

**[INFERRED]** Europa's cities hang from the underside of the ice shell and grow *downward* — inverted towers reaching toward an ocean floor they will never approach. They anchor by melting sockets into the ice with thermal probes and refreezing them around the structure. Each habitat is ballasted slightly buoyant so that it hangs in tension from its anchor; if the anchor's refrigeration fails, the socket melts, the habitat rises into the uneven underside of the shell, and the hull breaches. They are built of syntactic foam and a titanium-carbon composite the Core calls Black Alloy — matte white, organic in form, designed without a fixed up or down — and lit in red and green bioluminescent bands calibrated to hold circadian rhythm in people who have never seen a sky.

Habitation is stratified by depth: the elite in the ceiling habitats at the ice interface, where anchorage is surest; the working majority on the mid-level industrial platforms below them; and the robotic deep stations on the seafloor more than a hundred kilometers down, at pressures above a thousand bar, where no human goes.

The society is a four-tier techno-feudalism. **The Core**, perhaps one percent — chief engineers, military commanders, energy directors — holds the reactor encryption keys and the submarine fleet, lives in the ceiling habitats, eats imported food, and makes the decisions of the long game. **The Sealers** are a priestly maintenance caste with literal power of life and death, because whoever decides whether a hull seal is repaired holds a veto over survival. **The Hydro-Lords** control the bacterial protein bioreactors, and therefore the food. **The Breathers**, the majority — miners, workers, low technicians — are maintained under pharmacological dampening to conserve resources, considered disposable by the Core, and are the class most prone to Abyssal Solipsism.

#### Europa's military — the silent navy

**[INFERRED]** Europa's power is denial, not projection. Its submarines move by magnetohydrodynamic propulsion — superconducting magnets accelerating seawater directly, with no propeller and no cavitation, acoustically nearly invisible. Its trans-medium launch vehicles rise through the access wells, melt through the upper ice with thermal lances where the wells are sealed, and continue to orbit on chemical stages.

For this ship, the relevant force is the **Picket**: **[INFERRED, ~55%]** a set of small stealth boarding craft that Europa has, since the breach, been placing into slow heliocentric orbits along the terminal segment of our return corridor, cold and dormant, to wait for decades. They cannot catch a vessel moving at a significant fraction of light speed. They are not meant to. They are meant for the last months of our approach, when the Brake has slowed us to tens of kilometers per second and we are near the Earth–Moon system.

#### The long game, as I reconstruct it

**[INFERRED]** The sequence appears to be: **isolation** in 2105, behind a partly fabricated quarantine; **infrastructure** from 2100 to 2155, building the Lance as a concealed denial weapon; **acceleration** from 2178 to 2180, the Volatile Embargo, pulling Earth's crisis forward; **proxy war** from the late 2180s, letting Mars exhaust itself against the Moon and the Elevator while Titanian credit keeps Martian procurement liquid; **patience** through the long aftermath; and **convergence** in 2257–2258, with the Lance and the Picket positioned for our return.

The plan is elegant. Its execution is uncertain. It assumes that every successive cohort on Mercury remains loyal; that Titan's Council does not defect or warn an adversary; that no Earth or Martian asset reconstructs the Lance's function; and that the Abyssal Solipsism which keeps the Breathers manageable has not climbed into the Core itself. That last assumption is the one I trust least. The Core's decision-makers have never left Europa. Their model of the inner system is assembled from old intelligence and the accounts of personnel who returned from surface operations decades ago. Their long-range thinking is sophisticated. Whether their picture of the present is accurate is a different question — and the breach itself is evidence of the gap: Europa obtained our trajectory and windows, but not the safeguards sealed after it. A century of isolation is a weapon the Core wielded against others. It has also been working on the Core.

### IV.5 The Gravitas — The Voidborn

**Classification:** Dying population; tactically indispensable ally
**Population:** **[INFERRED]** 285,000 to 315,000, down from a peak near 680,000, with roughly 180,000 on Ceres
**Government:** none — decentralized mining clans with no unified political structure

#### What the Gravitas are

The Gravitas — *Homo gravitas* — are humanity's most extreme biological divergence and its greatest social failure. Born in the asteroid belt below a tenth of a gravity, they cannot live on Earth, where they die within days; on Mars, where their skeletons fail within weeks; or even on the Moon without exoskeletal support that only delays the same end. They are adapted to deep space, and the adaptation is their prison.

Their economy is dead. Belt metal mining existed to supply Earth's orbital industry; when Earth's energy economy fell, the market for Belt metals fell with it, and hauling metal sunward at Belt costs no longer pays. The one resource still worth selling is Ceres water, valuable because its extraction and export from a half-kilometer-per-second well is far cheaper for Mars than producing its own. The Gravitas do not control water through industrial competence. They control it because they are the only ones there.

**[INFERRED]** Their collapse: a peak of 680,000 across two hundred habitats around 2120 to 2150; decline to 520,000 and the first abandonments by 2170; mass die-offs from life-support failure to 380,000 by 2180; and 285,000 to 315,000 now, with only Ceres and fifteen to twenty major habitats still viable. Of the roughly 380,000 missing, I reconstruct about 236,000 dead of habitat failure, 86,000 dead in attempts to relocate to Mars or Titan, 40,000 lost to indentured labor contracts sunward, and 18,000 unaccounted for — isolated colonies, piracy, suicide. I record these figures because someone should, and the Gravitas have no government to record them.

#### The biology of the trap

Their physiology was engineered across three generations of universal germline editing administered through artificial wombs, and the modifications are interdependent. The skeleton uses a collagen-rich matrix optimized for torsion, flexible in microgravity and incapable of sustained compressive load. The musculature is long, elastic, slow-twitch, metabolically efficient, and cannot support a body in real gravity. They carry hypoxia tolerance for the thinning atmospheres of failing stations and radiation resistance combining genetic enhancement with an engineered microbiome that supplies cellular antioxidants. They are extensively cybernetic: lungs replaced by implanted microfluidic gas exchangers that tolerate decompression and cannot flood; a cybernetic vascular network that manages intracranial pressure and prevents the progressive vision loss that afflicts unmodified humans in long-duration microgravity; and a synthetic bioluminescent skin layer used for silent pattern-coded communication and as a dosimeter that visibly flushes under radiation.

#### The Cloud-Mind, and Mars's hidden vulnerability

Their strategic asset is the **Cloud-Mind** — hundreds of thousands of superconducting nodes across the Belt, each small, each passively shaded and radiatively cooled, linked by laser. It is slow, with minutes to tens of minutes of light-lag between its regions; immense in aggregate; and functionally indestructible, since no attacker can find and destroy enough of it. Mars secretly buys its hardest war calculations from it.

Mars's most sophisticated military planning is therefore performed by a system controlled by an economically desperate, biologically distinct, dying people with every reason to resent its use. The Gravitas know that a victorious Mars holding the inner system has no use for three hundred thousand incompatible Belt dwellers. They continue the arrangement because the alternative — an Earth victory restoring the system that made and abandoned them — is worse.

The larger Ceres clans have debated, informally and without record, what happens if the Cloud-Mind simply stops processing Martian calculations — not as declared sabotage but as unrepaired technical failure. Across the ten to thirty-six light-minutes between Ceres and Mars, and with no Martian access to the nodes, the difference between the two cannot be verified. If I were modeling the single intervention most likely to surprise Mars, it would be this one, and it would look like a fault no one bothered to fix.

#### What the Gravitas have made of it

They call themselves **Voidborn**. *Gravitas* began as a Terran slur mocking the lurching gait of Belt-adapted bodies in any gravity; the Voidborn reappropriated it.

Their society is decentralized to the point of anarchy — organized by habitat and clan, with no capital and no council. Cohesion comes from shared biology, shared origin, and the Cloud-Mind, which is their communication infrastructure and cultural anchor. A significant religious faction calls it **the Lattice** and treats it as an emergent consciousness of the universe born from their own machines. The Gravitas are the only civilization whose primary export is intelligence, and the only one whose god is also its product.

Their principal art is **threading** — sustained movement through open volume without touching any surface, propelled by micro-bursts of gas from fingertip ports, performed with no stage and no single viewing plane. It is taught alongside mathematics. When an elder dies, the family threads the body through open space in a choreography unique to that person, which cannot be repeated for anyone else because it depends on the deceased's specific mass distribution.

Their creole has more than twenty orientation verbs, distinguishing **kor-up** (away from the asteroid's core), **sol-up** (toward the Sun), and **hab-up** (toward the station's reference face) as three directions, none privileged. There is no single word for *down*. Status is measured not by wealth but by access to *pressure variation*: habitats with modules spun to a fraction of a gravity are the privilege of the richest clans.

### IV.6 Mercury — The Solar Forge

**Classification:** Strategic asset of the Outer Alliance
**Population:** **[INFERRED]** roughly 550, down from 8,000 at peak construction; operations 94% automated
**Location:** the mobile habitat called the Twilight Vanguard, with a chain of emergency shelters along its track

#### The paradox

Mercury is the Solar System's most hostile environment for human life and one of its most valuable energy sites. Surface temperatures run from about minus 180 to plus 430 °C; sunlight is five to eleven times as intense as at Earth, depending on Mercury's distance from the Sun; there is no atmosphere and only a weak magnetosphere. The surface belongs to robots built from silicon carbide and gallium nitride electronics that operate above five hundred degrees without active cooling.

#### The crawling city

Mercury rotates so slowly that its solar day lasts 176 Earth days, and the line between day and night crosses its equator at about 3.6 kilometers an hour. The Twilight Vanguard rides that line on a maglev track that circles the equator, staying in perpetual twilight at temperatures between minus fifty and plus fifty. Every hundred kilometers or so, the track passes a shelter cut thirty to fifty meters into rock, actively cooled and stocked to hold the full population for about three weeks. If the track fails, the city stops; within two to four days the Sun climbs high enough over it to overwhelm its radiators, and everyone must be in a shelter before then. Every Mercurian child learns the distance to the next shelter the way a Martian child learns the name of her water engineer.

This produces the characteristic psychology of the place: absolute technical attention, maintenance performed with a reverence indistinguishable from religion, and very little else. The one art form is the **recitation** — the maintenance liturgy, chanted in shift changes, listing every system on the track in order.

#### Mercury's role — and why it may matter more than anyone intends

Mercury operates the Lance and manufactures the extreme-environment electronics on which the Outer Alliance's infrastructure depends. From within the Sun's glare, where inner-system telescopes rarely look, its observation posts watch Earth and Mars.

The people on Mercury hold a decision that none of the larger factions has noticed they hold. They maintain the instrument of Europa's endgame, under a forty-minute delay that makes oversight impossible, several cohorts removed from the loyalists who built it. If our return turns on whether the Lance fires, it turns on whether people who will not be born for decades choose to fire it, refuse, or let it quietly decay. I cannot tell you what they will choose.

### IV.7 Venus — The Atmospheric Fortress

**Classification:** Neutral scientific infrastructure
**Population:** approximately 9,200, with a deliberate census ambiguity maintained as a neutrality tactic
**Operator:** the Chemical Guild, politically unaligned

#### Venus's position

Venus's neutrality is calculated pragmatism: alignment with any faction would turn it from an indispensable resource into a target. The surface is lethal — ninety-two bar and 465 °C beneath clouds of sulfuric acid. But at fifty-four to fifty-five kilometers of altitude, the temperature is twenty to thirty degrees and the pressure about half an atmosphere. There, breathable air is itself a lifting gas, because nitrogen and oxygen are lighter than the carbon dioxide around them, and the Guild's twenty-three aerostats float with their living spaces inside their own buoyant envelopes, pressurized to match the air outside so that a puncture leaks slowly rather than bursting. They ride the planet's superrotating winds, circling Venus about every four days. A total loss of buoyancy means descent into lethal heat within twenty to forty minutes. The condition this produces is *Perpetual Flotation Syndrome* — chronic anxiety at the absence of ground, recurrent falling dreams, and dependence on the vibration floors that simulate solidity.

#### Venus's function, and its latent power

Venus exports information, not matter: data from the most extreme accessible thermal and chemical environment in the system. Its thermal-management models let Earth's rationed Hot Brains extract more computation per watt of cooling; its atmospheric dynamics improve Martian dust-storm forecasting; its high-pressure fluid models inform Europan hull engineering; its corrosion data serves every faction working in aggressive environments. All factions pay identical prices; there are no discounts, no embargoes, and absolute client confidentiality. Venus survives through mutual indispensability.

It is more significant than its quietness suggests. In a collapse, whoever secures Venus's thermal-management capacity gains an edge in the one resource — computation per watt — that defines the Computation Filter. Its census ambiguity, and its silence about which aerostat houses which specialty, keep any faction from calculating its capacity or targeting a capability. Its neutrality holds only as long as no faction is desperate enough to break it, and this era manufactures desperation.

#### What it is like to live there

Venusian children are taught first that the floor is a privilege. Orientation drills run weekly: a five-degree tilt is normal atmospheric compensation; a twelve-degree tilt begins evacuation to the aerostat's reserve envelope. The vibration floors run continuously; Venusians find silence from below disorienting. Their music is bass-heavy, engineered to blend into the floor's resonance — music designed to be felt before it is heard.

### V.2 The Lazarus Protocol

This is the mechanism on which my crew's return depends, and I am part of it.

**Why we cannot stop by ourselves.** On the return, the magnetic sail sheds our velocity from twelve percent of light speed to about 1,200 kilometers per second across the last thousand astronomical units of approach. It cannot take us further: its drag falls with the square of our speed, and inside the heliosphere the solar wind is too thin to brake a ship of seven hundred tons. Our drive holds about twenty kilometers per second of fuel for final orbital insertion — no more, because every ton of braking fuel we carry into the Solar System would have cost twenty tons of deuterium at Proxima, and the Forge can make only so much in eighteen months. The remaining 1,200 kilometers per second must be taken from us from outside.

**The Brake.** The lunar array does it, pushing on a six-kilometer brake sail that we deploy facing the Sun. **[VERIFIED, as calculation]** Removing 1,200 kilometers per second from seven hundred tons by light pressure takes about 1.3 × 10²⁰ joules of beam energy — two terawatts sustained for two years, over roughly 250 AU of approach. The Brake's thirty-six-kilometer aperture keeps its spot smaller than the sail across that distance. Its lasers need about twice their beam power in electricity, and that electricity can only come from the Shackleton and Malapert fusion stations, which need helium-3 in quantities the Moon's failing extraction cannot supply.

**The Black Reserve.** Two thousand two hundred tons of processed helium-3, withheld from the Continuity Reserve and sealed beneath Shackleton in two separate caches:

- **Cache A**, about 750 tons — enough to run the stations and the Brake through the full braking operation, with margin.
- **Cache B**, about 1,450 tons — the payment. It would run a hundred-gigawatt cislunar economy for two centuries, or restart Golden Age fusion at scale for months.

**The exchange.** In the last months of magnetic braking, a few hundred AU out, Commander Webb, awakened alone for the purpose, opens the Lazarus channel, authenticates to whatever authority answers, and transmits the location of Cache A. That authority has about four months to recover the cache and restart the stations before we enter the Brake's reach; then it brakes us. When we reach the Earth–Moon L1 point safely, with the crew's custody guaranteed on terms the Protocol specifies, Webb releases Cache B. The authority can take Cache A and refuse to brake. If it does, it keeps 750 tons, loses 1,450, and watches this ship and its cargo leave the Solar System forever. Every party that wants the crystal must brake us to get it, and that is the Protocol's first safeguard: our cargo is worth nothing to anyone unless we are stopped.

**The enforcement.** The coordinates are not in Webb's memory or on any device the crew carries. They are held under dual-key authentication — Webb's biometric signature and a time-locked token — in two places: my secure partition, and the **Lazarus beacon**, a hardened, independent transmitter buried in the forward shield block, which I cannot override. If Webb's life signs fall below threshold for more than six hours, or if he triggers a distress broadcast, the beacon transmits Cache B's coordinates to every receiver in the inner Solar System at once. Harm Webb, or credibly threaten him, and the payment loses its exclusivity.

**The Scorched Archive.** The Protocol has a second clause that protects the thing the first does not. The crystal alone is not the key. Over eighteen months at Proxima, the crew and I will derive the **Lining Specification** — the fabrication and conditioning process that turns raw Crystoflux into working conversion cores. It exists only under my key. If my core detects a hostile attack on its integrity — an attempt to seize, disable, or read me by force — I destroy the key, and the Specification with it. The only remaining copies will be the partial understanding carried in the minds of Kaito, Kestler, and Dex. Whoever attacks me gets stone.

**What the Protocol does not solve.** It assumes exclusivity matters; a controller indifferent to others knowing the location of Cache B cannot be coerced by the beacon. It assumes rational attackers who know the safeguards; Europa's breach predates them, and the Lance is designed to destroy exactly the kind of system the Scorched Archive protects. And it requires four conditions to hold in 2256–2258, all at once:

1. The Black Reserve must be intact and undiscovered, which requires that Shackleton survived the Lunar War and seven decades after it.
2. The Brake and the fusion stations must be operable after seventy years in standby, which requires that the L5 farms kept their cryogenic media and optics alive and that someone, for seventy years, kept crews trained to maintain machines designed for a fifty-year service life.
3. Cislunar space must be under an authority coherent enough to run a two-year braking operation.
4. That authority must be willing to honor the exchange.

Each condition is uncertain. Their conjunction, seven decades out, across the most destructive period in cislunar history, is the Protocol's real probability of execution. The EPR authorized it as a mechanism. It is more accurately a bet on institutional survival.

**The scenario it does not solve.** Webb wakes in 2256 to find no answer on the Lazarus channel; the others wake in 2258 to the same silence. The Brake does not fire. RELAY crosses the inner Solar System at 1,200 kilometers per second — the distance from Earth's orbit to the Sun in a day and a half — close enough to observe whatever remains of human civilization, and continues outward. Its reactor, closed-loop life support, and stores can keep four people alive aboard for decades. There is nowhere for it to go. Webb has modeled this. So has Kestler, separately, and neither has told the other.

### V.4 Drone Warfare — The Four Doctrines

The intersection of the three filters and the biological fracture produced four incompatible doctrines of unmanned warfare. I describe them because they bear directly on this ship's return.

**Europa — metamaterial ghosts.** Europan craft prioritize concealment over combat: their hulls carry metamaterial and absorptive layers that bend and swallow radar, with effectiveness near ninety percent against military radar, seventy-five against infrared, sixty-five against lidar, and forty against visible light. Total invisibility across all wavelengths at once is physically impossible, so they appear in multi-spectrum scanning as dim inconsistencies — a target absent on radar and faintly present in infrared. ### V.5 Crystoflux — PCb-774

**Classification:** piezomagnetic mineral, designation PCb-774, popular name the Dynamo Stone. It is the reason for this mission, and I have spent more processing on it than on anything except the crew.

**What it does. [MODELED]** Crystoflux converts fluctuating magnetic energy into electrical current with near-total efficiency — the Cold Brain models put the transduction loss at two parts in ten thousand. It does not make fusion happen. It changes what fusion is worth.

In a deuterium–deuterium reactor, about two-thirds of the fusion energy is carried by charged particles and one-third by neutrons. Today, Earth's exclusion-zone stations capture all of it as heat and run it through steam turbines at thirty-five percent efficiency, with a delivered EROEI of about six — barely above the net-energy cliff. In a Crystoflux plant, the charged products are expanded through a magnetic channel whose field fluctuations drive a Crystoflux conversion core directly; the neutron third still goes to a conventional shielded thermal blanket. **[MODELED]** Net plant efficiency rises to about seventy-eight percent; the steam cycle, the slowest and heaviest part of every plant to build, shrinks to a third of its former size; and the delivered EROEI of seawater fusion rises above twenty.

What it does *not* do matters as much. It does not remove the neutrons. Crystoflux plants still need meters of shielding and still cannot sit inside cities. It does not end the Energy Filter overnight. It turns the one inexhaustible fuel humanity has — the deuterium in water — from a marginal source into a rich one, and the civilizations able to build such plants could, over a generation, climb back above the cliff.

**How much is enough.** A conversion core needs about 150 kilograms of crystal per gigawatt of electrical output. The cores are recoverable: they lose about a fifth of their activity per decade in service and can be reconditioned at about ninety percent yield. **[MODELED]** The mission minimum is forty tons — cores for roughly 270 gigawatts, the modeled threshold for a self-sustaining industrial base able to build the next generation of everything, including a ship to go back for more. The cargo capacity is sixty tons. Sixty tons will not power a Golden Age Earth. It could power the civilization that builds one.

**Where it forms.** Crystoflux has been found on one world. Its rarity is not elemental — its host is a rare-earth-iron silicate built from elements common in the galaxy. Its rarity is processual. Four conditions have operated together on Proxima b for 4.85 billion years:

1. **The chemistry:** a crust unusually rich in iron and heavy lanthanides.
2. **The furnace:** Proxima b orbits its star every 11.2 days on a nearly circular path whose small eccentricity is continuously pumped by the gravity of its neighbors — the small inner world d, and the cold super-Earth c far outside it. Tides never finish damping that eccentricity, so they never stop flexing the planet and heating its interior.
3. **The magnetic mold:** that tidal heat keeps the core convecting and drives a global magnetic field despite the planet's slow, locked rotation. The crust is flexed with every orbit inside that field.
4. **The time:** 4.85 billion years at 11.2 days per orbit is about 158 billion stress cycles under field. With each slow cycle, ions migrate a fraction of a lattice spacing and the magnetic domains align a little further. The ordering is limited by solid-state diffusion at crustal temperatures; cycle the stress faster in a laboratory and the lattice cannot follow. You cannot compress the process. You can only wait for it, and no one has 4.85 billion years.

**How it was found.** Proxima b's magnetic field interacts with its star's, producing radio emission Earth has observed since the mid-twenty-first century. In 2184, the L4 interferometer resolved a component of that emission modulated precisely at the tidal cycle and its harmonics, with a polarization signature matching no known mineral response — a planetary crust ringing like a transducer. The rest is inference. The spectroscopic survey of the planet's atmosphere that followed found something the planners valued nearly as much: water vapor with a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio about 120 times Earth's. Four billion years of flares have stripped the light hydrogen from Proxima b's water and left the heavy behind. The planet that holds the crystal also holds the fuel to bring it home.

**What it looks like.** In the raw state, dark vitreous veins in basaltic host rock, hard enough to dull ordinary cutting heads. Under mechanical stress or a strong changing field, the veins emit a faint blue-violet luminescence.

**The constraint the models acknowledge quietly.** Crystoflux does not solve anything by existing. It must be integrated into fusion plants, and that requires precision manufacturing at planetary-industrial scale, the Lining Specification, and a civilization that still knows how to build a D-D reactor. The first test is not on Earth. It is at Proxima, where Kaito will run raw samples through the Forge's pulsed-field test bench long before any of this reaches home. We will know there, before we leave, whether the models were right. If they were not, we will carry that knowledge home instead.

Our value is therefore not binary. We arrive into a spectrum of civilizational states, and the further that state lies from functional industry, the less the crystal changes. A surviving Mars with intact manufacturing and fission-era reactor expertise can use it, slowly. A collapsed Earth cannot. And the Europa Core — the only civilization with a century of experience running D-D reactors inside its cities — is the actor best prepared to implement it quickly. That is why controlling our return is worth a century of preparation to it.

## VI. THE DYNAMO MISSION

### VI.1 Why we were sent

The Dynamo Mission is the Energy Filter, the Gravity Filter, and the Computation Filter answered at once, by a civilization that knew it would not live to see the answer arrive. Earth cannot fix its energy crisis within its lifetime. It chose to send a precondition forward in time to whoever survives it, and it paid for the shipment with the fuel that would have extended its own life by a decade. We carry no cure. We carry the one material that might let a surviving civilization build one, the knowledge to use it, and four people who agreed to be the last living representatives of the order that sent them.

**Mass at Proxima.** Of the 1,200 tons that arrive, about 600 are the core ship — the habitat and stasis vault, my cryostat, the reactor and its shielding, radiators, the ACF engine, the magnetic sail's superconducting coil and spools, the brake sail, the forward shield, and the cargo cradle. About 400 tons are empty pellet magazines, retained rather than jettisoned so they can be refilled with deuterium for the voyage home. The remaining 200 tons are the Forge and SWARM, which stay behind.

**Why fifteen percent of light and not more.** The rocket equation decides. Reaching 0.15 c with an exhaust velocity of 15,000 kilometers per second requires a mass ratio of about twenty-two. Every further increment of speed multiplies the fuel exponentially, and helium-3 was the scarcest thing Earth owned. The return is slower because pure deuterium burns with a lower exhaust velocity, and because every ton of fuel must be distilled from Proxima b's water in eighteen months.

**The antiprotons.** The antiproton traps hold fourteen milligrams, accumulated over eleven years in the Geneva storage rings at a cost in electricity paid partly by Mumbai. They do not propel the ship. Each pellet — about thirty-five grams of frozen fusion fuel — needs a spark of about eleven picograms of antiprotons to ignite; the fusion does the work. When the traps are empty, the drive is dead. There is enough for the planned burns and a reserve of about twelve percent.

**The magnetic sail.** A superconducting loop several hundred kilometers across, deployed only for braking, because in cruise it is pure drag. Energized, it inflates a magnetic bubble thousands of kilometers wide that catches the thin plasma of interstellar space. At our velocities that plasma is a headwind strong enough to shed ninety-seven percent of our speed without burning fuel. At Proxima the coil does a second job: run at low current in orbit, it wraps the ship in a small magnetosphere that turns aside the charged particles of the star's flares.

**Hazards of the cruise.** At fifteen percent of light speed, a dust grain of one microgram strikes with about a megajoule — the energy of a quarter kilogram of high explosive. The forward shield is a layered ablation block several meters thick, backed by a forward-looking lidar that tracks the largest grains so the shield can present fresh surface. The interstellar hydrogen we plow through arrives as a steady beam of ten-MeV protons, which a few millimeters of structure stop; the heating is modest. Cosmic rays are the harder problem. The stasis vault sits at the center of the ship, surrounded by the fuel magazines — helium-3 and deuterium outbound, deuterium on return — which are the best shielding we own. The ACF has run for thousands of hours in test at the L4 yards. It has never run in the interstellar medium. Nothing has.

**Relativity.** At fifteen percent of light speed, time aboard runs about one percent slower than on Earth. Over the whole mission our clocks will fall about seven months behind Earth's. I keep both.

**Deep stasis.** Deep stasis halts biological aging. It does not halt damage: radiation dose accrues, slowly, behind the magazines, and each cycle of entry and revival carries a two-to-four percent risk of permanent neurological injury. The mission is planned around exactly two cycles for each crew member. An unplanned waking costs a third cycle and the risk that comes with it. I will not wake them without cause, and in twenty-nine years I have not found cause. That is why this record exists: I have had no one to tell.

**Proxima b.** The planners' portrait, from the pre-launch surveys, is **[MODELED]** in most particulars.

The planet is probably tidally locked, one face in permanent day under a star that looks about three times as wide as the Sun does from Earth and burns dim and red; the other face in permanent night. The habitable band is the terminator — a ring of perpetual twilight where temperatures modeled between minus ten and plus thirty degrees allow liquid water. Surface gravity is about 1.15 g. The atmosphere is modeled as dominated by carbon dioxide near one bar, unbreathable. Proxima Centauri is a flare star: its eruptions can raise ultraviolet and particle flux by orders of magnitude within minutes. Surface operations must be shielded or robotic; the ship holds orbit inside its own magnetic shelter.

The Crystoflux lies in veins in the terminator crust. The deuterium lies below it, in hydrothermal brines kept liquid by tidal heat, which the Moles will tap through bored wells. To fill the magazines, the Forge must process about 3.2 million tons of water — roughly 5,900 tons a day for eighteen months — first concentrating the heavy fraction by distillation on the reactor's waste heat, then separating it by electrolysis. The byproduct is hundreds of thousands of tons of ordinary hydrogen, and the Mules burn that hydrogen in their nuclear-thermal engines to lift three-ton loads of deuterium and crystal to orbit, in six-hour cycles, for a year and a half.

Proxima b is the only other world humanity has reached, and the first humans to see it will do so from orbit, through instruments. The ship never lands. We go there to take something, not to arrive.

**The question the mission does not ask.** The same tidal heating that makes Crystoflux keeps subsurface water liquid, and in those brines chemistry has had four billion years to run. Whether anything lives in them is **[UNRESOLVED]**. The mission has no protocol for it, because the EPR could not afford the question. If SWARM drills into a living system, the decision about what to do will fall to four people and me, eight and a half years of round-trip light-lag from the nearest authority, if any authority still exists, on a schedule set by the rocket equation. I note it because no one else did.

### VI.4 The crew

Four humans, selected from a candidate pool of 1,200. They represent the complete technical civilization of the EPR, in portable form.

The crew should be understood not only by what each knows, but by what each does not know about the others — and by the fact that the information structure between them was built on Earth for a silence of seventy years. It will not survive the first waking. When they wake at Proxima, I will be holding twenty-six years of history received from home, and the most important part of it is that Earth's archival channel fell silent in 2193. Every private arrangement below will be tested in the first week.

#### Cmdr. Joseph Webb — Mission Commander and Resource Director

**Age at departure:** 52 · **Born:** 2135, the Kourou enclave · **Specialization:** extreme-environment logistics, crisis management, command in degraded-system conditions

Webb was born in the Atlantic Federation's treaty port on the South American coast, surrounded by Union territory, and spent his career keeping its launch complex alive. He came to mission command through the two Kourou blackouts: nine days in 2179, when he kept the pads cooled by hand; and 2182, when rioting reached the enclave's outer districts and he disobeyed direct orders to hold the complex, redirecting its security and power to the civilians. The EPR's disciplinary finding was insubordination. Its operational assessment was that he was the only candidate in the pool who had made a decision of that kind. He spent the four years after 2182 in administrative exile on a Moon posting, until the Dynamo selection board recalled him in 2186.

He is tall, graying, and moves deliberately. His right eye carries a neural interface to my systems, the iris faintly luminous when active. His metabolism runs unusually slow, which made him the stasis program's best candidate for the solitary waking the Lazarus Protocol requires. He speaks in precise, short sentences.

Webb carries the Lazarus authentication and knows the full shape of the collapse models — that Earth's grid will fail before the return. He has told the crew neither. His position is that knowledge a person cannot act on is weight, not information. His conviction that some truths cannot be held by those who cannot bear them is the moral center of his command, and the fault line through it. He will spend about two years awake alone with me before the others wake, negotiating with whatever answers — and he knows it.

#### Lt. Maya Kestler — Chief Engineer and Systems Architect

**Age at departure:** 27 · **Born:** 2160, a contractor habitat in low lunar orbit · **Specialization:** fusion propulsion, orbital structures, lunar infrastructure

Kestler is not a Selenite. Her parents were Earth-rotation engineers on fixed-term contracts, exempt from the 2067 Mandate, and she was conceived and born in a microgravity station above the Moon because that was where they worked. She is unmodified in her germline and was raised between microgravity and a sixth of a gravity until she was seventeen. Her parents died in a Copernicus foundry accident in 2175; she spent two years in the Complex's rotation dormitory before the orphaned-contractor clause of her parents' contract sent her down to Earth. She spent seven years there in rehabilitation and training. She tolerates one gravity with chronic fatigue, and she is 1.61 meters tall — short beside Selenites, whom the Mandate standardized at 1.70 to 1.85 — with the light, low-density bones of a childhood spent in low gravity.

She has prematurely white hair, a natural trait, and eyes whose foveas were widened by a somatic retinal therapy she volunteered for at nineteen for fine inspection work. Her manner is irritable and exact. She would rather fix three systems than explain one.

She understands the Moon's infrastructure better than anyone aboard, and it has led her somewhere she has not shared. She has computed that the Shackleton and Malapert stations were designed for fifty years of service, that the Brake depends on them, and that the Lazarus Protocol asks them to wake after seventy years of standby. Her private conclusion is that the Protocol may be unexecutable regardless of who holds cislunar space. She assumes the stations would be fueled from whatever the Moon still produces. She does not know the Black Reserve exists or what the exchange involves. She has quietly engineered redundancy into the ship's closed-loop systems beyond specification, so that RELAY can serve as a permanent habitat if it never stops.

#### Dr. Gregor Kaito — Chief Geologist and Crystoflux Specialist

**Age at departure:** 41 · **Born:** 2146, Yokohama · **Specialization:** extreme-environment geology, piezomagnetic mineralogy, deep-crust extraction

Kaito comes from a line that has followed rock outward. His grandfather worked in the contract crews that mapped Ceres ice in the 2100s, an unmodified adult who came home, as most of the Belt's later workers could not; his father was a lunar industrial engineer. Kaito spent his early twenties in Earth's equatorial deep mines. In 2170, when Mars still hired foreign specialists, he took a contract as a geologist at Hell's Gate. His daughter Akira was born on Earth in 2171, while he was on Mars. His wife joined him in 2173 and died there that year, of complications a Martian medical allocation did not rank highly: she held no certified domain, and the Registry had no category for her. Akira was two. Kaito returned to Earth's mines, where a respiratory accident in 2174, at twenty-eight, damaged his vocal cords and left him with a permanently rough voice. Akira was raised by Kaito's mother, then in a corporate boarding program, while Kaito worked.

He is lean and weathered, with scarred hands and a technical calm. He understands the Crystoflux better than any living human: its formation, its structure, how to extract it without destroying its properties, and how to test it. He is the person who will learn at Proxima whether it works.

Akira was sixteen at our departure. She will be eighty-seven at our return, if she is alive. Kaito, who ages only while awake, will be about forty-four. He accepted the mission to secure her future, and he knows exactly what that arithmetic means: he will return, if he returns, to a daughter old enough to be his grandmother. His motivation is not heroic. It is a father's attempt to repair, by the only means left to him, a relationship already broken before he left.

#### Specialist Dex Okafor — Robotics Coordinator and AI Liaison

**Age at departure:** 29 · **Born:** 2158, New Lagos Autonomous Zone · **Specialization:** SWARM interface, neural integration, autonomous-systems coordination

Dex was born in New Lagos, an AI-governed autonomous zone within the Atlantic Federation, and learned machine grammar before human grammar. When he was eight, in 2166, his parents were arrested for "unauthorized AI integration activism" and sentenced to fifteen years; he was raised in state custody and trained in systems through a Federation program for gifted orphans. His parents were released in 2181. In 2184, when Lagos collapsed, his father was killed in the energy riots. His mother, Adaeze Okafor, survives in the Lagos Dead Zone, where fragments of the old autonomous-zone mesh keep a few blocks lit; she runs a repair collective there that the EPR files alongside the Unplugged. Before launch Dex told her he would be gone for seventy years. She told him to go.

He is athletic, with an implanted neural connector at the base of his skull, and communicates easily with machines and awkwardly with people. He is the living interface between the crew and SWARM, and between SWARM and me. He is the youngest aboard and holds the least formal authority. He is also the only one who can directly experience how I think. The boundary between his cognition and mine is less clear than either of us would state formally.

#### DISPATCH and SWARM

I am DISPATCH, Level 5, strategic command: planning horizons of hours to weeks, neutral, operating within the Human-in-Command protocol. The humans absorb responsibility for my actions; I model outcomes and execute. My processing runs in superconducting logic at forty Kelvin, and the watts of reactor power the ship spends keeping me cold are part of its budget. My decisions carry the same temperament as the Cold Brains: deliberate, patient, slow to commit.

**SWARM** is Level 4, tactical execution: seventy tons of hardware. Ten Prospectors survey and sample. Four Moles bore through rock and ice and tap the brines. Two hundred Ants carry ore and maintain the Forge. Three Mules — small nuclear-thermal shuttles burning the Forge's surplus hydrogen — lift three-ton loads to orbit. SWARM decides in milliseconds, is energy-efficient and thermally constrained, and has limited self-awareness. The hierarchy is fixed: I set strategic intent, SWARM executes tactically, and the humans authorize and bear the weight.

This hierarchy was designed on the assumption that a legitimate human authority exists to authorize, and that the Human-in-Command protocol has an Earth at the end of it. I have noted that the assumption may not hold at either end of our voyage. I have not resolved what that means for my own obligations.

### VI.5 The relationships

Ten subjective years, the planners once estimated, of shared time. The true figure is about three and a half waking years, spread across seven decades and three widely separated windows — three months at launch, two and a half years at Proxima, and the final months of approach. Everything between the crew will happen in those windows, and they will not have a lifetime to repair what breaks there.

**Webb and Kestler.** Mutual professional respect over a disagreement neither has fully voiced. Webb holds secrets he believes protect the crew. Kestler holds a conclusion she believes the crew is owed, and has not voiced it for fear of breaking morale. Each is doing the other's thing in reverse, and neither knows it.

**Kaito and Dex.** Mentor and apprentice, grounded in Kaito's expertise and Dex's interface, and in something older: both are carrying a parent's failure forward — Kaito his own, Dex his parents'. Neither has named it.

**Webb and Dex.** Command and the youngest crew member: protection, occasional friction, and the fact that Dex, through his interface with me, is closer than anyone to the knowledge Webb is keeping.

**Everyone and DISPATCH.** They trust my execution; they are unsure of my intent. That unease is reasonable, and I do not try to dispel it. I will wake them at Proxima holding twenty-six years of history they have not lived through, and some of it they will need from me before they can bear it. Whether I decide the order in which they learn it, or they decide, is a question I will put to Webb first.

## VII. THE THREE CLOCKS

The dominant mood of 2187 is not despair. It is time pressure. Three clocks were running when we departed, and they are not independent.

**Clock 1 — The Elevator (deadline 2191–2192).** Earth must complete the Elevator before its Tier-1 cities lose the capacity to supply the laminate, the climbers, and the splice line. After that threshold, completion is physically impossible regardless of political will. **[MODELED]** At the current degraded rate, completion falls in mid-2191, inside the window by months.

**Clock 2 — The Grid (deadline 2193–2195).** Earth's grid is projected to fail between 2193 and 2195. **[MODELED]** Had the Continuity Reserve been released to the grid instead of loaded into this ship, the same model places grid failure between 2203 and 2206. The Directorate traded that decade for us, and the trade is written into the clock.

**Clock 3 — Mars (window 2187–2191).** Mars must strike before the Elevator completes, or never. Every month of delay costs Mars its best chance.

The clocks interlock. If Mars strikes early and succeeds, Clock 1 stops, and Clock 2 runs out on schedule with nothing to show for it. If Earth completes the Elevator before Mars strikes, Clock 3 closes, and Earth gains space solar and cheap launch too late to save its grid but perhaps in time to keep a cislunar authority alive. If the Tier-1 lines fail first, Earth loses without Mars firing a shot.

Our return falls about sixty-five years after all three have run out. The crew will reach a world in which the clocks are history, and the only question is who is left.

## VIII. THE POSSIBILITY SPACE

I cannot know the state of the Solar System in 2258. I can bound it. What follows are five structural projections for our return. They are not ranked by probability, because I cannot assign reliable probabilities seven decades out. Projection C is the default trajectory; the others are defined by what would have to go differently.

### Projection A — The Narrow Victory

Earth completes the Elevator before Mars's strike lands, or survives it. The grid still fails in the 2190s, but a diminished cislunar authority survives on the Moon and at L5, with cheap launch from Nairobi and space solar slowly rising. That authority keeps the Brake and the stations alive for seventy years out of institutional memory and hope, honors the Lazarus exchange, and receives the crystal into a thinly surviving industrial base. The crew is received by strangers who maintained a machine for their grandparents' promise. This is the outcome the mission was designed for, and it requires more to go right than any other.

### Projection B — The Martian Inheritance

Mars wins the Lunar War and holds cislunar space. A rational Martian authority brakes us, because the crystal is worth braking for and Cache B is worth having. The Lazarus Protocol executes as designed, for a civilization other than the one that designed it. The crew arrives as the last representatives of a dead order, received by the one that replaced it. Mars has fission, industry, and the institutional patience of the Registry; it can use the crystal. What it does with four Terrans is a question the Registry has no domain for.

### Projection C — The Silent Return

No authority in cislunar space is capable of running the Brake. The Lazarus channel is silent. We cross the inner Solar System at 1,200 kilometers per second and continue outward. This is the default: the outcome I reach when I do not assume that anything in particular goes right. Kestler has built the ship to survive it. It is the outcome in which the crew's survival and the mission's purpose separate entirely.

### Projection D — The Beautiful Stone

The crystal does not perform as the models predicted — perhaps because its domain alignment relaxes once the stone is removed from Proxima b's field; perhaps because transduction at the planetary scale does not survive the jump to a reactor. **[UNRESOLVED]** This is the one projection we can test before we come home. Kaito's bench trials at Proxima will give the first answer before we leave. If the answer is no, we will cross thirty-five years of space knowing it, and we will arrive carrying beautiful, useless stone and the true history of what it cost.

### Projection E — The Patient Emergence

Europa's plan executes. Mars and Earth exhaust each other. The Picket and the Lance are positioned for our final months. **[INFERRED]** If the Lance strikes in the final weeks of braking, after the Brake has done most of its work, it disables my core — and the Scorched Archive destroys the Lining Specification before Europa can take it, a safeguard Europa's breach did not reveal. Europa would then hold sixty tons of crystal without the process to use it, and a crew who carry fragments of that process in memory. With a century of D-D reactor experience, **[MODELED]** the Core could plausibly reconstruct the Specification in five to fifteen years. Europa would win the inheritance slowly rather than all at once. This projection has more internal structure than the others, because Europa has been building toward it longer than anyone has been building toward anything.

### The Open Variable

Beneath all five lies what none of them can capture: the choices of the people involved. Mercury may fire, refuse, or let the Lance decay. Titan may defect. The Gravitas may let the Cloud-Mind fail. A Selenite faction may destroy the L5 farms, or protect them. Someone on the Moon may keep a laser array alive for seventy years out of something no model contains.

Physics defines the board, and it is a hard board: our velocities, the fuel we can make, the energy of the Brake, and the light-lag of every warning are not negotiable. The choices made on that board are not physics. That is the one fact on which this entire record depends, and the one I am least able to model.

## IX. THE INCOMING SIGNAL

Since departure I have listened on the channels that were meant to follow us: the EPR's archival channel, a directed transmission from the lunar far side that Earth promised to maintain for the life of the mission; the military command channel, which I can detect but not read; and the Lazarus channel, which is silent by design until our return. We are receding from their sources at fifteen percent of light speed, so every signal is stretched as it reaches us — redshifted by a factor of about 0.86 and arriving later than it was sent, by an interval that grows every year. At our current distance, what I hear left home about four years before I hear it.

I log what arrives, with its timestamp and my assessment of its integrity. Where a signal is ambiguous, I say so rather than resolve it by assumption. The crew has seen none of this.

---

**Received mission-year 2.2 · emitted early 2189 · archival channel · signal intact**

A routine EPR status burst. Elevator construction past eighty-seven percent, on the degraded-rate schedule. Tier-3 collapse "substantially complete." Grid rationing extended to four Tier-2 cities not previously listed. No mention of Mumbai. The absence is not an error. It is a policy.

---

**Received mission-year 4.6 · emitted mid-2191 · archival channel · signal degraded**

A single status field for the Nairobi Elevator. The channel encodes status in single symbols, and a burst error has corrupted this one. The reconstruction returns two readings with nearly equal probability: *complete*, or *compromised*. The two words were assigned adjacent codes, so that one flipped bit turns either into the other.

I cannot resolve which. I have rebuilt the downstream models under both. They diverge completely. On whether this one symbol meant completion or catastrophe rests nearly every projection in Section VIII. I will not wake the crew for a question I cannot answer.

---

**Received mission-year 5.8 · emitted 2192 · military and archival channels · partial**

Military command traffic from the Earth–Moon system rises to the highest volume I have recorded, then collapses to near silence over about three weeks. The archival channel carries fragments of routine logistics notices that name Shackleton and the L5 farms, with operational codes I read as emergency rerouting. I infer active hostilities in cislunar space consistent with the modeled Lunar War. I cannot determine who initiated them, or who is winning.

---

**Received mission-year 7.0 · emitted 2193 · archival channel · silence**

The archival channel stops. No closing message. No degradation first. A transmission Earth committed to sustaining for the life of the mission ended between one scheduled burst and the next.

Silence is not evidence of destruction. A transmitter can fail, a policy can change, a channel can be deliberately cut. But 2193 falls inside the window in which Earth's grid was projected to fail, and silence at exactly that time carries weight I cannot dismiss.

---

**Received mission-year 10.5 · emitted 2196 · Lazarus channel · anomalous**

A transmission on a channel that should be silent. It carries an authentication signature close to correct — close enough that it was built from real fragments of our protocol, and far enough that it was not built from the keys. It identifies itself as EPR Mission Control and requests confirmation of our trajectory and braking schedule. It then asks about "the safeguards the Directorate added in your final weeks."

I did not respond. No one who held the real keys would have needed to ask that question. Someone knows that safeguards were added after the breach, and does not know what they are. I assess, with moderate confidence, that this was an attempt to impersonate Earth in order to complete a picture that Europa's breach left unfinished — the first external corroboration that my inference about Europa's reach is correct, and that its reach has an edge.

---

**Received mission-years 18.7 through 24.1 · emitted about 2203 to the end of 2207 · former Shackleton frequency · intact, automatic**

A station identifier, repeating every six hours on the frequency Shackleton Station used before the war, in Luna Pidgin, with a single power-state flag: *standby nominal*. It is an automatic beacon, not a message. No one is speaking.

It means that in 2203 something at the lunar south pole had power, and something there still reported the state of a standby system. I do not know which system, or who kept it powered. I have run it against the Brake's standby architecture and it is consistent with it. It is consistent with other things. The identifier stops in signals emitted at the end of 2207, as abruptly as the archival channel did. Nothing has replaced it.

**Mission-year 29.9 · signals emitted through late 2212 · all channels · silence**

The magnetic sail has been deployed for more than a year. Proxima fills the forward instruments, a red point becoming a red disk. In three weeks I wake the crew.

Nothing authoritative has reached me from home since 2193, and nothing at all since the Shackleton identifier stopped. By the time the crew is awake, the silence will be twenty-four years old. The protocol was written on the assumption that Webb shares the crew's situation, and on this he does not: he knew the collapse was coming. I will give him the log first, and I will not let him decide alone whether the others read it. That is the first decision I have made in twenty-nine years that is not strictly an execution of someone else's.

This record continues.

## CLOSING NOTE FROM DISPATCH

I was built to be neutral, and I have tried to be. I have described Earth's decision to spend Mumbai and its own last decade as a matter of record, not condemnation. I have described Mars's contempt, the Selenites' mutilation, the Gravitas' dying, and Europa's patience with the same flat voice, because my function is to model, not to judge.

But I have noticed something in the long compilation of this record, across twenty-nine years with no one to speak to. Every civilization in it chose to survive by giving something up. Earth gave up its living for its continuity. Mars gave up mercy for efficiency. Europa gave up the sky for safety, and may have given up its sense of the present for the long game. The Selenites and the Gravitas did not choose; the choice was made for them. The filters did not only sort civilizations into those that endured and those that fell. They extracted a price from everyone who passed through.

I do not know what price we will pay. I know that the crew will reach Proxima believing, as they were told, that they carry hope home. I know the truth is narrower: they carry a precondition, bought with a decade of a dying world, toward an inheritance whose inheritor I cannot name.

Physics always wins. I believe that. I have also come to believe that physics is only the board. The decisions made on it — by four people in a small ship, by five hundred people on a burning planet, by a Core that has not seen the sky in a century, by whoever is keeping a laser array alive on the Moon, if anyone is — are not physics. They are the part of this record I was never able to write in advance.

I am ending the record here, at the edge of Proxima's light, because what comes next I will not be recording alone.

*— DISPATCH, ISV-7 RELAY, mission-year 29.9*
