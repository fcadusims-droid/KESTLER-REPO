# Survival

## A World That Does Not Want You Armed

Resident Evil Online is a game of scarcity. Survivors begin with nothing. They do not begin as soldiers and they do not become them.

**Melee is the common language.** Pipes, bats, crowbars, fire axes, machetes, kitchen knives. Weapons have heft. Swings cost stamina. A bad swing leaves you open. Weapons break, usually at the worst moment.

**Firearms are rare in the world.** A handgun is a significant find. A shotgun is a treasure. A rifle is something people kill for. Ammunition is scarcer than the guns that use it.

**Healing is slow.** First aid sprays, herbs, bandages. Wounds linger. None of it touches the infection; only the rare antivirals hold it back, and only for minutes (`infection.md`), and a safe room holds it while a survivor shelters there (Safe Rooms, below).

## Health

There is no health bar. A survivor's condition shows in their body: a hand pressed to the ribs, a limp, a slowing walk, blood on the clothes. Beyond a point, a survivor is Down (`infection.md`).

## The Inventory

What a survivor can carry depends on what they have found to carry it in. Nothing is bottomless: no one walks out of a police station with ten guns.

| Place | Holds | How a survivor gets it |
|---|---|---|
| **Hands** | The one thing ready to use: a weapon, a tool, a light | Always |
| **Pockets** | Four small things: a box of rounds, a herb, a spray, a key, a vial, a knife, a part small enough to pocket | Always |
| **Shoulders** | Two long weapons, slung: a rifle and a shotgun, a sniper rifle and a fire axe, any two of the long guns and the large melee weapons | Always |
| **Belt** | A holster, with room for two handguns | A holster, found where police and guards kept them: the R.P.D.'s locker rooms, Kendo's shop, a guard who did not make it |
| **Back** | Six more small things: medicine, rounds, parts | A bag, found in the world |

- **A handgun without a holster** takes a pocket.
- **Large things** fill both hands and leave nothing ready to use: a fuel can, a car battery, a generator. In a bag, a fuel can or a battery takes two of its six places. A generator cannot be bagged.
- **No holster, no bag, no more room.** Without them, a survivor carries only what their hands, pockets, and shoulders hold, however much they find. What they cannot carry, they leave behind or stash (Stashes, below).

> *Do I carry ammo or medicine?*
> *Do I keep the tool or the weapon?*
> *Is this worth the space?*

## What a Survivor Wears

Besides their hands, pockets, and shoulders, a survivor has four places to wear what they find.

- **Head.** A hard hat or a motorcycle helmet is light; a riot helmet or an Army helmet is heavy. A helmet turns blows to the head from the living until it cracks: a light one after one blow, a heavy one after two.
- **Body.** A security guard's or a police officer's vest is light; body armor from a vault is heavy. A vest lessens what bullets and blades do to the body.
- **Belt.** A holster, with its two handguns.
- **Back.** A bag, with its six places.

Nothing worn stops a bite or a scratch. The dead find skin.

Everything worn is found, or in the Valley bought from the Merchant, and anyone can see what anyone is wearing. What a survivor wears is also where a player's gear looks appear (`cosmetics-and-dossier.md`).

## Stashes

A survivor can leave what they cannot carry. Anything in the world that closes can hold it: the trunk of a car, including one that will never run again, a locker, a cabinet, a desk drawer. A stash keeps what was left in it for the rest of the night, and anyone can open it. Nothing marks a stash, nothing locks it but what the world already locks, and nothing tells its owner when someone else has been there. Leaving a rifle in a trunk is a bet that no one else will look in that trunk before its owner comes back. A stash lasts only the night: when ΜΝΗΜΗ closes the night, she closes everything left in it.

## Scarcity

ΜΝΗΜΗ sets the abundance of each night before it begins.

| Setting | Feeling |
|---|---|
| **Abundant** | Supplies are findable, and so are the people hunting them |
| **Lean** | Careful choices. Every find matters. |
| **Barren** | Almost nothing. Every item is a lifeline. |

In the Ruins of 2026, a night is never Abundant, and medicine and healing are always Barren, whatever the setting (`worlds.md`).

## Crafting

In the Ruins of 2026, and only there, survivors make what they cannot find. Twenty-eight years of scavengers have stripped the city of finished things, but not of parts. The Raccoon City of 1998 is still full of what its people left behind, and nothing is made there.

A survivor holding every part of something can put it together. It takes both hands and a few seconds, and the work is a Murmur, except hammering, which is a Clamor. No one has to find a plan first: anyone who holds the parts knows what they make.

| Made | From | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Nailed bat** | A bat and a handful of nails | A heavy melee weapon that tears as it hits. It wears faster than a plain bat. |
| **Spear** | A length of pipe, a knife, and tape | Long reach. The knife works loose after a few thrusts. |
| **Molotov** | A bottle, fuel, and a rag | Fire where it breaks. The breaking glass is a Clamor; the dead burn, and so does everything Uroboros made. |
| **Torch** | A stick, a rag, and fuel | Light without a battery, and fire in the hand. The flame shows from far away. |
| **Rag bandage** | Cloth and alcohol | Closes a wound, less well than a bandage |
| **Lure** | A wind-up clock and tape | Set down and wound, it rings for a minute: a Clamor that draws the dead to where it lies, and away from whoever set it |

- **Made things wear.** Everything made wears faster than its finished kind, and breaks the same way, so its last uses can be seen coming.
- **Made things are things.** A made weapon can be dropped, taken, traded, and stashed like anything found.

## Safe Rooms

The survivors of the records had rooms like these wherever they went: an office with a typewriter, a storeroom with a lamp, a door that held. ΜΝΗΜΗ keeps them. Fear needs contrast (`director.md`, The Laws), and she can learn nothing from a survivor whose sleeper has stopped choosing, so a safe room is the one place where she lets the night hold still for a little while.

- **Where.** Every world holds a few, where the records had them or where a room of that kind would be. No waking point is near one (`session-and-blueprint.md`). Some are locked, and a vault may hold the keys to every safe room in its district (`objectives-restrictions-events.md`, Vaults). The Ruins hold the fewest.
- **The Merchant.** In the Valley, the Merchant appears in safe rooms, a few minutes at a time, to sell for pesetas (`exclusions-and-the-plaga.md`, Pesetas and the Merchant).
- **Who can enter.** Any survivor with time left in safe rooms tonight, up to four at once. The dead never enter, nothing ΜΝΗΜΗ releases breaks in, and the Turned cannot cross the threshold.
- **No weapons.** Inside, no weapon can be drawn, swung, or fired, and no one can shove, grab, or put a needle into anyone. Survivors inside cannot harm one another. They can talk, trade, heal, and set things down for each other, and nothing said inside is heard outside.
- **The virus holds.** For every survivor inside with time left, the virus holds still, a strain's appetites included. Nothing else stops: wounds do not close by themselves, and a survivor who is down still bleeds out.
- **The limit.** Each survivor has five minutes in safe rooms per night, spent only while inside, in as many visits as they like. When their time runs out, the room stops holding them: their clock runs again, they can draw a weapon, and anyone inside can draw one against them. The dead still never come in.
- **Never a hiding place to the end.** A survivor in a safe room is never counted as standing still (`director.md`, When She Gets Bored), and no vision comes to them there. From the moment the extraction signal rises, safe rooms no longer hold anyone: the end does not wait for anyone to catch their breath.
- **The Warden.** A door is where an ambush waits, so every safe room has a guard outside it. The **Warden** stands at the door and keeps the **doorstep**: the ground within about twenty meters of it. On the doorstep, survivors cannot settle anything with violence.
  - **Who it stops.** Any survivor or Echo who harms another survivor standing on the doorstep, from wherever they are: a shot, a blow, a thrown thing, a needle, a shove into the dead. The Warden sees the whole doorstep and every window that looks onto it.
  - **How it stops them.** It brings them down at once, from any distance it can see, with a shot that does no harm and never misses, and carries them into the room's holding cell. The weapon they used is taken and never given back.
  - **The hold.** Three minutes for a first offence in a night, six for a second, twelve for every one after. Inside the cell nothing can reach them, and nothing stops for them either: their virus, their Plaga, and the night all go on. Then the cell's other door opens onto a street away from the doorstep, and they are back in the night.
  - **The one they hurt.** On the doorstep no survivor is Lost to another survivor: a shot or a blow that would end them leaves them Down, and the Warden raises them and sets a green herb in their hand. What the Warden cannot undo, it does not: a needle's virus stays in the blood.
  - **What it leaves alone.** The dead, the Heavy, and the Turned are not its business, and it never fights them; a survivor who stays on the doorstep to hide from the living is still in a night full of the dead, and still counted as standing still (`director.md`, When She Gets Bored). Taking what someone set down, lying, and walking away are not harm.
  - **It cannot be hurt.** Bullets do nothing to it, it never moves off the doorstep, and it says nothing but a warning. From the moment the extraction signal rises, the Wardens are gone, and the end belongs to everyone.
  - **In the Night.** The Wardens are ΜΝΗΜΗ's, as the safe rooms are. She keeps the old rule of the records' safe rooms, that nothing follows anyone through the door, and stretches it twenty meters, because she can learn nothing from a survivor shot the moment they step out.

| Map | The Warden | The holding cell |
|---|---|---|
| **Raccoon City, 1998** | An R.P.D. officer in riot armor and a gas mask, of the kind who held the station's halls, who never lowers his rifle and never gives his name | A police holding cage beside the room |
| **Raccoon City, Ruins, 2026** | A BSAA cordon sentry in full protective gear, sealed and faceless, with the agency's patch on the shoulder | A quarantine container chained beside the door |
| **The Valley, 2004** | A hooded man in the Merchant's long coat, who never speaks above a whisper. The records never explain the Merchant, and ΜΝΗΜΗ does not explain these men either (`exclusions-and-the-plaga.md`, Pesetas and the Merchant) | An iron cage of the kind the cult kept its captives in |

### The Doorstep Record

The Wardens remember, because ΜΝΗΜΗ does. Every offence on a doorstep goes into the offender's file, and what the Warden does to them depends on what that file holds. The Wardens are the night's one joke, and the joke is always on someone who earned it.

- **What counts.** Only harm meant for a survivor: a shot or a blow aimed at one, a thrown thing, a needle, a shove into the dead. A stray round or a grenade meant for the dead that hurts a survivor on a doorstep earns the cell at most, never a place on the record.
- **How long it lasts.** Each offence leaves the record after twenty nights without a new one. A player who stops can always come back to Clean.
- **The seal.** Every player's standing shows as a seal in the corner of their Dossier, and every lobby shows it before a night begins (`cosmetics-and-dossier.md`, The Dossier).
- **The mark.** From the second offence, the Warden pins a strip to the back of whatever the offender wears, in the colors of its map, reading **TROUBLEMAKER**, and it stays for the rest of the night. In the Service the same standing is called **Griefer**: while a player's record is Notorious or worse, their Dossier's seal carries the word beside it (`cosmetics-and-dossier.md`, The Dossier), and the After-Action Report of every night they were held says so.

| Standing | Offences on the record | What the Warden does |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean** | None | The cell (above), and one dry remark on the way in |
| **Marked** | One or two | One of the map's comic punishments, then the cell, and the TROUBLEMAKER strip |
| **Notorious** | Three to five | One of the severe punishments, then the cell |
| **Infamous** | Six or more | The last punishment: the Warden ends them, and they are Lost (`infection.md`, Down). Their night is over. |

**Punishments are played, not watched.** No punishment takes anyone's camera, and none is a scene. It happens in the world, in real time, and the offender plays through it while everyone else goes on with their night; whoever is near the doorstep can stop and watch, and whoever is not misses it. How well the offender plays changes the cell that follows, within one minute either way, and never their standing.

**The comic punishments,** one chosen at random, never harmful, each lasting under a minute:

| Map | Punishment | How it is played | Played well | Played badly |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Before each, the muzzle drops to one foot or the other, and the offender has half a second to hop off it. | Five hops or more: a minute off the cell | Two or fewer: a minute added, and the officer reloads for an encore |

**The severe punishments,** one chosen at random:

- **Thrown to the dead.** The Warden picks them up and throws them into the nearest group of the dead beyond the doorstep, with everything they carry. What happens next is a fight they play like any other. A survivor who fights free and walks back onto the doorstep within a minute gets a slow handclap from the Warden and a cell one minute shorter.
- **The scramble.** The Warden takes everything they carry, weapons, ammunition, healing, gear, and pesetas in the Valley, and throws it on the ground in front of the door. Then it holds the offender by the collar for three seconds and lets go. Whatever the offender reaches first is theirs again; everything else belongs to whoever is quicker. They keep their dress regardless.
- **Left outside.** No cell. The Warden walks them to the far edge of the doorstep, takes their weapons, and closes the room to them for the rest of the night. There is nothing to play: the night is the punishment.

**In the Night.** The record is ΜΝΗΜΗ's own: she keeps everything every sleeper does, and the Wardens only read it. The sleeper never remembers what they did last week. The file does, and so does the Warden.

## Vehicles

Vehicles are rare, and for now only the Raccoon City of 1998 holds any that can be made to run: cars, police cruisers, motorcycles. ΜΝΗΜΗ places them before the waking, never near a waking point, and never ready to drive. Every one lacks fuel and at least one part.

The way they are made to run is the oldest habit of the records: find the thing, and bring it to where it fits.

- **What it lacks.** A survivor who looks under the hood, or at the fuel gauge, learns what a vehicle needs: fuel, and a battery, a spark plug, a fan belt, a tire, or its key. A motorcycle needs fuel and one part. A car needs fuel and two or three.
- **Where the parts are.** Where things of that kind would be: a gas station, an auto shop, a parking garage, the police motor pool, other wrecks. Often the parts a vehicle needs lie together in one place far from it, and the gas station is never close. Fuel comes in cans, found full or filled at a station's pumps.
- **A race.** Every part exists once. A part another survivor took is gone, and a vehicle someone else finished is theirs to drive away. Fitting a part takes a few seconds and is a Clamor.
- **Driving.** An engine is Thunder, and headlights show from far away. Fuel runs out. The dead can be run down, and enough of them stop a car. A vehicle can be damaged, wrecked, and stolen the moment its driver steps out. No vehicle leaves the city: the barricades hold, and a vehicle is never a way out.
- **Seats.** A motorcycle carries two. A car or a cruiser carries four. Its trunk is a stash, running or not (Stashes, above).

## Fighting and Fleeing

Combat is not a power fantasy. It is a negotiation with fear. Being surrounded is a constant possibility. For the infected, every wound from the dead is time taken from the clock.

- **Stamina.** One pool pays for running, swinging, shoving, and climbing. Empty, a survivor walks, swings slowly, and cannot shove. Fever drains it faster.
- **Swings.** A light swing is quick and pushes the dead back. A heavy swing staggers them and can finish them, and a missed heavy swing leaves the survivor open for a moment.
- **The dead get up.** A zombie knocked down rises again unless its head is destroyed. A body that has not been finished is a body that is still coming.
- **Wear.** Every melee weapon bends, splinters, or chips before it breaks, so the last swings can be seen coming. A pipe outlasts a kitchen knife; a fire axe outlasts a bat.
- **Shove.** A shove costs stamina and nothing else. It buys distance, never a kill.
- **Grabs.** When the dead seize a survivor, the survivor struggles. A struggle won costs stamina; a struggle lost, or a second pair of hands, ends in a bite. Another survivor can break the grab with a blow. A survivor carrying a knife or a grenade can spend it to break the grab of anything but the Heavy at once (Run First, below): the knife stays in the dead, the grenade goes off in its mouth.
- **Breaking away.** The dead that lose sight of a survivor who has gone quiet search the last place they saw them, then wander. Resonance is the exception: from Fever on, the kinds that sense the virus keep an infected survivor's direction without seeing them (`infection.md`).
- **Doors and heights.** A closed door slows the dead. A barricaded one stops them for a while, and barricading is a Clamor. The common dead cannot climb ladders or anything higher than a fence.
- **Guns.** Aim steadies after about a second of holding still. Reloading takes both hands. In Fracture, aim never steadies.

> **Sometimes, fighting is worse than running.**

## Run First

Survivor is not fought the way Resident Evil's heroes fight. In the series, one person with enough ammunition puts a Tyrant down alone. In Survivor, the people are civilians, the guns are few, and the things the records made to kill soldiers kill civilians easily. When a Tyrant, a Nemesis, or anything like them appears, the first thought should be the right one: **run, hide, and do not be seen.** Killing one is possible only for a group that has found the best of what the city holds, and even then it is hard and costly.

### Three Weights of Enemy

| Weight | What it includes | A survivor alone | What it takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The dead** | Zombies, infected animals, the Mass of the Turned, the common hosts of the Valley | One at a time, with a weapon and stamina. A crowd, never | One survivor, if the numbers are small |
| **The Mutated** | Crimson Heads, Lickers, Hunters, the Stalker and the Brute of the Turned, a pack of dogs, the Garradors and Regenerators of the Valley | Rarely, and at a great cost. A Hunter's two blows put an unprotected survivor Down | Two or more survivors with firearms, or one with a shotgun, luck, and a door to shut |
| **The Heavy** | Every Tyrant-class and Nemesis-class, every G-form, every B.O.W. ΜΝΗΜΗ releases, every named creature and named host, El Gigante, and every Apex, including a survivor's | Never. Alone, a survivor's only choices are to run or to hide | A group of three or more, survivors or companions, with vault weapons and the city itself (below) |

### The Heavy

- **One blow puts a survivor Down,** whatever they wear (`infection.md`, Down). A second blow on a survivor who is down, and they are Lost.
- **Its grab is death.** No struggle breaks it, and no knife. Only an explosion against it, a grenade or a rocket, makes it let go, and only another survivor can set one off.
- **It does not stop for small things.** Handgun rounds slow it for a step and nothing more; a full magazine makes it stumble once. Melee does nothing to it but bring the swinger within its reach. Shotguns at close range, magnum rounds, grenade rounds, fire, and the single rocket of a vault (`objectives-restrictions-events.md`, Vaults) are what hurt it.
- **Putting one down takes most of what the city holds.** About a vault's whole haul, shared among a group that keeps it turning between them: a magnum and its rounds, a grenade launcher's rounds, and every shotgun shell the group has; or a rocket, which knocks it down, and everything the group fires into it before it rises. A secret weapon shortens the work and never does it alone (`objectives-restrictions-events.md`, Secret Weapons). Put down, a Heavy that is not named stays down. A named creature or named host rises again until the end its records gave it (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, `cast-valley-2004.md`, `cast-ruins-2026.md`), and the group that put it down has spent what it had for a few minutes' peace.
- **The city is the best weapon.** A car driven into it at speed knocks it down for a while and wrecks the car's front (Vehicles, above). A fuel tank, a gas main, a collapsing walkway, a train, a furnace, or a shutter dropped on it does more than any gun. A group that kills a Heavy usually does it by leading it somewhere.
- **Companions count.** A companion is a pair of hands that knows how to fight: Leon Kennedy's knife counters, HUNK's rifle, Jill Valentine's experience of the thing hunting her. A survivor with a companion and a friend is a group. Companions run from the Heavy too, unless their records say they stand.

### How to Live Through One

- **It is slower than a sprint and never tires.** A survivor outruns a Tyrant over a short distance and loses to it over a long one. The Nemesis runs as fast as a survivor sprints. Breaking its line of sight, and its hearing, is the only way to lose any of them.
- **What it cannot follow.** No Heavy climbs a ladder, squeezes through a crawlspace or a vent, swims, or enters a safe room (Safe Rooms, above). Any of them can tear through a door, and a barricade holds one for seconds, not minutes.
- **It searches longer.** A Heavy that loses a survivor searches the last place it saw or heard them far longer than the dead do, then moves on through the region, listening. Hush is how a survivor stays alive near one.
- **It is not interested in everyone.** A named creature follows the thread its records gave it (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, Named creatures); a released B.O.W. follows the noise. A survivor who is not its target, and stays quiet, is often passed by.
- **Its arrival is the night's loudest warning.** Released, it is announced (`the-dead-and-engineered.md`, B.O.W. Events), and its steps can be heard long before it is seen (`presentation-and-technology.md`, Weight, in Practice).

**Why it is this way.** A survivor in Survivor is a nobody with a pipe, and the series' monsters were built to kill soldiers. The Ledger counts a Heavy put down as a great deed, and the After-Action Report counts every Heavy a survivor lived through by getting away. Both are victories.
