# The Worlds

Every map is divided into regions, and the region is the unit ΜΝΗΜΗ thinks in: tension, threat, power, strain, and restrictions are all set region by region.

## The Open Worlds

Survivor is played on whole places, rebuilt as the records hold them. Every street, building, and tunnel the records hold is where the records put it. The records hold the districts that mattered, not every suburb, and ΜΝΗΜΗ rebuilds those districts and closes the rest behind barricades, fog, wire, and sea. Where the records leave a gap inside a district, she builds what a place of that kind, in that year, would have held, and nothing that contradicts what the records say.

Each world is a few square kilometers of dense streets, buildings, and tunnels: large enough that crossing it on foot takes most of an hour, small enough that sixty-four survivors keep running into one another. Vehicles are rare. For now only the Raccoon City of 1998 holds any that can be made to run, and none is ever found ready to (`survival.md`, Vehicles). Every engine is Thunder. Every map also hides a few secret weapons, the records' own, behind its hardest ways in (`objectives-restrictions-events.md`, Secret Weapons). The subway and the cable car run only where someone has restored the power. Every world holds a few safe rooms, where a survivor can stop for a little while (`survival.md`, Safe Rooms).

| Map | Version | Survivors | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Raccoon City** | September 1998 | 40 to 100 | The city in its last week, during the outbreak, before the missile |
| **Raccoon City** | Ruins, 2026 | 40 to 64 | The same city twenty-eight years later, walled, cratered, and still not empty. Not at launch. |
| **The Valley** | Autumn 2004 | 64 to 100 | A valley in rural Spain, its village, the castle above the sea, and the island beyond. Not at launch. |

### RACCOON CITY · SEPTEMBER 1998

The city as it was in its last week, rebuilt from every record in the Archive: a Midwestern city of about a hundred thousand people that died and kept standing, with the Arklay Mountains at its back. Wet asphalt reflects the glow of emergency lights. Wrecked cars choke the avenues. The city is closed. Its edges are the Army's barricades, and beyond them ΜΝΗΜΗ's world ends in fog and wire. Each night runs the city's last three days and ends with the missile (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`).

| Region | What it holds | Where something is kept |

| **Uptown** | Residential streets, the Apple Inn, the Kite Bros Railway station | Freight containers in the rail yard |

The R.P.D. is rebuilt whole, with every lock, statue, and panel it ever had. It is the hardest building in the city to cross and the shortest road to the train beneath it.

### RACCOON CITY · RUINS, 2026

The same city, twenty-eight years after the missile. A wall rings it, and the quarantine behind the wall never lifted. At its heart is the crater. Around the crater, blocks stand gutted and leaning, and the farther from the center, the more of the old city remains. The R.P.D. still stands. Beneath the crater lies ARK.

The dead here are old. Some have been dead since 1998, and the virus in them has had a long time to work. The ruins have had no power for twenty-eight years: every way out that needs power needs a generator found and carried to it.

The Ruins are Survivor's hardcore map. A sleeper chooses them in the Waiting Dark, and they are never given to anyone who asked only for the classic rotation unless that player accepts them when a long wait offers them (`presentation-and-technology.md`, Matchmaking). Everything that makes the Raccoon City of 1998 hard is harder here, and the city does almost nothing to fill the silence.

- **A dead city.** Twenty-eight years after the missile, the city has no color left: ash on concrete, dead grass, rust, and a grey sky over all of it. The only color in a night is what survivors carry, what they bleed, and the Marks the virus draws.
- **Near silence.** Most of the time a survivor hears only the wind, a door banging somewhere, and the dead groaning far off. There is less music here than anywhere (`presentation-and-technology.md`, Music). Nothing masks a sound here: every tier carries half again as far as it does in any other world, except in rain (`the-dead-and-engineered.md`).
- **Few dead.** The common dead are far fewer than in 1998, scattered through blocks that have been empty for decades, and some still carry what they held when they fell, an axe among them. What makes them dangerous is that a survivor rarely hears them before they are close. The rest are what the records put in the city that night: packs of Garmr in the Outer Blocks, the Titan Spinner and its young in a ruined block, the T-501 in the R.P.D., Plant 43 at the mouth of ARK, and Lickers only inside ARK (`cast-ruins-2026.md`).
- **Extreme scarcity.** The city has been picked over for twenty-eight years. A night's Scarcity is never Abundant here, and medicine and healing are always Barren, whatever the setting (`survival.md`, Scarcity). So are food and clean water (`survival.md`, Hunger and Thirst). A first aid spray is a treasure, and most survivors heal with what they make.
- **Crafting.** The Ruins are the only world where survivors make what they cannot find (`survival.md`, Crafting). The Raccoon City of 1998 is still full of what its people left behind, and needs none.
- **Few safe rooms, and no vehicle that runs.** The cars of the Ruins are wrecks rusted to the road. They still have trunks (`survival.md`, Stashes).
- **Every hour and weather.** The Ruins have every hour and every weather of the classic rotation (Time and Weather, below).

### THE VALLEY · AUTUMN 2004

A valley in rural Spain and the sea beyond it, rebuilt as the records hold them: a village of stone houses around a plaza, farms, a quarry, a deep lake, and a church on the hill. Above it all, on the cliffs over the sea, stands the castle of the Salazar family. Offshore is the island where Los Iluminados keep their laboratories and their soldiers. It is the largest of the Survivor worlds, and the only one that holds the Plaga (`exclusions-and-the-plaga.md`). It is not in the game at launch: it arrives after the Opening (`seasons-and-new-content.md`).

It is one world in three parts, and the story or the survivors decide when each part opens. Every survivor wakes in the village. The castle and the island are in the world from the first minute, in sight across the valley and the water, and closed. Each part opens with a beat of the story (`cast-valley-2004.md`), or with a way that belongs to no thread, which works only after night falls. If neither happens, it opens at the latest moment the story allows.

## The Edge of the World

Every world ends somewhere, and no one in a Survivor night walks or drives off its edge. There is no invisible wall. Nothing stops a survivor who heads for the city limits except what the outbreak left there, and what the outbreak left there is enough. A survivor who wakes, decides the cure is not worth the risk, takes a motorcycle, and rides for the edge of town will find out why everyone else is waiting for a helicopter.

### Why No One Walks Out of Raccoon City

The records hold the reasons, and ΜΝΗΜΗ keeps all of them.

- **The dead went first.** When the city fell, most of its dead did not stay in it. They walked out after the living who fled, into the fields and the Arklay woods, and they are still there: thousands of them in the dark under the trees, all around the city. The streets hold what was left behind.
- **The ones who got out shut the door behind them.** The people who escaped in the first days, police, the Army's first units, families in pickup trucks, closed the roads behind them so the dead could not follow: cars pushed nose to tail and chained, buses turned on their sides, trucks parked across every lane, trees felled across the back roads, earth pushed up into berms, wire strung between them. Some of them were on the far side when they finished. Some were not.
- **The Army keeps the ring.** Beyond the woods stands the quarantine: searchlights, towers, and an order to stop anything that comes out of the trees, living or dead, before it reaches the cordon. In the records, nothing the Army stopped was asked whether it was still human.

So a city that was meant to be left is ringed three times over, and every ring was built by someone who wanted out, or wanted the dead kept in.

### The Three Rings

| Ring | What it is | What happens there |
|---|---|---|
| **The Outskirts** | The last blocks before the city limits: houses with their windows broken, gas stations, motels, the last stretch of every road out | The dead that followed the convoys gather here, and so do the things that hunt by sound: Lickers in the last houses, packs of infected dogs come in from the woods, Crimson Heads where the convoys fought their way out. The Outskirts are at High threat all night (`the-dead-and-engineered.md`, Threat Levels). Whatever lives here goes first for what is loudest and fastest: an engine. |
| **The Barricades** | The wall of cars, buses, trucks, trees, and earth that the people who fled left across every road | No vehicle passes. Every barricade of the ring is of the Never tier (`destruction.md`, The Tiers): nothing breaks it, rams it, or blows it open. On foot it can be climbed in a few places, slowly, and the climb is a Clamor. |
| **No Man's Land** | The fields and woods beyond, and past them the Army's cordon | The dead of the woods, more of them than in any district of the city, and in the dark. Past them, the cordon: a searchlight, a loudspeaker ordering anyone in the trees to turn back, a flare, a warning shot, and then a shot that does not miss. A survivor who goes on after the warning has begun the Crossing (below), and almost never comes back from it. |

Beyond No Man's Land, ΜΝΗΜΗ's world ends in fog (One World, Every Mode, below).

### How the Edge Answers

The edge is not a punishment, and ΜΝΗΜΗ does not throw anything at anyone who reaches it. It is held the way any place is held, by her Laws (`director.md`, The Laws):

- **Everything at the edge was there first.** The dead of the Outskirts, the Lickers in the last houses, the dogs from the woods, and the barricades are placed at Assembly with everything else (First Law). When a survivor comes close, what was waiting wakes, as anything in the city wakes when someone comes close to it.
- **It falls on a place, not a person.** The Outskirts answer whoever enters them: the first survivor of the night and the hundredth, a Veteran and a Civilian, the clean and the marked (Second Law). They answer a crowd as they answer one rider.
- **It answers what was done.** An engine at the edge is Thunder at the edge (Fourth Law). The faster and louder someone comes, the more of what lives there comes for them.
- **The world says so.** No one reaches the edge without being told what it is: QUARANTINE ZONE signs nailed to the barricades, the Army's warnings on every road out, the loudspeakers in the trees, the bodies of the people who tried before (Fifth Law).
- **Hard, not hopeless.** Before No Man's Land, nothing at the edge kills a survivor by itself. It stops them, wrecks what they came in, makes them loud, and leaves them to get back into the city alive. Only the Crossing kills, and only someone who chose it after the warning.

**Example: the motorcycle.** A survivor finds a motorcycle in Uptown in the first ten minutes, fuels it, and rides west, away from everything.

| Step | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The engine is Thunder the whole way. The dead of every street they pass turn to follow the sound. | An engine is Thunder (`survival.md`, Vehicles). |
| 2 | The Outskirts. The last houses before the city limits, two motels, a gas station. From an upstairs window, a Licker drops onto the rider at forty miles an hour. | Lickers wait in the last houses; they hunt by sound, and nothing at the edge is louder than an engine. |
| 3 | The motorcycle goes down. The rider is thrown, hurt and on the ground, the bike is wrecked, and the crash is Thunder of its own. The Licker is on them, and the dead the engine dragged across the district are a minute behind. | The edge stops what reaches it. The crash is the rider's own noise. |
| 4 | If they live, they see what they were riding toward: three buses on their sides across the road, chained, and QUARANTINE ZONE sprayed across them. Past them, the dark under the trees, moving. | The Barricades and No Man's Land. The world says so. |

The rider was never told no. They were shown why, and now they are on foot, wounded, loud, at the far edge of the city, with the night still ahead of them.

### The Crossing

Walking out is possible. It is also suicide, and ΜΝΗΜΗ says so in numbers.

A survivor who has got past the Outskirts and over the Barricades on foot (no vehicle passes them) reaches the wire at the treeline, where No Man's Land begins. There the cordon's loudspeaker gives its warning, and ΜΝΗΜΗ gives hers, behind the eyes for the marked and through the earpiece for anyone wearing it, exactly as she gives the odds of any machine (`extraction.md`, The Odds):

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: YOU ARE LEAVING THE CITY ON FOOT.
CHANCE OF CROSSING THE CORDON: 1%.
```

- **The wire is the line.** Stepping through the wire is the choice, and it is the last one. From that step the Crossing is a scene: ΜΝΗΜΗ takes the camera and the survivor's hands, as in a Last Scene (`extraction.md`, The Last Scene), because nothing in the night can reach someone who has left it, and nothing they do now changes how it ends. Until that step, a survivor at the wire can still turn back.
- **One in a hundred.** The odds are 1%, on every night, at every hour, for everyone: before the signal and after it, alone or in a crowd, clean or marked, Civilian or Veteran. Nothing raises them. Each survivor who steps through the wire is settled alone, the moment they step, so a squad that crosses together may lose all four, or three.
- **Ninety-nine times.** The scene plays the dark under the trees, the dead coming out of it from every side, the searchlight finding the survivor, the loudspeaker, and the end: taken in the woods, caught on the wire of the cordon, or cut down in the open ground before it. The survivor is Lost (`the-turned.md`, The Lost), and everything they carried stays in No Man's Land.
- **Once.** The scene plays the same dark, the same dead, the same searchlight sweeping past, and then a road on the far side of the cordon, empty, with the city burning behind the trees. The survivor is extracted as they are: Clean, Cured, or Infected (The Boarding, in `extraction.md`). They earn what any extraction earns, and the Ledger marks the night.
- **Not a seat.** The Crossing takes no seat from anyone, opens before the signal as well as after it, and is never lit, announced, or named as a way out. It is the one way through the edge that no one made.

The Crossing exists on every map: over the wall of the Ruins and through the BSAA's guns, or out of the Valley through the forest past the cult's watchers. Its odds are the same everywhere.

### The Only Ways Through

Besides the Crossing, the only ways through the edge are the ways out (`extraction.md`), and that is why they are worth everything. A helicopter flies over all three rings. A train runs on a line the Army holds open for its own. The Checkpoint is the one gate in the Barricades, and before the signal its soldiers turn everyone back with warning shots. An improvised way out works because it does what the ways out do: it flies over, or it runs on rails or water the barricades never closed (`extraction.md`, Improvised Ways Out).

A car, a cruiser, or a motorcycle is for crossing the city: faster than running, louder than anything, with a trunk to carry what a survivor cannot (`survival.md`, Vehicles). It is never for leaving. Driving at the edge is the quickest way to learn it.

**In the Night.** Past the fog there is nothing: ΜΝΗΜΗ built only what the records hold, and no record holds the road beyond the cordon. She does not need a wall to keep anyone in. The records gave her three rings of reasons, and she uses all of them. The records also hold the very few who walked out through the woods and lived, and so she keeps that door open, exactly as wide as the records left it: one in a hundred. For the one who makes it, the night ends on a road she only has to draw as far as the next bend.

## One World, Every Mode

Each version of each place is built once, whole, and every mode plays on it. Survivor does not have a map of its own and no future mode will either: there is one Raccoon City of September 1998, one Ruins of 2026, and one Valley of 2004, and every night of every mode is played somewhere inside one of them. A new mode never needs a new map. It needs only to say which part of an existing world it plays in.

### The World and Its Bounds

- **The world.** Everything a version holds, built at full fidelity: every district, building, interior, tunnel, and sewer the records hold, rebuilt where the records put them (The Open Worlds, above). A world also holds the land just past Survivor's edge that the records of other exercises need: the woods and the abandoned hospital in the Arklay hills, the Army's staging base behind the barricades. Those outskirts are built with the first mode that plays in them, as part of the same world, never as a separate map.
- **A bound.** The part of a world that one kind of night plays in: which regions and interiors are open, where its edges run, how each edge is sealed, where its people enter, and where its ways out stand. Every mode plays a bound. Survivor's bound is the largest one: the whole place inside the barricades, the wall, or the sea.
- **Bounds only choose and seal.** A bound never adds a street, a room, or a door the world does not have, and never moves one. It opens part of the world and closes the rest. The only things a bound places are its seals and what its mode needs inside it: its people, its dead, its supplies, its objectives, as each mode's own rules say.
- **One place, one shape.** A room is the same room in every mode. The bar in Downtown where the eight began is J's Bar in a Survivor night, in an Outbreak scenario, and in a Mercenaries stage, with the same doors, the same back stairs, and the same window onto the street. Whoever knows it from one mode knows it in all of them. What changes from mode to mode is who is in it, what lies in it, and which of its doors are sealed.

### Seals

A bound's edges are never invisible walls. Every seal is something the world could hold, of a kind the records used, and anyone who reaches one can see why they can go no farther.

| Seal | Where it serves |
|---|---|
| **Barricade** | A street, an alley, a doorway: the Army's concrete and wire, a police line, cars pushed nose to tail, furniture stacked by someone who did not make it |
| **Collapse** | A street, a stairwell, a corridor: a building front, a ceiling, or a section of tunnel brought down, from the collapses the world already holds (`destruction.md`) |
| **Locked and shuttered** | A door, a gate, a shop front: steel shutters down, a chained gate, an Umbrella lockdown door that answers no card |
| **Fire** | A block or a floor: burning the way the records' city burned, too hot to cross |
| **Water** | A tunnel or a sewer branch: flooded to the ceiling |
| **Fog and distance** | The far edges: the fog and wire where ΜΝΗΜΗ's world already ends |

- **Built once, used by every bound.** Each seal is set dressing made once and placed wherever a bound needs it, so a new bound is a choice of places and seals, not new building.
- **Seals hold.** Nothing breaks a seal: not a survivor, not a Heavy, not an explosion. A seal is of the Never tier (`destruction.md`, The Tiers) for as long as its bound lasts, even where the same wall can be broken in another mode.
- **Seals tell the truth.** A seal looks like what it is. A door that is only locked tonight looks locked, not sealed. A seal never looks like a puzzle, a way out, or a door that could be opened.

### What a Bound Changes

- **Only what is inside it exists.** The dead, the supplies, the people of the records, and the sounds of a night exist only inside its bound. Nothing outside it is simulated, and no sound crosses a seal.
- **The state of the version holds.** A bound plays its version as it is: the 1998 city whole and burning, the Ruins grey and walled. A mode sets its own hour, weather, and condition inside it, by its own rules (Time and Weather, below).
- **Everything inside works as it does in the world.** A puzzle, a lock built for two, a vault, a safe room, a subway's power, and a breach behave the same in every bound that holds them, unless the mode's own rules switch them off (`future-development.md`, Which Rules Would Hold Where).
- **A bound may change between nights.** A mode can hold several bounds on the same world and choose among them, or change one from night to night: a different door sealed, a different street opened. The world stays the same.

A survivor who once crossed J's Bar in a Survivor night would find, in the exercise of the eight, the same bar, with the same stain on the floor.

## Time and Weather

The records hold Raccoon City's last days mostly by night and in rain, and most of what anyone remembers of the city happened in the dark. ΜΝΗΜΗ is not bound to the records' sky. An outbreak does not choose its hour, and she wants to know how the same people, in the same streets, behave by daylight, in the long light of an afternoon, or on a grey wet morning. So at Assembly she sets each night's hour and weather, as part of its condition (`session-and-blueprint.md`).

- **The hour.** Dawn, morning, afternoon, dusk, or night. The sky moves on from there at the pace of the night, so a night that begins at dusk ends in darkness. The events of a story night keep their order and their acts whatever the hour; only the sky changes. On a night set in daylight, the missile still comes at the end, under whatever sky she chose (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, The Last Morning). Whatever the hour, ΜΝΗΜΗ calls every session a night, and so do these documents: for the sleeper it is one, lived asleep in the Cellar, even when its sky is noon. A story night can run from morning to morning; a night set at noon is still a night. When these documents mean the time of day, they say the hour.
- **The weather.** Clear, overcast, mist, drizzle, or rain: the ordinary weather of the place and the season. There are no storms: no thunder, no lightning, no gale, no snow. The weather may turn once during a night, from dry to rain or back, and the world shows it coming (`director.md`, What She Can Do).
- **The Valley.** The Valley of 2004 keeps the records' hours, because its story turns on them: day in the first act, night falling at the second, and the small hours on the island (`cast-valley-2004.md`). Only its weather varies.

| Hour or weather | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Dawn, dusk** | Long shadows and low light. Shapes are seen before faces. |
| **Morning, afternoon** | Visibility is high. The dead are visible too, and so is every survivor on an open street. A drop's strobe is harder to see than its smoke. |
| **Night** | Light is a resource. Darkness is a weapon. |
| **Overcast** | Flat light, and no shadows to hide in. |
| **Mist** | Sight ends early, and shapes come out of it late. |
| **Drizzle, rain** | Sound is muffled. So are warnings. Rain muffles more than drizzle. |

## Conditions

Besides its hour and weather, a night may hold one condition of the city itself.

| Condition | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Blackout** | No power. No light. No security. |
| **Quarantine** | Sealed districts, checkpoints, forced routes |
| **Infestation** | The dead are everywhere before the session begins |

| Map | Hours | Weather | Blackout | Quarantine | Infestation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Raccoon City, 1998 | All | All | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Raccoon City Ruins, 2026 | All | All | — | — | ✓ |
| The Valley, 2004 | By act | All | ✓ | — | ✓ |

The ruins have been dark for twenty-eight years, so Blackout changes nothing there, and their wall is a quarantine that never ends. The Valley was never quarantined.
