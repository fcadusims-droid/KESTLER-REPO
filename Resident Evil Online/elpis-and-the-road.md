# Elpis in the Night

> *One cure exists.*

Elpis is the only way out of the virus. It cannot be crafted, bought, or scavenged. It is not in the pharmacy, not in the hospital vault, and not on any of the city's dead. It appears only when ΜΝΗΜΗ decides a survivor has earned it, and when it appears, everyone nearby can see.

It is scarce and fought over, and it lasts the whole night: some of it can still be earned in the night's last ten minutes (The Reserve, below). The Valley of 2004 has none (`exclusions-and-the-plaga.md`). Antivirals can hold the virus for a few minutes (`infection.md`), but nothing else ends it.

## What It Is

A vial of luminous yellow liquid seated in a single-use injector, unmistakable at a glance, glowing faintly in the dark. Its name is the Greek word for hope, and the word that unsealed it in ARK was *Hope*. It answers every virus in ΜΝΗΜΗ's world.

## The Road

ΜΝΗΜΗ does not hand Elpis to anyone. From the minute a survivor's Mark rises, she sets them a **road**: a chain of Trials, usually three, never fewer than two, rarely more than four. The Trials arrive one at a time, privately, through the Mark. She never says how many remain. When the last one is met, the Mark pulses once, and the sky begins to sound.

Trials come in three kinds.

**Missions.** The objectives of the session: restore the power, recover the components, rescue the one who needs rescuing.

**Challenges.** Feats of risk: put down a Licker in melee, hold a position through an overrun, carry a wounded survivor through a hostile district, enter a sealed zone and come back out.

**Rules.** Conduct she demands for a stretch of the session: cross a district without firing, stay within sight of a partner, spare a survivor who surrenders, share a supply with a stranger. Rules reward restraint, cooperation, and mercy as often as skill. Breaking one voids that Trial, and she sets a harder one in its place.

**Shared Trials.** She often gives the same Trial to several survivors at once, usually survivors already in the same place. Each who sees it through moves one step along their own road. A shared Trial can finish one survivor's road and leave the others still walking theirs. If it finishes several roads at the same moment, a dose falls for each of them while doses are open, and the rest wait at the end of their roads (The Reserve, below).

**Equal roads.** Waking points are equal ground, and so are roads. ΜΝΗΜΗ prices every Trial by the time and the danger it should cost the survivor who receives it, from where they stand when it arrives, and she builds every road to the same total. A road of two Trials is made of harder Trials than a road of four. No Trial is set farther away than the next region, none depends on something another survivor already holds, and none on the road to Elpis asks a survivor to kill another survivor. A Shared Trial costs every survivor who receives it the same. She does not lengthen roads as the Reserve runs low; she stops setting them when it is gone.

**Roads in the Frenzy.** A road set after the signal is built to end before the last way out can leave. It costs the same total as every other road, paid in danger instead of time: two Trials, short ones, in the hardest places nearby. A road set in the night's last minutes is as short as two Trials can be. Whether anyone can finish it in time is up to them.

**Never the same road twice.** ΜΝΗΜΗ draws every Trial from what is around the survivor tonight: the objectives in play, the dead and the people nearby, the place itself. A road never holds the same Trial twice, and no file is set the same Trial twice within ten nights.

**Strangers are strangers.** A Trial that asks something of or for another survivor, a stranger, or someone who surrenders never counts a squadmate, a companion, or anyone the survivor woke beside. A Trial that wants a partner says so.

No two survivors walk exactly the same road, and no road is ever given back once abandoned.

## The Reserve

The Reserve is **scarce**: one dose for every ten survivors, rounded down, and never fewer than four (`session-and-blueprint.md`, The Shape of a Survivor Night).

The Reserve is also **paced**. ΜΝΗΜΗ never lets the whole night's cure be earned in its first half hour. She opens it one dose at a time, and she keeps two doses back for the end.

- **The night's doses.** Every dose but two. They open one at a time, at equal steps across the first hour, the last at the sixtieth minute. A dose that opens and is not yet earned stays open.
- **The Signal Dose.** Sealed until the signal, and opened by it.
- **The Last Dose.** Sealed until ten minutes remain before the night's end: the missile in the Raccoon City of 1998, the fall in the Ruins (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, `cast-ruins-2026.md`). It opens when her public voice calls the ten minutes.

| Survivors | Reserve | The night's doses open at minute | Held |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 4 | 30, 60 | Signal Dose, Last Dose |
| 64 | 6 | 15, 30, 45, 60 | Signal Dose, Last Dose |
| 100 | 10 | Every 7½ minutes, from 7½ to 60 | Signal Dose, Last Dose |

If the signal rises before the sixtieth minute (`extraction.md`, ΜΝΗΜΗ's Verdict), every night's dose not yet opened opens with it.

**The end of the road.** A survivor who finishes their road while a dose is open earns it at once. A survivor who finishes it while none is open waits at the end of the road. The Mark pulses once and stays warm, and the sky stays silent. Through the Mark she tells them only this: *"Your road is finished. Wait."* She does not say when the next dose opens, or how many are waiting. While they wait, their clock runs as anyone's does. An antiviral or a safe room can hold it (`infection.md`, Antivirals; `survival.md`, Safe Rooms).

**The Last Trial.** When a dose opens and survivors are waiting at the end of their roads, ΜΝΗΜΗ sets each of them one more Trial, privately, through the Mark. Each is priced like every other Trial, by the time and the danger it costs from where that survivor stands, and all of them cost the same. The first to meet theirs earns the dose, and it falls as every dose falls. Every other Last Trial ends unmet, and those survivors wait again for the next dose. A survivor who finishes their road while a Last Trial is running is given one too. A survivor keeps their place at the end of the road until they are cured, Turn, ask for a strain (`strains.md`, The Petition), or the night ends.

**At the signal.** On a map with Elpis, the signal says how many doses the Reserve still holds (`extraction.md`, The Signal). At the ten-minute call she says the last one is open:

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: ELPIS. THE LAST DOSE IS OPEN.
```

The Reserve is finite. When the last dose has been dropped, ΜΝΗΜΗ says so, and every road to the cure ends where it stands. Because of the pacing, that can happen only in the night's last ten minutes. A dose still unearned when the night ends is lost with everything else in it.

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: ELPIS RESERVE DEPLETED.
```

Anyone who had not yet earned a dose has just learned something terrible.

## The Drop

When a survivor earns a dose, by finishing their road while a dose is open or by winning a Last Trial, she calls it down.

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: ELPIS DEPLOYED.
```

Rotors somewhere above, close and heavy: the only aircraft still flying over the city. A flare ignites. A case descends under a canopy, lit by an amber strobe, and lands within sight of the one who earned it: near enough to run for, and near enough for everyone else to run for too. It always lands in the open, at least fifty meters away, where anyone can reach it on foot: never inside a building, behind a barricade, on a roof only one survivor can reach, or anywhere a survivor could have prepared for it. Where the sky cannot reach, underground or on the deep floors of a building, the case comes up a service lift or out of a locker that unseals within sight instead, under the same strobe, and as loud, and never inside a room that is barricaded or sealed. Every drop from the sky follows the same rules.

There is no marker, no ping, no icon. If a survivor sees the smoke, hears the rotors, or watches the canopy fall, they know. If they do not, they do not. The drop is Thunder, and the dead come too.

## Taking It

Earned is not owned. The case opens for anyone living. The survivor who finished the road has no claim, no lock, no protection. Whoever reaches it first may take it, and in a Survivor night, whoever carries it may be killed for it. The Turned cannot lift it. To them it is nothing.

**One dose a night.** A survivor can take hold of Elpis once per night. The first dose that comes into their hands is theirs to hold, whether it came from a case, from a body, or from another survivor's hand, and whether they earned it, took it, or were given it. After that, no case opens for them, and no injector stays in their hand: it slips from the grip the way it will not fire into a clean body. They can still be injected by someone else, and they can still give away, trade, or lose the dose they hold. The rule does not touch the Reserve, which stays as scarce as it is. It only stops one pair of hands from gathering the night's cure.

- **Who it binds.** Every survivor and every Echo. A clean survivor who steals a dose for a squadmate has used their hand for the night.
- **Who it does not.** The survivor who finished a road and never touched the case has taken nothing. Neither has a survivor who was injected by someone else.
- **In the Night.** The injector reads the hand that holds it, as it reads the body it fires into. ΜΝΗΜΗ wants to watch what a person does with one cure, not how many they can carry.

## Using It

Injecting takes about four seconds, during which the survivor is rooted in place and exposed. Damage taken mid-injection interrupts it, and the dose falls to the ground intact.

A survivor can inject themselves or another survivor. Giving the dose away is allowed, and it is the purest act of trust the game offers.

The injector will not fire into a body past the Threshold, and it will not fire into a clean one: Elpis answers a virus, and where there is none it has nothing to do. A clean survivor can carry a dose, trade it, or give it away, but cannot spend it on themselves before they need it. A dose carried by a clean survivor is worth nothing to them until they are bitten, and everything to everyone around them who already has been (`dilemmas.md`).

## What It Does

- The virus stops, permanently, for the rest of the session.
- The Mark fades to a pale scar. The fever clears.
- Every gift the strain gave goes with it.
- The survivor cannot be infected again, by any strain, for the rest of the session. No bite, no water, no needle can take hold.
- The survivor falls silent to Resonance. The dead and the Turned must find them by sight and sound.
- Wounds are not healed. The dead and the living can still kill the cured.

## The Scar

A cured survivor is the most useful ally in the city, and every survivor who sees the scar knows it.

A cured survivor can walk through a region of a strain no one else dares enter, drink its water, wade its sewers, and take the bites of its rats with nothing worse than the wound. They can carry a latent vial without fear of it, and cannot be needled. Nothing that senses the virus feels them coming from across the city, not the dogs, not the G-Virus's brood, and not the Turned. A cured survivor can scout where the marked cannot, fetch what the marked cannot reach, and stand between a marked friend and the dead without lighting up the street.

That is what the scar proves, and what every other survivor wants: not a dose, which is gone, but a person who can no longer be lost to the virus. It also proves something less comfortable. A cured survivor has nothing left to fear from the dead but their teeth, and nothing left to gain from any other survivor but a seat.

## Not the End

Elpis saves a survivor from the Turn. It does not save them from the night. A cured survivor is still standing in the same city, with the same empty hands, among the same dead, and among survivors who now know exactly what their scar means.
