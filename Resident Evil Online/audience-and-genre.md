# Audience and Genre

This document asks three questions about Resident Evil Online. What do the people who already love Resident Evil want from it online, and what have they refused? What does it have that the games of its genre do not, and is that enough to be a new genre, or only a new game? And whom, fans or not, can it draw? It is written in the Service's register. Its evidence is what players did, with games and with their money, and what they said in reviews; it is never a survey of opinion. `market-precedents.md` reads the market; this document reads the audience.

## The Premise Under Test

Resident Evil Online launches as Survivor and nothing else (`launch-scope.md`). Outbreak, Versus, Mercenaries, Recollections, Endless, and the Refuge are kept as possible future development: none is scheduled, and each opens only if the service can pay for it without taking anything from Survivor (`future-development.md`). No case in the business case pays for one. The richest tier, Full, spends $10M a year on content, and that pays for the seasons; in the Base case the service runs at Keep-alive from the Opening on, with $0.3M a year for content. The largest addition any case funds is the Valley, a world and not a mode, built only in the Hit case and paid for outside the tiers (`business-case.md`, Running It, Four Cases).

So the question "is it different enough to attract players" has to be answered for Survivor alone. The other modes can make a successful Survivor last longer and reach further. They cannot rescue one that does not draw players on its own. They are judged below on what they would add if Survivor succeeds.

## What the Resident Evil Community Wants Online

### The Record

| Game | What it was | What happened | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Resident Evil Outbreak** and **File #2** (2003–2004) | Four-player co-op survival horror: eight ordinary people, an infection gauge, ad-libs instead of microphones, scarce items | 1.45 million copies for the first file. The official servers closed in 2007 and 2011; fans rebuilt the online service from packet captures and restored it in 2014, and still run it. Calls for a remake recur in the press every year | A small, durable community wants co-op survival horror with ordinary people, scarcity, and an infection that runs inside the body |
| **Operation Raccoon City** (2012) | A squad shooter in the city's last days, with Leon, Claire, and Jill to fight against | 2.8 million copies, Metacritic 48–52. IGN called it "a poor man's SOCOM and a destitute man's Resident Evil" | The fans bought the city and its people, and then judged the game as a Resident Evil and found it wanting |
| **Resident Evil 6**, Agent Hunt (2012) | Other players dropped into a campaign as its creatures | A side mode, never brought back | The idea of a person inside a monster is old in the series |
| **Umbrella Corps** (2016) | A competitive shooter | Never on Capcom's million-seller list; Metacritic 36–38. Critics called it a generic shooter with a Resident Evil skin | The fans refuse Resident Evil worn by another genre |
| **Resident Evil Resistance** (2020) | Four survivors against one Mastermind, packed in with the *Resident Evil 3* remake | A Steam peak of 4,181; Steam reviews Mostly Negative, 39% positive across 2,362. Reviews complain of empty queues, abandonment, and pay-to-win | An asymmetric game can draw Resident Evil players, and loses them to a thin population and anything that sells power |
| **Resident Evil Re:Verse** (2022–2025) | A five-minute PvP deathmatch in which the dead return as bioweapons, packed in with *Village* behind a premium pass | 2,080 Steam players in its first month, never above 100 after its second; shut down in June 2025. Its reveal was met with open displeasure | A PvP shooter "makes absolutely no sense in the context of Resident Evil", as one critic put it, and the fans agreed by not playing it |
| **The Mercenaries** (in *Resident Evil 3*, *4*, *5*, *Village*, the *4* remake) | A score attack against a clock | Given free in the *Resident Evil 4* remake in April 2023; its absence from the *Resident Evil 3* remake was among that game's complaints | The extra the fans expect in every game |
| **Dead by Daylight's** Resident Evil chapters (2021, 2022) | Nemesis, Leon, Jill, then Wesker, Ada, and Rebecca, in someone else's asymmetric horror | Two chapters a year apart, the second with a large cosmetics line | Resident Evil's people sell in a multiplayer horror game that respects them |

**What is not in the record.** Capcom's own surveys ask the fans which games to remake, not which online games to make, and the polls that ask fans to choose a remake did not even list Outbreak. The demand for Outbreak is real and loud in the press and among its players; it has never been measured against the size of the series' audience. The fair reading is that the Resident Evil audience is mostly a single-player audience, and that the part of it that plays online is small and specific (`business-case.md`, What the Fans Are Worth).

### What They Want

1. **Survival horror, played together.** Outbreak is the one online Resident Evil the fans have kept alive themselves. Co-op, ordinary people, scarcity, an infection that runs out.
2. **The series' own things.** Its people as they were, its places as they stood, its monsters, its puzzles, its safe rooms, its herbs. Operation Raccoon City sold on the city and its people before anyone had played it.
3. **The Mercenaries.** Every time.
4. **Story that adds to the series.** Not a mash-up of assets, but something that says more about the world.

### What They Refuse

1. **Resident Evil as a competitive shooter.** Umbrella Corps, Re:Verse, and the deathmatch modes before them. Every PvP-first Resident Evil failed.
2. **Leftovers and pack-ins.** Resistance and Re:Verse came in the box with other games, and were treated as extras.
3. **Paying for power, or paying before playing.** Resistance's pay-to-win complaints, Re:Verse's premium pass, the *Resident Evil 4* remake's weapon tickets (`the-service-and-promises.md`, What the Record Shows).
4. **A game that dies.** Outbreak's servers closed twice; Re:Verse was switched off. What the fans could not play anymore, they remember.
5. **An empty queue.** Resistance's most common complaint was the wait.

### Resident Evil Online Against That List

| The fans | Resident Evil Online | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Survival horror, played together | Survivor is survival horror first: the dead, the virus, and the Heavy are the main threat, and survivors who wake alone can make squads of up to four in the night (`survivor-mode.md`, Squads). But every stranger can kill them | **Partly.** It is horror together, inside a night where other players are a danger. The PvP the fans have refused is in it, as a choice rather than the point |
| The series' own things | The whole Raccoon City of 1998, its people on their threads, the Nemesis in every night, the people of Outbreak among the Stranded, the R.P.D.'s medallions, the safe rooms, the herbs, the classic looks as recorded (`story-nights.md`, `cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, `objectives-restrictions-events.md`, Puzzles) | **Yes, more than any online Resident Evil has offered** |
| The Mercenaries | A future mode, not scheduled (`mercenaries.md`) | **No, at launch** |
| Story that adds to the series | ΜΝΗΜΗ, Pithos, the Sealed File revealed season by season, and story nights that play the records differently every night | **Yes**, and it is the part no other game can copy |
| No competitive shooter | Fighting the living is possible, never required; a night is won by leaving, not by killing (`survivor-mode.md`, Winning) | **Yes, with a risk:** the fans who hear "PvPvE" may hear Re:Verse |
| No leftovers or pack-ins | A game of its own, sold on its own | **Yes** |
| No paying for power | Nothing sold changes a night; nothing random is sold (`the-service-and-promises.md`, The Promises) | **Yes** |
| A game that does not die | Every piece opens offline within a year; if the service ends, nothing goes dark (`offline-and-ownership.md`) | **Yes, and it answers the series' own history directly** |
| No empty queue | Echoes fill up to a quarter of a night; regions merge; nights start at forty (`business-case.md`, Population) | **Yes**, unless the launch misses badly |

Resident Evil Online answers almost every refusal in the record. Where it falls short is that the two things the online fans ask for by name, an Outbreak and the Mercenaries, are not in the launch, and the one thing they refused, other players as enemies, is.

## What Resident Evil Online Has That Its Genre Does Not

Every system in Survivor has a precedent somewhere. The table names the closest shipped one, and what Survivor does differently.

| In Survivor | The closest precedent | What is different | How new |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The clock inside the body** (`infection.md`) | Outbreak's virus gauge (2003); Dying Light 2's immunity timer (2022); Project Zomboid, where a bite is death | It is per survivor, invisible, read only from the body, and starts at a different minute for everyone | A known idea, used at a new scale |
| **One cure, earned privately, dropped publicly** (`elpis-and-the-road.md`) | Battle royale airdrops, contested by everyone who sees them; Hunt: Showdown's bounty, earned by one team and revealed to the rest | The cure is earned by one survivor's road and falls where everyone can run for it; earned is not owned | **A new combination** |
| **The hidden bite** (`the-living.md`) | Social deduction: The Thing (2002), where infected squadmates broke trust; Among Us; Dread Hunger | No one is dealt a role. Anyone can become the one with a secret, at any minute, by bad luck, and can hide it until Fracture | **A new combination** |
| **The infected keep playing as monsters** (`the-turned.md`) | Infection modes in Halo and Call of Duty; Outbreak's fallen hunting their team; Resident Evil 6's Agent Hunt; Dead by Daylight's killers | The shape depends on the strain and on how the Turn came, quick or long, quiet or loud; and the Turned have no victory, only Hunts | A known idea, with new rules |
| **A director with Laws** (`director.md`) | Left 4 Dead's AI Director; Alien: Isolation; Helldivers 2's human Game Master | She is a character with a voice, rules that bind her, and a story of her own, and she never lies | A known technique, made into a character |
| **The story night** (`story-nights.md`) | Fortnite's one-time live events; Sea of Thieves' scripted Tall Tales; single-player games like Hitman, whose people keep their schedules around the player | A canonical story, with its people walking their threads among sixty-four strangers, every night differently, and every beat breakable by anyone | **New.** No multiplayer survival, extraction, or battle royale game has done it |
| **Voice heard by the dead** (`presentation-and-technology.md`, Voice, in Practice) | Phasmophobia, Lethal Company, R.E.P.O. | How hard a voice is spoken, not the microphone, decides who hears it | A proven idea, done more carefully |
| **No HUD; the body is the status** (`survival.md`, Self-Examination) | Dead Space's suit; Metal Gear Solid 3's Cure screen, where wounds were treated one by one | Examining yourself is public: whoever is close sees what you see | A known idea, made social |
| **Looks as recorded** (`story-nights.md`, As Recorded) | Halo: Combat Evolved Anniversary's switch between old and new graphics | Old and new records stand in the same street at the same time | Small, and new |
| **Seats, not survival, decide the winner** (`extraction.md`) | Extraction games' exits; some of Tarkov's exits limited to one group | A few seats for many, and every infected passenger costs the rest (`economy.md`, Exposure) | **A new combination** |
| **Echoes of real players** (`echoes.md`) | Forza's Drivatars | Built from horror choices, not driving lines, and a player can meet their own in an offline night | Known in racing, new here |

Four of these are new combinations and one, the story night, has no precedent in its genre. Each of the others is borrowed from a game that proved it works. That is the strongest position a design can have: nothing in it is untested, and the whole has never been made.

## Is It a New Genre?

**Not by itself, and that is not what decides whether it succeeds.** A genre is named by players after one rule becomes the reason they play: last one standing made the battle royale, leave with what you found made the extraction game. Survivor has no single rule of that kind. `survivor-mode.md` calls it an extraction game played as survival horror, which differs from the extraction shooters in one rule: no item is carried in and none is kept. That one rule is larger than it sounds. It splits Survivor between two existing structures:

- **The start of a battle royale.** Everyone arrives empty-handed, scavenges, plays one match, and keeps nothing. That is battle royale's loop, not the extraction game's.
- **The end of an extraction game.** The night is won by leaving, as many win as there are seats, and no one has to be last. That is why `survivor-mode.md` is right that it is not a battle royale.
- **Two layers neither has.** Survival horror as the main threat, and a social-deduction layer, the hidden bite, that neither genre has ever had.

It belongs fully to neither. That matters most for the extraction audience. What keeps players in ARC Raiders and Tarkov is the gear they carry out and bring back: the risk of losing it and the slow climb of what they own. Survivor deliberately has none of it. Its progression is Hardening, the Ledger, and what a player wears (`survivors.md`, `after-action-report.md`, `cosmetics-and-dossier.md`). An extraction player who arrives looking for that loop will not find it, and will be right that it is missing.

What Survivor offers in its place is what neither genre has: the reason to come back is not what you own, but what happened. Who was bitten, who lied about it, who took the dose, who was left at the door. That is the loop of the social-deduction games and the voice-horror games, whose audiences are large and whose games are short (`market-precedents.md`).

**The honest name for it** is a survival horror game for sixty-four strangers, won by leaving. If a genre comes out of it, players will name it after the moment they share most, and the documents already say what that moment is: *you, or them?* (`dilemmas.md`).

## Whom It Can Attract

| Who | How many | What draws them | What keeps them away |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Resident Evil fans who play online** | Small: the best online Resident Evil sold 2.8 million | The city, its people, the story night, the Nemesis, the records as recorded; a game that will not be switched off | Other players as enemies; the memory of Re:Verse and Umbrella Corps; no Outbreak, no Mercenaries at launch |
| **Resident Evil fans who do not** | Most of 213 million units | The story night, and ΜΝΗΜΗ's world | Online at all. Every map launches online only, and opens offline after a hundred thousand finished nights or twelve months, whichever comes first (`offline-and-ownership.md`) |
| **Extraction players** | ARC Raiders' 16 million, Tarkov's core | Higher stakes than any extraction game: the clock in the body, the cure, the seats | No loot to keep, no gear to build. They will compare it to what it deliberately is not |
| **Voice and social-deduction players** | Lethal Company's 10 million, Phasmophobia's 20 million, Dread Hunger's million | The whisper that saves a squad, the shout that dooms it, the bite no one admitted | Its length, its complexity, and its price against games that cost $10 |
| **Zombie-survival players** | DayZ and Project Zomboid, both still setting records years in | A zombie city full of strangers, trust, and betrayal | A night that ends; DayZ's world never does |
| **Streamers and their audiences** | Every hit in `market-precedents.md` spread through them | Moments a stranger understands in thirty seconds: the drop and the scramble, the marked stranger at the Subway's door, a squadmate turning in a helicopter, the Nemesis walking through a crowd toward Jill | Long silences, and a night that can last an hour and a half |

**What it needs to draw people who are not fans** is the four moments in the last row, and they are already in the design. None of them needs the player to know Resident Evil. Each of them needs the player to understand a rule they were never told, which is the risk.

## What Stands in the Way

- **Complexity.** Six strains, four stages, roads, Petitions, Embraces, Hunts, the Doorstep Record, hunger and thirst, Self-Examination, the Crossing: no game in `market-precedents.md` asked a new player to learn this much without a HUD, and Marathon's reports name its learning curve among its first problems. What a player's first nights teach is set in `director.md` (A File's First Nights); the rest of the depth is still learned by playing.
- **Length.** No one gets out of a Survivor night in its first hour unless the dead have already brought the living down to the seats; the signal rises by minute 90 at the latest, and the city falls twenty minutes after it (`extraction.md`, `session-and-blueprint.md`, Scale). A night that goes the distance lasts eighty minutes to almost two hours. An ARC Raiders raid lasts thirty. The Turned keep playing, which keeps the bitten in the night, but a clean survivor killed at minute ten has the rest of it only to watch.
- **PvP in a series that refused it.** The fans who have read about Re:Verse will hear "PvPvE" and stop listening. The design's answer, that the living are a choice and the dead are the point, has to be shown, not said.
- **The name of the mode.** *Resident Evil Survivor* (2000), the first of the Gun Survivor spin-offs, is rated 39 on Metacritic. The fans who remember it will hear it in "Survivor".
- **How the game describes itself.** `survivor-mode.md` used to open with *Extraction survival horror · PvPvE*. Both labels were accurate, and both named the comparisons Survivor loses: "extraction" to the games with loot, "PvPvE" to Re:Verse in the fans' memory. Readers of the published documents did stop there, taking it for a generic extraction shooter. It now opens as survival horror among strangers, won by getting out, and `start-here.md` answers the comparison directly.

## The Modes Beyond Survivor

Each future mode, against what the community has done and what the market has shown.

| Mode | What the record says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Outbreak** (`outbreak.md`) | The one online Resident Evil the fans kept alive themselves, and the one they ask for by name. Its eight people already wait as the Stranded of Survivor (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`) | **The strongest fan case.** It was in the launch, with four scenarios, before the launch narrowed to Survivor (`future-development.md`, The roadmap that was) |
| **Mercenaries** (`mercenaries.md`) | Expected in every Resident Evil, given free in the *Resident Evil 4* remake, missed when absent. Its stages are bounds of the world already built (`worlds.md`, Bounds on Each World) | **The cheapest fan case.** Little to build, a long record of demand. It too was in the launch that was |
| **Versus** (`versus.md`) | Every PvP-first Resident Evil failed: Umbrella Corps, Re:Verse, and the paid Versus mode of *Resident Evil 5* (`the-service-and-promises.md`, What the Record Shows) | **The weakest case.** It is the one thing the fans have refused every time |
| **Recollections and Endless** (`recollections-and-endless.md`) | Frames over the survival modes, in the tradition of the series' unlockable modes | Low cost, low risk, modest draw |
| **The Refuge** (`the-refuge.md`) | No record either way | Last, as the documents already place it |

The order in which the documents would bring these modes is not set (`future-development.md`). The record suggests one: Mercenaries first, because it costs least and is asked for most; Outbreak second, because it is the fans' own; Versus last, and only redesigned.

## What Would Make It a Game Changer

A game changes its genre when other games start copying one thing it did. In Resident Evil Online that thing is the story night: a canonical story playing out, live and breakable, among strangers who did not come for it. Nothing else in the design is as hard to copy, because it needs a series with thirty years of people and places to draw on, and Capcom has one.

The rest of Survivor makes it a strong game, not a new genre. It becomes a game changer only if four things hold:

1. **The story night is the headline.** It is what a fan buys, what a stranger remembers, and what no competitor can answer (`market-precedents.md`, How Resident Evil Online Succeeds). It is also costed, and the business case keeps it on the launch line for that reason (`business-case.md`, What the Fans Are Worth).
2. **The first nights teach it.** A design this deep is only a strength if a new player can learn it by playing.
3. **The living are shown to be a choice.** Every trailer and every first night should show strangers surviving together before it shows them killing each other.
4. **It is never called an extraction shooter.** That invites a comparison with ARC Raiders that it loses on purpose, by keeping no loot.

## Open Questions

- **Where a player's first nights are played.** What ΜΝΗΜΗ teaches in them is set (`director.md`, A File's First Nights). Whether they are played among the experienced or apart from them is not.
- **Whether a shorter night exists.** Nothing in the documents lets a player choose a night of thirty minutes. A map's bound could hold one (`worlds.md`, One World, Every Mode), but it would be a new kind of night.
- **The mode's name and its label.** Whether "Survivor" stays, knowing what *Resident Evil Survivor* was; the label is settled (What Stands in the Way, above).
- **The order of future modes.**
