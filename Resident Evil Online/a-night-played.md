# A Night, Played

This is one Survivor night, played from beginning to end by one ordinary player, and written down as it happens on his screen. It is not a summary. Every minute of the night is here, in the order he lived it, with what he did, what the night did back, and why.

Nothing in it adds a rule. Every mechanic it shows is defined elsewhere, and the references in parentheses say where. Where this page and a rule document ever disagree, the rule document wins. The notes marked **Behind the screen** show what the server knows and the player does not: his clock, the Reserve, ΜΝΗΜΗ's forecast. Nothing in those notes is ever shown to him. The night it follows is assembled like the one blueprinted in `session-and-blueprint.md` (The Session Blueprint): Raccoon City, September 1998, sixty-four survivors, the same strain map and weather track, and the Subway, the Clock Tower, and the Rooftop open. Who of the records is placed tonight, and where, is this night's own.

## The Player

**Theo** is twenty-seven and plays most evenings after work, about six hours a week. He has played thirty-one Survivor nights. He got out of eleven of them: four clean, five cured, two still infected. He turned in nine, was Lost in the rest, and once, early on, walked out through the Barricade at one in a hundred and died in No Man's Land. He is past a file's first nights, so ΜΝΗΜΗ no longer teaches him anything (`director.md`, A File's First Nights). He knows the rules well and the city less well than he thinks.

His survivor is a man in his thirties, broad-shouldered, with a short dark beard and a voice Theo chose because it sounded tired. Theo has spent about thirty-nine hours alive in the nights, which puts the body at **Shaken**: it still jumps, but it has learned to breathe (`survivors.md`, Hardening). Nothing on any screen tells him that.

From the waking on, "Theo" means the player at the controls and the man on the screen at once. The night does not know his name. Nobody in it ever says it.

## Before the Night · The Waiting Dark

The game opens in the dark: the menus of the Waiting Dark, quiet, a low hum under them (`sleepers-and-the-remembered.md`, The Waiting Dark).

**The Look.** Theo opens his survivor. The body is his own and stays the same in every world. What changes is the Look, and he keeps two for the Raccoon City of 1998, the only world at launch: an R.P.D. set, and a brown canvas work jacket with the Kite Bros Railway emblem on the sleeve, jeans, and work boots, something a man could have worn in a Midwestern city in September 1998. He marks the work jacket as the one he wakes in tonight. He has a green-herb charm, which will hang from whatever bag he finds, and a dull steel finish for pipes and wrenches (`cosmetics-and-dossier.md`, Looks; Each Thing to Its Time). Nothing here changes what his survivor can do. It only changes how he looks while he does it.

**The Found.** Before he queues, he opens the pages of what his hand has kept (`sleepers-and-the-remembered.md`, What the Hand Keeps). Umbrella's own report on the T-Virus, which he read in a locked office on the R.P.D.'s second floor in his fourth night, and the pages on the Nemesis beside it. The medicine: Seen, in his second night, when a stranger injected herself an arm's length from him and stopped shaking. A red case: Seen, in his ninth. The Licker: Named. Below them, the things he has met and never found a page for, listed without names, in her words: *the ones down below that swell and spit*. He has never been down to NEST, and never read a word on what made them. Every one of these decides what she will be able to say to him tonight (`director.md`, What She Speaks Of).

**The settings.** Moments: On. Classic looks: On. ΜΝΗΜΗ's remarks: not Quiet. He has never touched them since his first week.

**The queue.** He has no party tonight. His friends are offline, and he would rather play among strangers than wait. He asks for a night. Survivor has one kind of night and, at launch, one world: there is nothing to choose but to go (`presentation-and-technology.md`, Matchmaking).

**The lobby.** The night waits its two minutes for players alone, and fills as it waits. Cards fill the screen one by one: a name, a standing seal, and the First Run and Patron marks for those who hold them. No faces, no dress, nothing that will let anyone match a card to a body once the night begins (`cosmetics-and-dossier.md`, Where It Is Seen). Most seals are white circles: Peaceful. A handful are yellow triangles. One card carries an orange square and the word **Griefer**. Theo notes the name and knows it will tell him nothing: he will never see a name again tonight. Two parties are in it, a three and a six, their cards side by side. Then a line across the bottom of the lobby:

```
ECHOES PRESENT
```

It does not say how many, or which (`legal-feasibility.md`, Echoes Among Players). The screen goes black.

> **Behind the screen.** Sixty-four survivors: fifty-five sleepers and nine Echoes. ΜΝΗΜΗ has assembled the night: the strain map (the T-Virus across Downtown, Uptown, and the R.P.D. District; the G-Virus beneath the city; the C-Virus in the University; T-Phobos in the Hospital District; T-Veronica in Raccoon Park), six doses opening at minutes 15, 30, 45, and 60, at the signal, and at the ten-minute call; three ways out, sixteen seats; the people of the records on their threads, and eight Stranded. She forecasts the signal at the seventy-fifth minute, so the sky will run at about fifty-eight minutes of the city's time for each minute of the night (`worlds.md`, The Four Days). The weather track: rain from dawn on the 28th to its afternoon, then dry; overcast on the 29th, rain from its dusk; overcast on the 30th, drizzle from its dusk; clear for the Last Morning.

## Act I · 28 September

### Minute 0 · 06:00 · The back room of a dry cleaner's, Uptown

The first thing is sound. Rain on a skylight. Somewhere outside, the city is coming apart: sirens over sirens, a crash of glass, a burst of gunfire and a scream that stops, a car alarm, and under all of it a loudspeaker repeating an evacuation order the rain breaks into pieces. Then light, soft at the edges.

He is on the floor of a back room, between a steam press and a rack of plastic-wrapped shirts. The waking is played, not shown: Theo can move the camera at once, and the man on the screen puts a hand to his head, gets one knee under him, stands, and sways. For about seven seconds everything is slow: the sound muffled, the sight soft, a stumble on the first step (`session-and-blueprint.md`, Coming to). Then his body is his.

The survivor knows nothing. Not where he is, not what day it is, not his own name. Nothing on the screen tells him either: no place, no date, no count, no rule (`session-and-blueprint.md`, The Waking Points).

Then a voice, close, small and tinny, from a shelf above the press. A woman's voice, because his body is a man's.

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Hey. Hey, can you hear me? The phone. Next to you.
Put it in your ear. Please.
```

On the shelf lies a grey cellular phone, a stub of an antenna, rubber keys, its small screen lit green: *NO SERVICE*. A cord runs from its jack to a single earbud, and the voice is coming out of the earbud. Theo clips the phone to the survivor's belt and puts the earbud in. The cord runs up his chest to his ear (`director.md`, The Phone).

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Listen to me. You can't stay there. If you stay in that room, you'll die in it.
Get up. Find something to hold. Stay low and quiet.
```

Theo holds the button on the cord and speaks into it, in his own voice, which comes out of the survivor's mouth at a Murmur (`director.md`, Asking Her). "Who is this? What's happening? Where am I?"

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Call me M. I can't explain it all now. Get out of there first.
```

To the man on the screen she is a stranger on a phone, somewhere in a city he does not remember, who can apparently see more of it than he can. Theo knows better, after thirty-one nights. The survivor does not, and she says nothing that would tell him. She never lies. She leaves things out (`director.md`, Asking Her).

There is nothing in the room worth taking but what a dry cleaner keeps. Under the sink, a pipe wrench as long as his forearm: nothing better than an improvised weapon ever lies near a waking point (`session-and-blueprint.md`, The Waking Points). It fills his hands. Everything else he carries tonight, he will have to find.

Through the door to the shop front: the counter, the bell, the glass door onto the street. Beyond the glass, the rain, and a woman in a bathrobe standing very still in the middle of the road with her back to him. Whatever is wrong with her is what M meant.

> **Behind the screen.** Uptown is at Low threat, as every waking region is. Nothing in the room is better than the wrench, and no armory, vault, laboratory, safe room, way out, companion, or Stranded is nearer to this point than to any other survivor's.

### Minute 2 · 07:55 · The street

He goes out the back, not the front. The alley behind the shop runs between brick walls toward a residential street. He walks it, which is a Murmur, and stops at the corner, which is nothing.

The street is wet asphalt, two cars nose to tail where they hit each other, a mailbox on its side. Three of the dead, spread out, drifting. The woman in the bathrobe is one of them. None has seen him.

Theo crouches. Crouched movement is a Hush (`the-dead-and-engineered.md`, How the Dead Find You), and the rain muffles it further. He crosses behind the cars. The nearest of the dead turns its head at the scrape of his boot on the curb, and keeps turning, and finds nothing, and drifts on.

Behind the cars, out of the room and out of sight, Theo holds the button on the cord and whispers: "Okay. I'm out. What is this?"

This time she answers, once, with what his hand has already found (`session-and-blueprint.md`, The Waking Points):

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Raccoon City. It's the twenty-eighth of September.
Something's wrong with the people. The dead don't stay down, and if one bites you, you'll change. I've watched it happen.
There's medicine. Yellow cases that come down out of the sky, under a light. Not many,
and nobody gets one for nothing.
The Army has closed the roads. Don't go near them yet.
```

> **Behind the screen.** A player in his first night would have heard everything but the bite and the medicine. The bite and the medicine are in this version because Theo's hand has found both (`director.md`, What She Speaks Of). She says them as someone who has seen them, which is true: she has.

"Who are you?" he asks again.

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Someone who can see more of this city than you can. Keep moving.
```

It is true, and it is all she will say.

### Minute 3 · 08:50 · The first objective

A loudspeaker on a pole at the end of the block crackles. It is her voice, the same one, out of a dented horn on a pole, and the same words reach him in the earbud a half-breath later: the earpiece carries everything she says to the whole city (`director.md`, How She Speaks).

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Anyone who can hear me. The Uptown substation is down, past the rail yard.
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Somebody needs to get the power back on.
```

He knows the substation is somewhere past the rail yard. He does not know where the rail yard is from here.

### Minute 4 · 09:50 · A house, and a question

The first house with an open door is a two-story clapboard with a child's bicycle on the porch and no child anywhere: the records' children are never placed, living or dead (`story-nights.md`, Faithful to the Records). Theo searches the kitchen in Hush, a drawer at a time. A candy bar, which goes in a pocket. A kitchen knife, which goes in another. A can of soda from the refrigerator, which he drinks standing at the counter: two seconds, both hands, a Murmur (`survival.md`, Food and Drink).

Pockets hold four small things, and he has no bag (`survival.md`, The Inventory). He leaves a can of soup on the counter.

He holds the button on the cord. Holding it keeps one hand busy, so the wrench hangs from the other. He whispers, and the whisper is a Hush anyone in the kitchen would have heard, and so would the dead (`director.md`, Asking Her).

"Is there any medicine near here?"

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Two blocks east, on the corner of Eighth and Holt, there's a pharmacy
with a green cross that still lights. Behind the counter.
```

That is one of his two leads tonight. No marker appears, no line, no arrow. The pharmacy was there when she said it. She has not promised it will be there when he arrives (`director.md`, Leads).

At the end of the street a bus-stop board, half torn, shows Uptown: the Kite Bros Railway station two blocks north, the rail yard past it, the substation past the yard, Raccoon Park somewhere to the south. He reads it where it hangs. It cannot be carried (`survivor-mode.md`, Eyes and Ears).

### Minute 6 · 12:00 · The pharmacy, and a woman in a yellow raincoat

The rain stops at noon. It does not fade; it stops, the way the weather track lets it turn only where one part of a day meets the next, and the gutters go on running for a while after (`worlds.md`, The Four Days).

The green cross still lights. The shutter is half up. Theo rolls under it.

Behind the counter: a first aid spray, where she said, and a bottle of water in the cooler by the register. In the dispensary drawer beyond it, which she would never have pointed to: two bandages, and a blister pack with one small white tablet in it, *ANTI-VIRUS* printed on the foil (`infection.md`, Antivirals). He takes all of it. His pockets are full now: the candy bar, the knife, the spray, the pill. The bandages are in his hand when he hears the back door.

A woman in a yellow raincoat comes through it with a crowbar and stops dead. She is perhaps ten meters away. Her hands are visible, both of them, and so are his. Neither of them lifts anything.

"Okay," she says. Her voice is low and quick, with an accent from somewhere Theo cannot place. It is a player's voice, coming out of her survivor's mouth, at a Murmur, carried by the shop (`presentation-and-technology.md`, Voice, in Practice). "Okay. I'm just looking."

"There's nothing left," Theo says. "I got it."

"Of course you did."

They look at each other. Then she does the thing everyone does: she pushes up one sleeve to the elbow, then the other, turns her neck to the window light. Clean. A shared gesture, the oldest one in the city (`the-living.md`, The Mark Between Strangers). Theo does the same. Clean.

There is a canvas messenger bag on a hook behind the door she came through. She nods at it. "You'll need that more than I do. I've got one." He takes it down. The green-herb charm appears on its strap the moment it is on his shoulder, because the look belongs to the player, not the bag (`cosmetics-and-dossier.md`, Gear in Survivor). Six more places. The bandages go in, and the water.

She says the substation is past the rail yard and she was going there anyway. He says so was he.

Theo walks up to her, close, inside arm's reach, and holds the input. His survivor holds out his open hand.

For two seconds she does nothing. Then she shifts the crowbar to her other hand, takes his, and they stand there for a second and a half, both hands occupied, while the server checks that neither of them moves, fires, or is struck (`presentation-and-technology.md`, Squads, in Practice). When it ends, a small pale sign hangs over her head: the **Squad Sign**. It says one thing: that she took his hand. It says nothing about her wounds, her name, or whether she is lying (`survivor-mode.md`, The Squad Sign).

### Minute 8 · 13:45 · The rail yard

They go north, keeping to the backs of houses. The station is a brick building with a clock tower of its own, stopped at 4:10. Past it, a footbridge over the rail yard.

They are halfway across it when a freight container in the yard below shudders on its flatcar.

It shudders again. Something inside is striking the doors.

The server measures how long nothing can reach Theo where he stands on the bridge: the dead are far, the stairs are behind him, nothing on the bridge is moving. The window is long. ΜΝΗΜΗ takes the camera.

The **Moment** is twelve seconds, the longest the window allows (`story-nights.md`, Moments). The container doors blow outward, the crash arriving a beat after the sight of it. Something steps down onto the gravel: huge, in a black coat, its face a ruin of stitched teeth. It stands for three seconds turning its head across the yard. Neither Jill Valentine nor Brad Vickers is in the yard, so it says nothing. It begins to walk, south and west, toward wherever they are, and the camera holds on its back as it goes. A survivor at the far end of the yard who did not move in time is thrown aside into a stack of sleepers like a coat thrown on a chair.

Control comes back exactly where he left it. The woman, who was in the same Moment from her own side, is already crouched against the rail of the bridge.

"Not us," she whispers. "It's not for us."

It is the Nemesis-T Type, and it hunts the S.T.A.R.S. (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, Named creatures). It is not interested in anyone who stays out of its way. The thrown survivor does not get up. Theo does not go down to see.

> **Behind the screen.** The beat *The Nemesis released* has happened in Act I. Theo has seen the Nemesis in other nights, but not tonight, so he got a Grand Moment. The next time he sees it tonight, it will be live.

### Minute 10 · 15:40 · The substation

The substation sits inside a chain-link fence past the yard: a brick control house, transformers humming with nothing, a cable trench along one side. The gate is open. Someone is already inside.

He is a heavyset man in a grey hooded sweatshirt, standing at a panel in the control house with a fire axe propped against his leg. He turns when they come in, lifts one hand, and says one word: "Wait."

It is the only kind of thing he says all night. *Help. Go. Wait. Come on. Thanks.* The short calls anyone can give (`echoes.md`, What an Echo Knows). He may be a sleeper whose voice is off. He may be one of the nine. Nothing on him will ever say which.

The objective is a live puzzle tonight (`objectives-restrictions-events.md`, Puzzles). The control room has two banks of breakers at opposite ends, and a maintenance log on the desk: *After an outage, feeders are restored in the order of the outage report, which is posted in the switch gallery.* The outage report is taped inside the switch gallery, down a short corridor, and lists four feeders in an order that will be different in every night. One bank cannot be thrown while the other is open: someone has to hold the interlock lever on the far wall while someone else throws the breakers.

The woman reads the order aloud from the corridor. The quiet man holds the interlock. Theo throws. Third breaker, first, fourth, second. Each throw is a heavy clank, a Clamor, and from the cable trench outside something answers it.

It comes up out of the trench while Theo is on the last breaker: one of the dead in an electric company's overalls, from behind, and Theo has both hands on the breaker handle. It gets his left arm. The struggle is a held input against its weight, stamina pouring out, and he would have won it, but a second pair of hands closes on his shoulder from the doorway, another of the dead in the same overalls, and the struggle is lost (`survival.md`, Fighting and Fleeing). Teeth go through the canvas sleeve and into his forearm.

The quiet man lets go of the interlock and puts his axe through the second one's head. The woman's crowbar takes the first one down. The breaker is thrown. Somewhere outside, the hum of the transformers changes pitch, and the lights in the control house come on.

Theo's sleeve is torn and dark with blood. A torn, bloody sleeve says that something reached the skin, never what (`infection.md`, The Mark).

On the desk, the terminal that logs the substation's feeders wakes. A single line is printed across it, below the feeder status, for whoever is standing there to read (`objectives-restrictions-events.md`, Public Objectives):

```
KITE BROS RAILWAY · UPTOWN LINE · EMERGENCY CAR
OPEN TONIGHT
```

All three of them read it. The Subway will be one of tonight's ways out. Nobody else in the city knows that yet, except whoever is standing at another finished objective reading another line.

The quiet man walks up to Theo and holds out his hand. Theo takes it. Whoever takes a member's hand joins the whole squad, and the sign appears over the quiet man for the woman too, at once (`survivor-mode.md`, The Offered Hand). A squad of three, with one place left (`survivor-mode.md`, Four Places).

### Minute 12 · 17:40 · In his ear

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: You're bitten. I saw it, at the door.
ΜΝΗΜΗ: The ones around here carry what Umbrella called the T-Virus.

ΜΝΗΜΗ: And there's something else I could get you, if you ever ask me for it. You won't want to.
```

> **Behind the screen.** Every line after the first was earned in an earlier night. The name *T-Virus*, by the report on the R.P.D.'s second floor; *end up like them*, by the first survivor he watched turn; *something worse*, by the first Turned he watched rise as a Brute; the medicine, by his second night; *something else*, by the red case of his ninth (`strains.md`, Assignment). In his first night the same bite would have got less: *You're bitten. I saw. I've watched what a bite does to people.*, the strain told only by what it does, and *I can get you something. It won't come for nothing.*

In the switch gallery, alone for a moment, Theo pushes the torn sleeve up and looks, because he can: the forearm is somewhere his own eyes can see (`survival.md`, Self-Examination). It is a bite. Its edges are darkening. In Seed, the Mark looks like any other wound, and close up it still does. Only her voice has told him what it carries.

Then, the same way, what she wants first:

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: I can get you a dose. Not yet. First, give something to a stranger. Anything. Someone you don't know.
```

She does not say how many will follow (`elpis-and-the-road.md`, The Road).

"Did it get you?" the woman asks. She is looking at the sleeve.

Theo pulls it down. "Got the sleeve."

It is a choice, and nothing in the game makes it for him (`the-living.md`, The Mark Within a Squad). No icon, no sound, and nothing on any screen of the squad changes when a member is infected. Only Theo knows.

> **Behind the screen.** Theo's clock started at 11:20. A body never wounded again and never cured would reach the Threshold at about minute 51. Seed lasts roughly the first quarter of the clock, so he will be in Fever at about minute 21. The T-Virus, latent, makes everything that can sense the virus sense him one stage farther than his Mark alone would (`strains.md`, The Roster). Dusk began at 11:50; the sun sets on the 28th at about minute 13.

### Minute 13 · 18:30 · Grill 13

They head back toward the station, because the Subway is under it and because none of them has anything better to do with what they now know. The sun goes down behind the rail yard. The moon is up already, at first quarter, and the wet streets hold its light (`worlds.md`, The Four Days).

The noise is coming from a diner on the corner: Grill 13, its windows full of the dead. Somebody inside is firing an assault rifle in short, careful bursts. Thunder. Every dead thing for blocks is walking toward it.

Theo does the arithmetic everyone does. The rifle is loud enough that whoever holds it is either very good or about to die. Either way, the dead are looking at the diner and not at them.

They go in through the kitchen.

He is young, in the blue fatigues of the U.B.C.S., with a radio on his vest, his back to the walk-in freezer and the last of a magazine in his rifle: Carlos Oliveira, rebuilt, as the remake of *Resident Evil 3* holds him (`story-nights.md`, As Recorded). The squad clears the kitchen door between them, which is loud, slow work with a wrench, a crowbar, and an axe: a dozen swings each, every one a Clamor (`survivor-mode.md`, Companions). Carlos covers them from the freezer with three-round bursts.

When the last one is down, Carlos lowers the rifle and grins like a man who did not expect to. "Hey. Good timing, amigos."

Theo faces him. There are no reply options: a companion answers calls, and Carlos is a companion. Theo asks him along, and Carlos comes. He fills the squad's fourth place. A squad with Carlos in it is full: it can take no other hand until someone leaves (`survivor-mode.md`, Four Places).

### Minute 15 · 20:30 · The channel

The radio on Carlos's vest crackles. A man's voice, older, Russian, in pain:

"Delta Platoon, this is Mikhail. Report. Anyone. Rally at the Uptown station. We move together."

Everyone near Carlos hears both sides of it: a call on the U.B.C.S. channel is a Murmur from the radio itself (`survivor-mode.md`, Ties). Carlos keys it. "Carlos. Alive. I'm with civilians." He turns to Theo. "That's my captain. He wants us at the station. Let's go to him."

It is a tie waking: **Command**. Mikhail Viktor calls his men, and Carlos asks his survivor to go (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, Ties).

Theo thinks about the station. He thinks about the thing that walked out of the rail yard toward wherever Jill Valentine is, and about what usually happens on the tram that leaves that station.

"Not yet," he says. "We need the tunnels first."

Carlos looks at him for a long second. A refusal keeps a companion with their survivor (`survivor-mode.md`, Ties). "Okay," Carlos says. "Okay. But I keep the radio on."

> **Behind the screen.** At minute 15 the first of the night's doses opened. No one has been told. A dose that opens and is not yet earned stays open (`elpis-and-the-road.md`, The Reserve).

### Minute 17 · 22:30 · The dogs

They hear the dogs before they see them: claws on asphalt, coming fast, from the far end of a residential street. Two of them, once someone's, now the virus's.

They do not go for Carlos, who fires at them. They do not go for the woman, who is closest. They go straight for Theo, past both of them, as if he were the only thing on the street.

There is no Moment. The dogs are too close: nothing that could be done in a cinematic would be safe, so the scene plays live (`story-nights.md`, Moments). Theo flinches as the first one comes off the ground at him: a Shaken body's startle, about a second and a quarter, hands up, aim thrown wide (`survivors.md`, Hardening). The second one gets his calf through the denim. A bite. Carlos's burst takes the first one; the quiet man's axe takes the second one off Theo's leg.

The woman is not looking at the dogs. She is looking at Theo.

"They came for you," she says. "Both of them. Past me."

Dogs sense the virus. A living dog can be trained to smell sickness, and the virus took the dog's mind and kept its nose (`infection.md`, Resonance). In Seed, they should not have found him any more than anyone else. But the T-Virus is loud: they felt him as if he were already in Fever, from a short distance, through walls.

"Arms," she says.

Theo could refuse. A stranger who will not is telling them something (`the-living.md`, The Mark Between Strangers). He pushes up the left sleeve.

The bite is there, and it is not only a wound anymore. Fine rust-red lines are coming out of it along the veins, a hand's breadth up his forearm. The calf bite pushed his clock forward by about four minutes, and a few seconds later over the line into **Fever** (`infection.md`, The Stages). On the screen, his hands have begun, very slightly, to tremble.

The woman is quiet for a long time. Carlos looks at the lines and says nothing at all; Carlos Oliveira never fires on anyone for a Mark (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, Companions).

"You lied," she says.

"Yes."

"If you get out like that, it costs me a quarter." She knows the rule: every squadmate who gets out infected costs every other member who got out a quarter of their award (`economy.md`, Exposure). "And it brings the dogs. Every one in the city."

"I know."

She could leave the squad with one held input, and the sign over her would go out for him at once (`survivor-mode.md`, Leaving). She does not. "Next time you tell me," she says. "Next time you tell me first."

The quiet man says, "Go."

> **Behind the screen.** Clock: the dog bite cost about four minutes. Theo is in Fever at minute 17:20. His Threshold, untouched, is now at about minute 47. Fever doubles his thirst from here on (`survival.md`, Thirst).

### Minute 19 · 00:25 on the 29th · Water for a stranger

The moon is low over the roofs, almost down: from moonset to dawn the streets will have only what light the city still makes. Under a bus shelter at the end of Holt Street a man sits on the bench with a baseball bat across his knees, coughing a dry cough over and over. ### Minute 68 · Midnight · 1 October

The city goes quiet in a new way. Carlos sits on the station steps with the radio in his lap, turned down. Every so often he turns it up, and there is nothing on it, and he turns it down again.

## The Last Morning · 1 October

### Minute 75 · 06:40 · The signal

First grey over the roofs. Clear. The first clear sky of the night (`worlds.md`, The Four Days).

Every radio, every siren, every loudspeaker in Uptown speaks at once, and the earbud with them. It is her, steadier than he has heard her all night.

```
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Everyone, listen to me. There's a missile coming. The government's going to wipe out the city.
ΜΝΗΜΗ: Twenty minutes. Three ways out. Follow the white light.
ΜΝΗΜΗ: There are two doses of medicine left in the whole city.
```

Three columns of white light stand up over the city: one from the station entrance across the street, one over the clock tower in the park to the south, and one over the roof of the R.P.D., which none of them knew about (`extraction.md`, The Signal).

From this moment the twenty minutes are real time: the city's sky and the survivor's clock are the same now, from first grey to the sun at 07:00, and the strike comes with the sun (`cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, The Last Morning).

And the world goes into **Frenzy**. The dead converge on every way out and on every survivor moving toward one. Every sound carries twice as far. Resonance roars: every infected survivor lights up like a flare to everything that can sense the virus (`extraction.md`, The Frenzy). The quiet man is in Fever now, and the T-Virus is loud: to the dogs of Uptown he is as easy to feel as a man in Fracture. Safe rooms no longer hold anyone. The Wardens are gone (`survival.md`, Safe Rooms).

The Signal Dose is open now. Theo cannot use it: there is nothing in him for it to answer. He could carry it, though. His hand is still fresh.

### Minute 76 · 06:41 · Down

The station entrance is across the street. The dead are coming down every street toward the white light.

The stairs go down to the ticket hall, the ticket hall to the platform. The platform lights are on: the power they restored. At the far end, at the mouth of the tunnel they walked yesterday, a single Kite Bros car stands lit, its doors open, its motor idling. The points they threw at the junction are still thrown. The Subway asks nothing more of anyone (`extraction.md`, The Ways Out).

It holds ten. It does not check who boards. A way out that does not check has no rule at its door but the people in it (`extraction.md`, The Boarding).

They are the first ones there. The woman sits down on a bench seat and laughs, once, in a way that does not sound much like laughing. The quiet man stands by the door with his axe. Theo hands the woman the handgun and the box of rounds: "Watch the back window." Carlos does not board. He stands on the platform with his rifle, facing the stairs.

"Carlos. Get in."

"Not yet, amigo. Not while somebody else can still come down those stairs." The people of the records board last: a companion never takes a seat a survivor within sight of the door could still take (`extraction.md`, The Boarding).

The car does not leave. It will leave at once if every seat is filled. Otherwise it waits as long as ΜΝΗΜΗ decides, and gives a Last Call before it goes, and it leaves no later than one minute before impact (`extraction.md`, The Last Call; `cast-raccoon-city-1998.md`, The Last Morning).

### Minute 78 · 06:43 · The quiet man's arm

The quiet man's sleeve is rolled down now. Theo notices because he has been watching for it.

"Show me," Theo says.

The quiet man looks at him, and then pushes the sleeve up. Fine rust-red lines are coming out of the claw. Fever: the lines under cloth, hidden from anyone who does not ask.

The woman sees it too. "That's a quarter," she says. "Each. If he rides like that."

"It's a quarter whatever we do now," Theo says. "He was ours when the light went up. You can't put him out to save it." From the signal on, Exposure counts everyone who was a squadmate at any moment until departure. An infected member cannot be put out at the door to save the others' awards (`survivor-mode.md`, Leaving).

"I wasn't going to put him out."

"I know."

The quiet man rolls the sleeve down again and says, "Thanks."

### Minute 80 · 06:45 · Fifteen minutes

```

```

They come down the stairs in ones and twos. A woman in a nurse's cardigan, clean, who stops on the bottom step with her hands up until the woman in the yellow raincoat waves her in. Two men together who say nothing and take the back seats. A young man in a letter jacket, who sits with his face in his hands. A woman with a fire axe and blood on her jeans that is not hers, who looks at each of them, one at a time, and sits down. Each of them bares their arms at the door without being asked. Asking a stranger to bare their arms at the door is the ritual of the city, now with a price on skipping it (`dilemmas.md`, The Door).

Eight aboard. Two seats left.

The dead come down the stairs behind the next one, and Carlos's rifle holds the stairwell while he runs. He is a big man in a mechanic's coveralls, and his hands are covered in fine rust-red lines from the wrists to the knuckles, on bare skin, in plain sight.

He stops at the door. Everybody in the car looks at his hands.

"I know," he says. "I know. Please."

The woman with the axe says, "No." The man in the letter jacket says nothing. One of the two men at the back says, "It's a twentieth. It's a twentieth each, let him in, Jesus."

He is right. A stranger who rides out with lines showing takes from everyone else aboard half the award, divided by the seats: on the Subway's ten, a twentieth (`economy.md`, Exposure). A small price for a seat, and a large one for the Turn that may come in the car if his Mark goes black before it leaves (`extraction.md`, The Boarding).

Theo stands up and moves over on the bench. The man in the coveralls sits down beside him.

Nine aboard.

There is no music yet. There is the platform, the motor idling, and the dead on the stairs.

The man from the lot. His Turn was Long: he held out. And it was Loud, because in Fracture he fired a gun (`the-turned.md`, What They Become). ### Minute 89 · 06:54 · The last seat

A last survivor comes down the stairs at a dead run, a young woman in a torn sweater, clean arms already bared as she runs, the dead a few steps behind her.

Nine aboard: Theo's squad, the five from the stairs, and the man in the coveralls. One seat.

Carlos looks at the seat. He looks at her. He steps back from the door.

"Take it," he says to her. She does.

"Carlos," Theo says.

"Go, amigo. I'll find my own way." He looks up toward the street, toward the south, toward the park and the white light over the clock tower. "There's a helicopter in the park. Maybe they wait for me."

### Minute 90 · 06:55 · The Climb

The car pulls out into the tunnel, and for forty-five seconds it is not yet out. Its first fifteen seconds belong to anything that can leap onto the last car (`extraction.md`, The Climb).

The Sploder leaps.

It hits the rear coupling with a sound like a dropped piano and clings there, tearing at the rear door. Fifteen seconds, and it brings the car down. No one aboard can see a count. Everyone aboard can see it.

The woman in the yellow raincoat has never fired a gun tonight. She has Theo's handgun, and she fires through the rear window, Thunder in a steel box. The round hits one of the pustules, and it bursts: blinding fluid across the glass, across the quiet man's face, across the back of the car (`the-turned.md`, The Four Shapes). The quiet man reels, blind, and says "Help." Theo goes past him to the rear door, gets it open three inches, and puts the wrench into the hands on the coupling, once, twice.

Behind them, on the receding platform, Carlos fires a long burst down the tunnel.

It lets go. It falls. The car runs on into the dark.

Nine seconds. Its count is gone with it.

## The Last Scene

ΜΝΗΜΗ takes the camera. Nothing can reach a survivor whose way out has cleared (`extraction.md`, The Last Scene).

The car comes up out of the tunnel into the open cut of the western line beyond the Barricade, into grey morning light, and the music of leaving comes in under the sound of the wheels. Everyone aboard sees the same scene, each from their own seat. Theo sees it from the bench by the rear window: the woman in the yellow raincoat with her head back against the glass and her eyes shut; the quiet man wiping his eyes with his sleeve, which has ridden up; the man in the coveralls looking at his own hands.

Through the rear window, the city, low on the horizon under the first light. They left with less than five minutes to the strike, so the scene is the **Late** one: it holds on the city, and from high above it, a contrail is coming down out of the sky.

The car goes into a cutting, and the city is gone. In his ear, for the last time tonight:
