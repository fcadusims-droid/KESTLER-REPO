## 1. Purpose

When the IUN began withdrawing its peacekeepers from Usea in 2020, it left behind three threats that no single state could address:

1. **Proliferated Belkan autonomy.** Z.O.E.-derived software, ADF-11F Ravens, MQ-101s and drone-control architectures on black markets, in terrorist cells and in failed states.
2. **Corporate armed power.** Conglomerates acquiring bankrupt manufacturers and private military capacity through reconstruction contracts.
3. **Gray-zone conflict.** Undeclared, proxied and deniable fighting that doctrines built around sovereignty and declared war cannot describe.

The Initiative was chartered to answer them: a small, fast, multinational force with no homeland, operating where a national force would be politically impossible.

## 2. The Name

The original Independent State Allied Forces was the coalition, built around the FCU, that defeated Erusea in the Continental War of 2003–2005. Usean politics has treated its memory as either a golden age or a warning, depending on the decade. Reviving the acronym was meant to invoke the first meaning. In Erusea it invokes the second, and the Initiative's charter concedes that the name costs it legitimacy where its aircraft most often fly. The name was kept on the reasoning that a new one would have to earn a history, and the Initiative did not expect to have the time.

The ISAF Aviation Academy at North Point, a training institution that kept the coalition's name after the coalition itself stood down, supplies many of the Initiative's younger pilots.

## 3. ISN *Albatross* (CVN-X1)

| Field | Data |
|---|---|
| Type | Conventional-takeoff CATOBAR fleet carrier |
| Origin | Hull laid down for an Osean fleet carrier; completed under joint funding after the Lighthouse War |
| Operator | ISAF Revival Initiative, multinational crew |
| Air wing | Composite: Osean and ISAF-standard F/A-18E Super Hornets, a small number of F-35C Lightning IIs, and a few foreign-origin airframes (including one Rafale M) maintained at considerable cost |
| Home port | North Point |
| Status in 2029 | Forward-deployed, rotating theaters |

The mixed deck is operationally inefficient and politically essential. A carrier that launches an Emmerian-trained pilot in a stealth fighter alongside a Yuktobanian–Osean dual national in a Super Hornet is the Initiative's argument in physical form.

The albatross follows ships for thousands of miles without landing. In the old sailors' superstition it is also the bird whose killing curses the ship that did it. The crew accept both meanings.

## 4. Operating Model

| Strength | Cost |
|---|---|
| Tactical diversity from mixed training lineages | Cultural and historical friction between members from former enemy nations |
| Access to places national forces cannot go | No homeland to return to |
| Resistance to infiltration through loyalty to an ideal | Command answers to a council, not a clean chain |
| Proof that post-war cooperation is real | Legitimacy contested in Erusea and Estovakia |

The Initiative's internal language calls its units *operationally orphaned*, which is why they were chosen to hunt orphaned threats.

## 5. Loyalty Risk

Counter-intelligence rates 6AMEꓤƎꓥO9 a low desertion risk under legitimate command and an unquantifiable one under compromised command. Their loyalty is to the Initiative remaining what they believe it is. The same corporate consolidation that drives General Resource toward MINOZIS bears on the Initiative from another direction: the possibility that corporate interests are quietly instrumentalizing it. Should the pilots conclude the Initiative is no longer itself, the regional precedent for what disillusioned aces do next is well documented and not encouraging.

## 6. Tasking to the Counter-MINOZIS Task Force

6AMEꓤƎꓥO9 had been on the Task Force's standing list of assets since the Liaison Instrument. In March 2029, with the dossier complete and a Sotoan offensive assessed as likely within twelve months, the Task Force requested the squadron by name. Three reasons were recorded:

- Its record against proliferated autonomy, including operations against Z.O.E.-lineage drone remnants.
- Its home platform: a carrier answerable to no single government, able to move between theaters without either government explaining why.
- Its people. The cell judged that the squadron's psychological profile made it both unusually resilient and unusually exposed against this enemy, and that a unit which knows its own wounds is safer than one that does not. See [Squadron Threat Surface](RELATIONSHIPS.md#4-the-threat-surface).

The ISAF council approved the tasking on the condition that the squadron never operate inside Yuktobanian or Osean national airspace without both governments' written consent. Both governments gave it, in writing that does not exist.
