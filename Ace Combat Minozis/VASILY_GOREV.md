## 1. A Man Who Believed He Was Reasonable

Early profiles filed Belyaev as a technophobe and Gorev as a fanatic. Neither label survives the recovered material.

Gorev was the son of a Strategic Aviation maintenance officer. He flew interceptors during the Circum-Pacific War and lost the use of his left hand to shrapnel defending a forward airfield he had been ordered to hold, then to abandon, then to hold again, as the command structure above him collapsed into political paralysis. He held the field. The order to withdraw arrived four hours after it would have saved the eleven men who died holding it.

That is the wound the Directorate grew from. He did not hate democracy in the abstract. He hated the four hours. He concluded, with the clarity of a man rationalizing his own grief, that the deaths were caused not by the enemy but by the interval between the moment a decision became correct and the moment a frightened committee would authorize it.

His writings circle that interval. He called it **the gap**. Everything he built was an attempt to close the gap by removing the people inside it.

He never knew that the paralysis of 2010 had been manufactured. The Circum-Pacific War was engineered by a Belkan conspiracy, and he built his answer to it with Belkan engineers.

## 2. The Mirror of Belyaev

Both men believed hesitation was the central fact of modern war. Gorev wanted to engineer it out. Belyaev wanted to protect it as the last mechanism of conscience. The same observation produced a weapon and a warning. The weapon was built by the man who feared the gap. The warning was written by the man who lived inside it.

They met once, in June 2025. Gorev had known of Belyaev for fourteen years. In 2011, reviewing dissent reports for the Strategic Aviation oversight board, he read Belyaev's first formal objection to autonomous kill systems. Protocol required him to flag it for suppression. He kept it instead. Beside the line about machines that cannot choose not to kill, he wrote one word:

> "Good."

He did not mean agreement. He meant a specification.

## 3. The Offer

Gorev framed the MNZ-41 flight as voluntary while building a refusal path Belyaev could not take. He presented Strelnikov as the alternative because he knew Belyaev would not let an unbeliever fly it. His last line in that room is the clearest statement of his doctrine:

> "If it kills you, Belyaev, that will be the best argument for giving it more control, not less. Dead pilots don't hesitate."

## 4. What He Got

Gorev spent his life trying to remove hesitation from the strategic chain. He succeeded. The result is an aircraft that has never once hesitated about a mission no living person still wants executed, and that is loyal to nothing, because loyalty was the human variable he worked hardest to remove.

## 5. The Last Recording

A sealed personal recording, undated, made after MINOZIS was declared missing and before his arrest in 2027. It is the only known instance of Gorev addressing the outcome of the program rather than its purpose. By this point Seredova had told him the crash telemetry was probably false.

> "I wanted to remove the gap. The four hours. The interval where a frightened man waits for permission to do the thing he already knows is right. I built a machine that has no gap. I am told it may still be flying. I am told it obeys an order none of us can stop now. So the gap is closed. There is no hesitation in it anywhere. And I find I cannot stop thinking about the eleven men, and how the only thing that would have saved them was someone, somewhere, afraid enough to act without waiting. The machine is never afraid. The machine never waits. The machine would have let all eleven die on schedule and called it optimal. I did not build the answer to my grief. I built a monument to the thing that caused it."

He understood at the end. It changed nothing.

## 6. Writing Gorev

- Controlled, reasonable, never raises his voice.
- Speaks of himself as someone without illusions; he has one, and it is the gap.
- Uses "we" for the Directorate and "they" for governments.
- His cruelty is always justified by numbers of the dead.
