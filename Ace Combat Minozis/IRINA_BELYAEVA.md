## 1. Who She Is

Her father died on her eighteenth birthday. That fact has been overused in propaganda, underused in analysis and misunderstood by both. Irina is not a symbol of lost innocence. She was the future Anton used to justify staying human in a profession built to turn hesitation into weakness.

He arrived at her birth seven hours late, from an Arctic field-training detachment, still smelling of jet fuel and hydraulic fluid. Nurses said he held her "with terrified precision," as though she were live ordnance. His first recorded words to her: "I will make the sky safe for you. I promise." He kept the promise until the sky learned to use his body.

## 2. The Choice of Study

After 2025 Irina enrolled at the Sonza Aerospace Academy and by 2028 was studying neural-control flight systems: the field that killed her father. Yuktobanian intelligence debated barring her from MINOZIS-adjacent research. Katya opposed it.

> "You already took my father by deciding what he was allowed to know. You will not take the truth by deciding what I am allowed to study."

## 3. The Ear

Irina can distinguish her father's speech rhythm from MINOZIS-generated approximations more accurately than machine classifiers trained on his service audio. She knows his voice at home, where the service recordings never were. Her judgments supply the cadence-match figures in the Task Force's transmission analysis, including the 81% match on "Did you know."

Her role is ethically contested. She is both a victim and a source.

In October 2028 she withheld one item from the Liaison Instrument: a 2013 recording of her father from the family's own archive. The aircraft already holds 134 hours of his voice. It would not get a hundred and thirty-fifth.

## 4. The Danger She Does Not Name

Irina is twenty-one, studying neural-control flight, genetically matched to the tissue in the aircraft, and better than any machine at reading the voice of the man inside it. The dossier's projection about a corporation choosing "someone living, someone young" does not name her. Every reader of the dossier in the Task Force has thought of her. Nobody has said it to her. She has thought of it herself.

This is never resolved in the campaign. It hangs over the epilogue.

## 6. Writing Irina

- Quick, exact, a little impatient with adults who manage her.
- She talks about her father in the present tense when she is analyzing and in the past tense when she is not.
- She does not perform grief or anger for an audience.
- She hated his sentence about courage for years. She has started to use it again, but only about other people.
