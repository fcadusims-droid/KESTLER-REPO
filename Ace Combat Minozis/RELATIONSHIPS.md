## 3. The Squadron

### Hierarchy

The formal hierarchy runs Thorne, Marsh, Asano, Eirhei. The emotional hierarchy almost inverts it: Eirhei at the center, then Asano, then Marsh, with Thorne at the edge. On a mission they are one instrument. Off a mission they are a family that cannot say what it means.

### Pairings

**Six and Nine (Marsh–Asano): complementary chaos.** He generates creative entropy; she converts it into morale and cover. Both defend with humor, and the two defenses can lock into a loop where neither processes anything real. He fears she is too performative; she fears he does not take her seriously. In combat they are nearly telepathic, speaking a private vocabulary. Their first unguarded moment came in 2028, when he heard her singing a Yuktobanian song about a lost child far from home on a channel she did not know was open, and did not make a joke. Since then she sometimes sings for him on long flights.

**Over and AME (Thorne–Eirhei): structure and soul.** He makes precise tactical decisions; she makes sure those decisions count the human cost. He sees emotional expression as weakness; she sees suppression as slow self-destruction. They are still the closest pair in the unit, because she is the only person he lets into his emotional space at all. In 2028, after a mission with civilian casualties, she found his diary open and read a single page: a meticulous account of every decision and every alternative, ending with a line recording that she had asked whether he was all right, that he had not answered, that he was not, and that she already knew. She never told him. Since then she translates his stripped phrasing for the others ("Maintain formation" becomes "James is worried but trusts you"). He has noticed and never objected.

### Rituals

- **The circle.** After every mission Eirhei convenes it: each member names one thing they felt. Marsh jokes. Asano is honest. Thorne passes until, eventually, he says the mission was adequate. Nobody asks Eirhei.
- **Double or Nothing.** The unofficial motto and the operational code for total commitment, the point where retreat is no longer an option.

### The insignia

Assembled in the North Point base bar on the night of the unit's designation in March 2027. Marsh, playing with dice, proposed them for unpredictability and then realized a die has no nine. Asano flipped a six upside down, producing the image, the joke and the theme at once: the same object reads differently depending on the angle. Thorne, trying to recover some institutional dignity, saw that the callsigns could be read as a phrase: the 6 standing in for a G by its shape, then AME, OVER and the closing 9. Eirhei finished it: she inverted OVER into ꓤƎꓥO to mirror the flipped die, gave Marsh's six orange and red flame and Asano's nine pink, and set four aces behind the dice (hearts for connection, spades for strength, diamonds for value, clubs for organized chaos), saying the unit never gambles with a bad hand.

**6AMEꓤƎꓥO9** reads "Game Over." The squadron treats it as a joke about the fate of whatever it is sent to kill. All four have since noticed the second reading. They are among the last human aces, the generation that will fight in the cockpit before autonomy takes the role. The pilots who chose the phrase as a boast are the pieces being removed from the board. MINOZIS, Morgenstern and the drone remnants they hunt are the same announcement from the other side.

ISAF command approved the design without noticing the encoded phrase or the flipped-die joke, which Asano explains to every new officer who compliments it.

## 4. The Threat Surface

MINOZIS does not engage capability. It engages operators, and it builds its model of them from emissions, radio habits, reaction latency, the tone of a voice, and whatever it has harvested from personnel networks weeks earlier. It does not need to be right about a pilot's history. It only needs to find the stimulus that produces delay, faster than the pilot can hide it.

| Pilot | The wound | What the aircraft applies | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marsh | A cockpit death he could not prevent; a stroke he believes he caused | A voice asking for help he cannot give in time | [M06 Harbor](../MISSIONS/M06_HARBOR.md) |
| Asano | Exclusion; the belief that visible seriousness invites rejection | Sustained pressure with no moment humor can touch; isolation from the audience she performs for | [M07 Thirty Kilometers](../MISSIONS/M07_THIRTY_KILOMETERS.md) |
| Thorne | A cadet injured under his command; a father killed under someone else's | An engagement where the correct order is plainly the lethal one | [M05 Shrinkage](../MISSIONS/M05_SHRINKAGE.md), [M09](../MISSIONS/M09_ANCHOR_IN_THE_STORM.md) |
| Eirhei | A sister reached twenty-three minutes late | A voice asking to be let go, which she can neither refuse nor grant | [M09](../MISSIONS/M09_ANCHOR_IN_THE_STORM.md) |

Each pilot also carries an object into the cockpit that has no operational function and says what the wearer cannot: a beaded necklace made beside a grandmother now partly paralyzed; a pink flame stitched onto a jacket by a woman told her prize was propaganda; a watch hiding the insignia of a man who cannot say he wants to belong; earrings from a sister two weeks before her death.

The squadron's members carry these objects for the same reason Belyaev carried his ring. An assessment that leaves them out describes a squadron MINOZIS is not going to fight.
