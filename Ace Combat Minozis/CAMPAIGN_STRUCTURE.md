## 1. Shape

| Block | Missions | Dates | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | P00 | 18 Jun 2025 | Teach the enemy by being its victim |
| Act I: Orphaned Threats | M01–M03 | Mar 2027 – Nov 2028 | Teach the squadron; show human judgment winning; show the enemy only through effects |
| Interlude I | I01 | Feb 2029 | First contact with the voice |
| Act II: The Cold File | M04–M06, I02 | Mar – May 2029 | The hunt: doctrine, deception, the first direct engagement |
| Act III: The Drag | M07–M08 | Jun 2029 | Wearing the aircraft down; the cost of doing so |
| Finale | M09 | 18 Jun 2029 | The forced crash |
| Epilogue | — | Jun–Jul 2029 | The circle; the canon horizon |

Twelve playable missions (one prologue, eight squadron missions, two interludes, one three-phase finale). Target campaign length: 8–10 hours.

## 2. Point of View

The campaign rotates its point of view among existing characters. Each POV has a distinct aircraft, a distinct signature mechanic and a distinct way of being pressured by the enemy.

| Mission | POV | Aircraft | Why this POV |
|---|---|---|---|
| P00 Moroz | Anton Belyaev | MNZ-41 | The player must feel the override from inside |
| M01 Four of a Kind | Thorne | F-35C | Leadership tutorial; learning to trust a wingman |
| M02 Glass Ferry | Marsh | F/A-18E | His read, his call, his gun pass |
| M03 Key Rotation | Asano | F/A-18E | Her perception sees what the instruments miss |
| I01 Falcon Two | Vance | F-35A | The question is addressed to her |
| M04 Eyes Only | Eirhei | Rafale M | Introduces the empathy display just as the threat to operators is revealed |
| M05 Shrinkage | Thorne | F-35C | The correct order that feels wrong |
| I02 Phantom Limbs | Althéa | EF-2000B | The other side of the mirror |
| M06 Harbor | Marsh | F/A-18E | A voice asking for help he cannot give |
| M07 Thirty Kilometers | Asano | F/A-18E | Pressure with no release |
| M08 Light-Bearer | Eirhei | Rafale M | Translating for a stranger |
| M09 Anchor in the Storm | Thorne → Eirhei → Vance | F-35C → Rafale M → F-35A | Each phase belongs to the person whose wound it reaches |

Rules for POV design:

1. Every POV switch is announced by a title card with the pilot's callsign, aircraft and anchor object.
2. In squadron missions, wingmen not under player control are always the same four squadron members, with the POV pilot's slot taken by the player.
3. The player never flies MINOZIS after the prologue.

## 3. Escalation of the Enemy

| Stage | What the player sees of MINOZIS |
|---|---|
| Prologue | Everything, from inside |
| Act I | Nothing directly: hijacked drones and an Erusean shrug |
| Interlude I | A glow at distance, a scream, a voice |
| M04 | The scream; network probing; instrument lies |
| M05 | A slow, radar-visible aircraft at night that does not fight |
| M06 | First direct attack; first discharge window |
| M07 | Seven minutes of loop above 30 km; first heavy damage |
| M08 | Close attack on a defended target; illumination |
| M09 | Every system it has, failing |

The player learns the encounter's rules in the order the Task Force learned them, and the [Intelligence Ledger](../GAME_DESIGN/CORE_GAMEPLAY.md#5-the-intelligence-ledger) records each lesson as a dossier entry whose confidence grade rises as the player gathers evidence.

## 4. Between Missions

Each mission is framed by up to five beats aboard *Albatross* (or at the POV character's location):

1. **Briefing.** Delivered by the Task Force cell over dossier pages, with Vance adding pilot-level warnings from Act II onward.
2. **Training.** After M04, M05, M06, M07 and M08, a short simulator scene aboard *Albatross* shows one pilot drilling against the Ledger's threat model. That pilot uses the drill in the next mission. See [Adaptive Progression](../GAME_DESIGN/ADAPTIVE_PROGRESSION.md#3-training-scenes).
3. **Hangar.** Aircraft, loadout, parts and doctrine modules; see [Progression and Hangar](../GAME_DESIGN/PROGRESSION_AND_HANGAR.md).
4. **Sortie.**
5. **The circle.** Eirhei's post-mission ritual, played as a short scene. The POV pilot chooses what to name. Choices affect squadron cohesion (see [Squadron and Wingmen](../GAME_DESIGN/SQUADRON_AND_WINGMEN.md)) and unlock dialogue in later circles. They do not change the plot.

Interlude missions replace the circle with a single scene for the interlude character.

## 5. Pacing

| Mission | Intensity | Emotional register |
|---|---|---|
| P00 | Rising to unbearable | Dread, helplessness |
| M01 | Medium | Friction becoming trust |
| M02 | High, then precise | Moral pressure, triumph without celebration |
| M03 | High, chaotic | Confusion; unease |
| I01 | Very low | Dread; the voice |
| M04 | Low and long | Paranoia |
| M05 | Medium | Restraint |
| I02 | Very low | Grief, recognition |
| M06 | Very high | Protectiveness; horror |
| M07 | High, sustained | Exhaustion; ethical discomfort |
| M08 | High | Connection across the mirror |
| M09 | Extreme, then still | Release |

Quiet missions (I01, M04, I02) sit before each major escalation. Every quiet mission must still contain one hard moment.

## 6. Narrative Choices

Ace Combat's tradition is a fixed plot with personal variation. This campaign follows it.

| Choice type | Effect |
|---|---|
| Circle answers | Cohesion values; later dialogue |
| Optional objectives (civilian protection, evidence capture) | Ledger confidence upgrades; debrief lines |
| Firing discipline in I01 | If the player attempts a lock, the flight lead's rebuke and the aircraft's reaction change; the outcome does not |
| Ledger reading | Optional entries unlock extended briefings and the final epilogue paragraphs |
| Scenes, sorties and drills | Squadron lessons and enemy counter-adaptations ([Adaptive Progression](../GAME_DESIGN/ADAPTIVE_PROGRESSION.md)); they change how missions play, never how they end |

No choice changes who lives or dies among named characters. The story's thesis depends on outcomes the player cannot optimize away.
