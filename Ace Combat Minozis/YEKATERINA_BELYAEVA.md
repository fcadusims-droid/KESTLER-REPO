## 1. Who She Is

Katya was the only person who could challenge Anton without making him withdraw. She did not share his military worldview, but she understood machines well enough to know when institutions were lying about them. She understood why he flew. She never accepted that the reason made the cost inevitable.

She met him in October 2005 at a recovery facility outside Cinigrad, where she had come to sit with an uncle who was not going to recover. She found him at three in the morning in the common area, taking his sidearm apart and putting it back together.

> **KATYA:** "You're going to wear out the springs."
> **ANTON:** "Better the springs than my hands."
> **KATYA:** "Are your hands shaking?"
> **ANTON:** "Not anymore."

They talked for four hours. They married eight months later.

## 2. What She Knew and When

| Date | Knowledge |

| Oct 2028 | Liaison Instrument. She withholds nothing. |
| 2029 | Fatigue analysis of the repaired MNZ-41 identifies the port-side panel line as its most probable structural failure point under thermal shock. |

The machine kept her husband's body alive as hardware. She is the person who identifies where the hardware will fail.

## 3. Psychology

Katya's grief is not soft. It is engineering. She deals with loss by finding where the load went. Her anger is directed at institutions, not at Anton and not at the machine:

> "They asked me if I blamed him for going. I said no. I blame them for making him choose between his conscience and his family. He chose conscience. That was who he was. I loved him for it. I hate them for forcing it."

She does not believe the residue is Anton, and she does not believe it is not. She refuses to let other people decide that for her.

Her decision in M09 is the moral center of the finale. She is using the love the machine kept him alive with to bring the machine down. She knows exactly what she is doing, and it is the difference between her harbor and General Resource's.

## 5. Writing Katya

- Precise, technical vocabulary used without showing off.
- She asks the question behind the question ("What you are actually asking is whether I will let you admit it").
- She does not cry in scenes. She has done that already, offscreen, for four years.
- When she softens, it is with one image, never a speech.
